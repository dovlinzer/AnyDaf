# Menachot 106 — Daf Yomi Shiur

## Mincha Vow Uncertainty

### Designating Mincha

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי מִנְחָה, וְקָבַעְתִּי בִּכְלִי אֶחָד שֶׁל עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: יָבִיא מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל עֶשְׂרוֹנִים מֵאֶחָד וְעַד שִׁשִּׁים, שֶׁהֵן אֶלֶף וּשְׁמוֹנֶה מֵאוֹת וּשְׁלֹשִׁים.
>
> **Translation:** **The Sages taught** in a *baraita*: If one says: **I specified** that I would bring **a meal offering, and I established** that they must be brought **in** one **vessel of tenths** of an ephah, **but I do not know what** number of tenths **I specified, he must bring** one **meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah. This is **the statement of the Rabbis. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He must bring** sixty **meal offerings of tenths** in sixty vessels, each containing an amount **from one**-tenth **until sixty**-tenths, **which are** in total **1,830** tenths of an ephah.

Today's *daf* is Menachot 106. We pick up on 106a about halfway down the page, at the case of *perashti minchah shel esronim* — "I designated a *minchah* of *esronim*." The *mishna* presents a scenario in which someone vowed a *minchah* in the singular form — that is, one *minchah* consisting of some number of *esronim* — but cannot remember how many *esronim* he specified.

### Chachamim vs. Rabbi

The *Tanu Rabanan* elaborates: *perashti minchah v'kavati b'kli echad shel esronim v'eini yodeia mah perashti* — "I designated a *minchah*, set it in one vessel, of *esronim*, and I do not know how many *esronim* I specified." When one says a singular *minchah*, it is understood — whether explicitly or implicitly — that the entire quantity is to be contained in a single vessel, as opposed to the scenario discussed previously in which multiple vessels might be used.

*Divrei Chachamim*: *yavi minchah she-shishim esronim* — the *Chachamim* rule that one brings sixty *esronim*, since sixty is the maximum number of *esronim* a single *minchah* can contain. One then stipulates: whatever quantity corresponds to my *neder* is my *neder*, and the remainder is a voluntary *nedavah*, an additional *minchah* offered of one's own free will.

*Rebbi omer*: *yavi menachot shel esronim me-echad v'ad shishim* — Rebbi holds that since the vow specified an exact singular quantity, the offering must be exactly what was said. Because the person cannot remember whether he said one *issaron*, two, or any number up to sixty, he must bring each possibility: a separate *minchah* of one *issaron*, a separate *minchah* of two *esronim*, and so on all the way up to sixty.

### Sum 1–60: 1830

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** שֶׁהֵן אֶלֶף וּשְׁמוֹנֶה מֵאוֹת וּשְׁלֹשִׁים.
>
> **Translation:** **which are** in total **1,830** tenths of an ephah.

The *Gemara* then asks: *she-hen* — how much is the total? That is, what is the sum of all the numbers from 1 to 60? The answer is 1830. *Tosafot* provides an elegant method for computing this. Arrange the numbers from 1 to 60 in a line, then pair the first and last: 1 and 60 sum to 61. The next pair, 2 and 59, also sums to 61. Continue inward: 3 and 58, and so on, until the final pair of 30 and 31. Each of the thirty pairs sums to 61, yielding 30 × 61 = 1830. As *Tosafot* states: *she-hen elef u-shmoneh me'ot u-shloshim keitzad kach b'yadcha me-echad v'ad shishim v'tzaref techilatan v'sofan ad ha-emtza k'gon echad v'shishim hen shishim v'echad* — "take in your hand from 1 to 60 and add the beginning to the end toward the middle, for example: one and sixty equal sixty-one." This same insight, incidentally, is attributed by legend to Carl Gauss (1777–1855), who reputedly computed the sum of 1 through 100 as a young boy by recognizing this pairing trick. The presence of the identical technique in *Tosafot* suggests it was articulated centuries before Gauss — though perhaps he was the first to independently rediscover it.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי, וְאִי זוֹ מֵהֶן פֵּירַשְׁתִּי, וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ כַּמָּה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא חָמֵשׁ מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, שֶׁהֵן שְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת, דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: יָבִיא חָמֵשׁ מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים מֵאֶחָד וְעַד שִׁשִּׁים, שֶׁהֵן תִּשְׁעָה אֲלָפִים וּמֵאָה וַחֲמִשִּׁים.
>
> **Translation:** The *baraita* continues: If one said: **I specified** a certain type of meal offering with a certain number of tenths of an ephah to be brought in one vessel, **but I do not know what I specified, or which** type **of** meal offering **I specified, and I do not know how many** tenths of an ephah **I specified, he must bring** the **five** different types **of meal offerings,** and each one must contain **sixty-tenths** of an ephah, **which are** in total **three-hundred-**tenths of an ephah. This is **the statement of the Rabbis. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He must bring five** different types of **meal offerings,** and for each type he must bring **sixty** meal offerings, each with a different number of tenths, **from one until sixty, which are** in total **9,150** tenths of an ephah.

The *baraita* continues with a further layer of uncertainty: *v'eini yodeia mah perashti v'eizo mehem perashti v'eini yodeia kamah perashti* — not only does one not remember how many *esronim*, one also does not remember which *type* of *minchah* was intended. Since there are five types of *minchah*, the *Chachamim* rule: *yavi chamesh menachot she-shishim esronim* — bring five *menachot*, each of sixty *esronim*, one of each type, and stipulate accordingly. For Rebbi, however, since each offering must be the exact amount, and since there are five types and sixty possible quantities per type, one must bring every combination from 1 to 60 for each of the five types. The total number of *esronim* is therefore 1830 × 5 = 9,150, as the *baraita* states: *she-hem tishat alafim u-me'ah va-chamishim*.

### Obligation/Voluntary Mix

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בְּמַאי קָא מִיפַּלְגִי? אָמַר רַב חִסְדָּא: בְּמוּתָּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה קָא מִיפַּלְגִי, רַבִּי סָבַר: אָסוּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה, וְרַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: מוּתָּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **With regard to what** principle **do** the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi **disagree? Rav Ḥisda said: They disagree with regard to** whether it is **permitted to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that **it is prohibited to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard.** Therefore, it is necessary to bring sixty meal offerings of each type, as if one merely brings one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah in a single vessel, he may be in violation of this prohibition, as it is possible that he vowed to bring less than that amount, and the surplus amount is non-sacred. **And the Rabbis hold** that **it is permitted to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard.** Therefore, even if the meal offering is of a greater volume than necessary, it is inconsequential.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רָבָא אָמַר: דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא אָסוּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה, וְהָכָא בְּמוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה קָא מִיפַּלְגִי.
>
> **Translation:** **Rava said** that the dispute between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi is with regard to a different issue: **Everyone** agrees that **it is prohibited to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard, and here they disagree with regard to** whether it is permitted **to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וְרַבִּי סָבַר: אָסוּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה.
>
> **Translation:** **The Rabbis hold** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and therefore one may bring sixty-tenths in one vessel and stipulate that the amount beyond his obligation will be a gift offering. **And Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that **it is prohibited to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and therefore one cannot make such a stipulation; every possible meal offering requires its own vessel. Concerning each vessel, he stipulates that if this one can fulfill his vow, it should count as fulfillment of his vow, and otherwise it should serve as a gift offering.

The *Gemara* now asks *b'mai kamifligi* — what is the underlying dispute between Rebbi and the *Chachamim*? Why can't one simply bring sixty in one vessel and declare that whatever corresponds to one's *neder* is the *neder*, with everything else treated as a *nedavah*?

*Rav Chisda* proposes: *b'muttar lehachnis chullin la'azarah kamifligi* — the debate concerns whether it is permissible to bring non-consecrated (*chullin*) items into the Temple courtyard (*azarah*). Under this framework, the extra flour beyond the actual *neder* would not be designated as a *nedavah* but would remain *chullin*. Rebbi holds *assur lehachnis chullin la'azarah* — it is forbidden to bring *chullin* into the *azarah* — and therefore one cannot bring sixty in a single vessel if some of it would remain *chullin*. The *Rabbanan* hold *muttar lehachnis chullin la'azarah*. This framing is admittedly difficult: ordinarily everyone agrees one cannot bring *chullin* into the *azarah*, and moreover, the flour would need to undergo *kemitza* and be offered on the *mizbe'ach* — it is hard to see how *chullin* could function there at all.

*Rava* therefore rejects this framing entirely: *d'kulei alma assur lehachnis chullin la'azarah* — obviously everyone agrees that *chullin* cannot be brought into the *azarah*. *Vehacha bemuttar le'arev chovah binedava kamifligi* — the real debate is whether it is permitted to mix one's *chovah* (the obligatory vow) together with a *nedavah* (a voluntary offering) in a single vessel. The *Rabbanan* hold *muttar le'arev chovah binedava*; Rebbi holds *assur le'arev chovah binedava*. That is the core of the disagreement — and the *Gemara* will now turn to examine the practical complications that arise when *chovah* and *nedavah* are indeed mixed together.

## Two Kematzim Issue

### Abaye: Two Kematzim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ אַבָּיֵי לְרָבָא: לְרַבָּנַן דְּאָמְרִי מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, הָא בָּעֵינַן שְׁנֵי קְמָצִים? דְּקָמֵיץ וַהֲדַר קָמֵיץ.
>
> **Translation:** **Abaye said to Rava: According to the Rabbis, who say** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and one vessel with sixty-tenths of an ephah can serve in part to fulfill the obligation and in part as a gift offering, there is a difficulty: **Don't we require** the removal of **two handfuls,** one for the obligatory meal offering and one for the gift offering? Rava answered: According to the Rabbis, it is required **that** the priest **removes a handful and again removes a handful.**

Abaye poses a challenge to Rava's formulation: according to the *Rabbanan* who permit mixing *chovah* and *nedavah* in a single vessel, you now have a situation where the sixty-*issaron* vessel contains some flour that is the *neder* and some that is the *nedavah* — but you do not know how much is each. This means that the *minchah* potentially requires *sheni kematzim* — two acts of *kemitza* — one for the *chovah* and one for the *nedavah*. The *Gemara* responds: *dekometz vehadar kometz* — simply take one *kematz* and then take another. But this raises an immediate problem.

### Kematz Order Problem

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא קָא קָמֵיץ מֵחוֹבָה אַנְּדָבָה וּמִנְּדָבָה אַחוֹבָה!
>
> **Translation:** Abaye said to Rava: **But** if so, since the tenths that fulfill an obligation and the tenths that are a gift are mixed together in a single vessel, when the priest removes a handful, **isn't he removing a handful from** tenths that fulfill **an obligation** to account **for** tenths that are **a gift** offering, **and** removing a handful **from** tenths that are **a gift** offering to account **for** tenths that fulfill **an obligation?**

The *Gemara* presses: *veha kometz mechovah anedava uminedava achovah* — each handful is taken from a mixture, some of which is *chovah* and some of which is *nedavah*, since it all sits together in the same vessel. You cannot isolate which flour belongs to which category. The *Gemara* also raises the question of order: in what sequence do you offer up the two *kematzim*? If you offer the *nedavah* first, then when you come to offer the *chovah*, you face a problem — perhaps the whole sixty was *chovah*, in which case the flour you already burnt as the *nedavah*'s *kematz* was actually *shirayim* of the *chovah*. And the principle is: *shirayim shechasru bein kemitzah lehaktarah ein maktir kometz aleihem* — if *shirayim* are diminished between the time of *kemitza* and the time of burning the *kometz*, one may not burn the *kometz* at all. Conversely, if you offer the *chovah* first and then come to offer the second *kematz* as the *nedavah*, the problem is equally acute: perhaps the entire sixty was the *chovah*, in which case there is no *nedavah* at all, and the second *kematz* is nothing more than *shirayim* of the *chovah* being wrongly placed on the *mizbe'ach*.

### Kohen's Hand Solution

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** דְּתָלֵי לֵיהּ בְּדַעַת כֹּהֵן, דְּאָמַר: כֹּל הֵיכָא דְּמָטְיָא יְדָא דְּכֹהֵן הַשְׁתָּא – חוֹבָה, וּלְבַסּוֹף – נְדָבָה.
>
> **Translation:** Rava answered Abaye: This is not difficult, **as** the one who brings the meal offering **renders it dependent on the intent of the priest, as he says: Wherever the priest's hand reaches now,** when he removes the first handful, shall be the location of the tenths that fulfill my **obligation, and** wherever his hand reaches **at the end,** when removing the second handful, shall be **a gift** offering.

The *Gemara* addresses the mixing problem by proposing *detali lei bedaat kohen* — the designation of which portion is *chovah* and which is *nedavah* is made contingent on the *kohen*'s hand. One declares: wherever the *kohen*'s hand reaches first and takes the *kometz* from, that section is hereby designated as the *chovah* — *kol heicha dematya yadeih dekohen hashta chovah* — and the next section from which he takes the second *kematz* is the *nedavah*. *Hashta chovah ulevasof nedavah* — first the *chovah*, then the *nedavah*. By anchoring the designation to the *kohen*'s physical act, the issue of mixed flour is resolved in principle: the first handful is the *chovah*, the second is the *nedavah*.

### Second Kematz Status

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַקְטוֹרֵי הֵיכִי מַקְטַר? לַיקְטַר נְדָבָה בְּרֵישָׁא – דְּחוֹבָה הֵיכִי מַקְטַר לַהּ? דִּלְמָא כּוּלַּהּ חוֹבָה הִיא, וְחָסְרוּ לְהוּ שִׁירַיִם.
>
> **Translation:** Abaye asked Rava: **But how does** the priest **burn** the handful upon the altar? If the priest **will burn** the handful from the **gift** offering **first, how can he** then **burn** the handful **of the** tenths that fulfill the **obligation? Perhaps the entire** meal offering **is** for the **obligation,** as the vow was to bring sixty-tenths of an ephah, and everything apart from the first handful, including the second handful, is the remainder, **and** by burning part of it the priest causes **the remainder to be lacking.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:* וְאָמַר מָר: שִׁירַיִם שֶׁחָסְרוּ בֵּין קְמִיצָה לְהַקְטָרָה – אֵין מַקְטִיר קוֹמֶץ עֲלֵיהֶן!
>
> **Translation:** **And the Master said:** With regard to the **remainder** of a meal offering **that became lacking between the removal of the handful and the burning** of the handful upon the altar, the *halakha* is that **one does not burn the handful on its** account, as it is not considered a valid meal offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לַיקְטַר חוֹבָה בְּרֵישָׁא – דִּנְדָבָה הֵיכִי מַקְטַר לַהּ?
>
> **Translation:** Alternatively, if the priest **will burn** the handful that fulfills the **obligation first, how can he burn** the handful **of the gift** offering?

This, however, leads directly to the question of the second *kematz*. If the person in fact vowed sixty — the maximum — then the entire vessel is *chovah* and there is no *nedavah* component whatsoever. The second *kematz* the *kohen* takes would then be *shirayim* of the *chovah*, which the *kohanim* are supposed to eat, not burn on the *mizbe'ach*. Burning it would violate the prohibition derived from the verse *ki kol se'or vechol devash lo taktiru mimenu isheh l'Hashem* — the principle that anything from which a portion has been offered as a fire-offering (*mimenu leishim*) may not itself be subsequently burnt as an offering. As the *Gemara* states: *vechol shehu mimenu leishim harei hu beval taktir*. So: *heichi maktir lei* — how can one burn this second *kematz*? *Dilma kullah chovah hi vechasru lehu shirayim* — perhaps the entire sixty is *chovah*, and the *shirayim* have been diminished by the removal of this second *kematz*, thereby also invalidating the burning of the first *kematz* of the *chovah*.

### Firewood Solution

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי יְהוּדָה בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן פַּזִּי: דְּמַסֵּיק לְהוּ לְשׁוּם עֵצִים, וּכְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר. דְּתַנְיָא, רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר אוֹמֵר: לְרֵיחַ נִיחוֹחַ אִי אַתָּה מַעֲלֶה, אֲבָל אַתָּה מַעֲלֶה לְשׁוּם עֵצִים.
>
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Yehuda, son of Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi, said:** The priest does not burn the oil as an offering but **burns it for the sake of wood,** i.e., not as a sacrificial rite, **in accordance with** the opinion of **Rabbi Eliezer. As it is taught** in a *baraita* that **Rabbi Eliezer says:** The verse states: "No meal offering that you shall bring unto the Lord shall be made with leaven; for you shall make no leaven, nor any honey, smoke from it as an offering made by fire unto the Lord. As an offering of first fruits you may bring them unto the Lord; but they shall not come up for a pleasing aroma on the altar" (Leviticus 2:11–12). This verse indicates that **you may not offer up** leaven and honey **as a pleasing aroma,** i.e., as an offering. **But you may offer up** leaven and honey and other substances **for the sake of wood,** not as an offering.

*Rav Yehuda brei d'Rav Shimon ben Pazi* answers: *d'maseik lehu l'shum etzim* — one places the second *kematz* on the *mizbe'ach* not as a ritual offering but simply as firewood, as fuel. The stipulation is: if there is indeed a *nedavah* here, meaning the person vowed less than sixty, this second *kematz* functions as the *kometz* of the *nedavah* and is a legitimate offering. If however the entire sixty was *chovah*, then this second *kematz* is *shirayim* that should be eaten by the *kohanim*, and in placing it on the fire one is not performing any ritual act of offering — one is merely using it as kindling. This is permitted, as *d'tanya Rabbi Eliezer omer*: *l'reach nichoach ee atah ma'aleh aval atah ma'aleh l'shum etzim* — while you may not offer it up as a *reach nichoach*, as a formal offering, you may place it on the fire as wood. The principle is anchored in the verse *al hamizbe'ach lo ya'alu l'reach nichoach* — the prohibition is on offering it up as a sacrifice, not on using it as fuel. With this resolution in hand, the *Rabbanan*'s position is coherent: mixing *chovah* and *nedavah* is permitted, and the practical problems of offering the two *kematzim* can be navigated.

## Alternative Theories

### Rav Acha Theory

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ רַב אַחָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרָבָא לְרַב אָשֵׁי: וְדִלְמָא דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וְהָכָא בִּדְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר קָא מִיפַּלְגִי – דְּרַבָּנַן אִית לְהוּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר, וְרַבִּי לֵית לֵיהּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר?
>
> **Translation:** **Rav Aḥa, son of Rava, said to Rav Ashi: But perhaps everyone agrees** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and here** Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi and the Rabbis **disagree with regard to** the statement **of Rabbi Eliezer** that it is permitted to burn on the altar as fuel even items that are prohibited from being burned as offerings. **As the Rabbis accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer,** and therefore they allow one to bring one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah, since the handful removed from it may be burned, **and Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **does not accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ: אִי סָלְקָא דַעְתָּךְ לְרַבִּי מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וּדְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר לֵית לֵיהּ, אֶפְשָׁר דְּמַיְיתֵי שִׁיתִּין בְּחַד מָנָא, וְחַד בְּחַד מָנָא, וּמַגַּע לְהוּ, וְקָמֵיץ.
>
> **Translation:** Rav Ashi **said to him:** This cannot be the dispute, as **if it enters your mind** that **according to Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **it is permitted to mix** an offering brought as **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and he does not accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer,** Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi would not require sixty separate vessels. Instead, **one could bring sixty-**tenths of an ephah **in one vessel, and one** additional tenth **in one** other **vessel,** from which the handful of the gift offering would be removed. **And** he could **touch them,** i.e., place them so they touch each other, so that they are considered as one, **and** the priest could then **remove a handful** from that tenth for the gift, and remove another handful from the vessel with sixty-tenths to fulfill the obligation.

*Rav Acha brei d'Rava* said to *Rav Ashi*: perhaps we have misidentified the debate entirely. *V'dilma d'kulei alma mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — perhaps everyone agrees in principle that one may mix *chovah* and *nedavah* together. *V'hacha b'd'Rabbi Eliezer kamiflagi* — and perhaps the dispute here concerns a different question: whether one holds like *Rabbi Eliezer* who permits placing the doubtful second *kematz* on the *mizbe'ach* as firewood. On this reading: *derabbanan it lehu d'Rabbi Eliezer* — the *Rabbanan* accept *Rabbi Eliezer*'s ruling that one may place it as fuel, and therefore the practical problem can be resolved. *V'Rebbi leit lei d'Rabbi Eliezer* — Rebbi does not accept *Rabbi Eliezer*'s leniency, and therefore there is no viable way to offer the second *kematz*, which means one cannot mix *chovah* and *nedavah* in the first place, and each *minchah* must be brought in a separate vessel.

*Rav Ashi* responds to *Rav Acha*: *ee salka dach'takh l'Rebbi mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — if Rebbi in principle permitted mixing *chovah* and *nedavah*, and his only objection were the technical problem of how to offer the second *kematz*, there is an elegant solution that bypasses *Rabbi Eliezer* entirely. *Efshar d'mayti shitin b'chad mana v'chad b'chad mana* — one brings sixty in one vessel and one additional *issaron* in a separate vessel, *u'maga lehu v'kometz* — and places the openings of the two vessels touching one another so that the flour forms a continuous surface, then takes the *kemitza*. The *kemitza* from the large vessel addresses the *chovah*, and the single *issaron* in the small vessel ensures there will always be a genuine *nedavah* to draw from. If the person vowed sixty, the entire large vessel is *chovah* and the single separate *issaron* is the *nedavah*, from which the *kohen* takes the *kometz* of the *nedavah* with certainty. If the person vowed less than sixty, the remainder in the large vessel plus the separate *issaron* together constitute the *nedavah*, and again the *kometz* is taken from the separate vessel. In either case, one is offering a genuine *kometz* from a genuine *nedavah* and never burning *shirayim* of the *chovah* under false pretenses. This solution requires no reliance on *Rabbi Eliezer*. Since such a solution exists, if Rebbi believed mixing were permissible, he would have no objection. The fact that Rebbi objects at all must mean his objection is principled — he holds that mixing *chovah* and *nedavah* is simply not permitted.

### Rava: Oil Proportion

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רָבָא אָמַר: דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וּדְכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא אִית לְהוּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר, וְהָכָא בִּפְלוּגְתָּא דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב וְרַבָּנַן קָא מִיפַּלְגִי.
>
> **Translation:** **Rava said** that there is an alternative explanation of the dispute between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: **Everyone agrees** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering brought as **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and everyone accepts** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer. And here they disagree with regard to** the issue that is the subject of **the dispute between Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov and the Rabbis.**

Rava offers yet a third explanation of the dispute. *D'kulei alma mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — everyone agrees one may in principle mix *chovah* and *nedavah*. *V'kulei alma it lehu d'Rabbi Eliezer* — and the concern about the second *kematz* is not the issue, since *Rabbi Eliezer*'s ruling resolves it and everyone accepts it. *Vehacha bepelugta deRabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov veRabbanan kamipalgi* — the underlying dispute is actually between *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* and the *Rabbanan* regarding the proportion of oil required for a *minchah*.

### Rabbi Eliezer Rule

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** דִּתְנַן: אֲפִילּוּ מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עִשָּׂרוֹן נוֹתֵן לָהּ שִׁשִּׁים לוֹג. רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב אוֹמֵר: אֲפִילּוּ מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עִשָּׂרוֹן אֵין לָהּ אֶלָּא לוּגָּהּ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״לְמִנְחָה וְלֹג שָׁמֶן״.
>
> **Translation:** **As we learned** in a mishna (88a): Each tenth of an ephah of flour requires one *log* of oil. Accordingly, **even** if one brings **a meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah of flour, **one adds to it sixty *log*** of oil. **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov says:** Each meal offering, irrespective of its volume, **even a meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah of flour, requires **only its** single ***log*** of oil, **as it is stated** with regard to the offering brought by a poor leper on the day of his purification: "And a tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil **for a meal offering, and a *log* of oil"** (Leviticus 14:21). The juxtaposition of "a meal offering" with "a *log* of oil" teaches a principle that pertains to all meal offerings, that each offering requires only one *log* of oil.

The *Gemara* cites the relevant *tannaitic* dispute: *afilu minchah shel shishim issaron noten la shishim log* — the *Rabbanan* hold that oil is always proportional: one *log* per *issaron*, so sixty *esronim* require sixty *lugin*. *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov omer: afilu minchah shel shishim issaron ein la ela loga* — no matter how large the *minchah*, only one *log* of oil is required per *minchah*, as derived from the verse *shenemar lamincha velog shemen* — "for the *minchah* and a *log* of oil," implying a fixed *log* per *minchah* regardless of size.

### Oil Uncertainty

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי [כְּרַבָּנַן], דְּאָמְרִי: שִׁשִּׁים לוֹג, וְכֹל חַד וְחַד עִשָּׂרוֹן – לוּגָּה קָא שָׁקֵיל.
>
> **Translation:** Rava explains: **The Rabbis** in the mishna **hold in accordance with** the opinion of **the Rabbis,** who disagree with Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov and **who said:** A meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah requires **sixty *log*** of oil. Therefore, in a case of uncertainty, it is possible to bring sixty-tenths of an ephah, **and each and every** measurement of **one-tenth takes** one ***log*.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְרַבִּי סָבַר כְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב, דְּאָמַר אֵין לָהּ אֶלָּא לוּגָּהּ, וְלָא יָדְעִינַן אִי חֲדָא מִנְחָה הִיא – דְּסַגִּי לַהּ בְּחַד לוֹג, אִי שְׁתֵּי מְנָחוֹת נִינְהוּ – דְּבָעֵינַן שְׁנֵי לוּגִּין.
>
> **Translation:** **And Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds in accordance with** the opinion of **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov, who said** that a meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah **has only its** one ***log*** of oil. **And** therefore, when one is uncertain about his vow and brings sixty-tenths, he cannot bring them with one *log* of oil, as **we do not know whether** the entire sixty-tenths **is one meal offering,** so **that one *log* suffices for it,** or **whether they are two meal offerings,** one that is obligatory and one that is a gift, **which require two *log*** of oil. For this reason, there is no way of resolving the uncertainty except by bringing sixty separate meal offerings, with one *log* of oil for each.

The practical consequence for our case is striking. If the oil requirement is always proportional, then whether the vessel contains one *minchah* or two, each *issaron* carries its own *log*, and it makes no difference how the sixty is divided between *neder* and *nedavah*. But if one holds like *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* — one *log* per *minchah*, regardless of size — then it becomes impossible to determine the correct oil quantity when you do not know how many *menachot* are in the vessel. *Velo yadinan ei chada minchah hi desagi la bechad log* — is the entire sixty one *minchah* requiring only one *log*? *Ei shtei menachot ninhu deva'ayan shtei lugin* — or is it two *menachot* requiring two *lugin*? The ambiguity is intractable. Therefore: *Rabbanan savri keRabbanan de'amri shishim log* — the *Rabbanan* hold like the *Rabbanan* of that dispute, and since the oil is purely proportional, each part of the single vessel gets its proportional share of oil regardless of how the *neder*-*nedavah* line falls. *V'Rebbi savar keRabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov de'amar ein la ela log* — Rebbi holds like *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov*, and since the number of *menachot* in the vessel is uncertain, there is no way to assign the correct oil amount, and therefore every *minchah* must be in its own separate vessel with its own determined quantity of oil.

## Exact vs. Minimum

### Rav Ashi Theory

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַב אָשֵׁי אָמַר: בְּקָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל קָמִיפַּלְגִי. רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: קָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל – יָצָא, וְרַבִּי סָבַר: לֹא יָצָא.
>
> **Translation:** **Rav Ashi said** that there is alternative explanation of the disagreement between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: **They disagree with regard to** the *halakha* of one who is obligated to bring **a small** offering **and** instead **brings a large** offering. **The Rabbis hold** that if one is obligated to bring **a small** offering **and brings a large** one instead **he has fulfilled** his obligation, **and Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that in such a case **he has not fulfilled** his obligation. Therefore, in the case of the uncertainty in the mishna, Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds that by bringing one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah one does not fulfill his obligation if he vowed to bring a smaller offering.

*Rav Ashi* offers yet another interpretation of the fundamental dispute: *bekatan vehevi gadol kamipalgi* — the debate concerns whether, when one vows a specific quantity, that quantity represents an exact obligation or merely a minimum. If a vow of X means "at least X," then even if one cannot recall the exact number of *esronim* vowed, bringing sixty suffices in every case — sixty is certainly no less than whatever was said, and one is *yotzei*. But if a vow of X means precisely X, then bringing sixty does not fulfill the vow when a different number was intended, and one is left with a mixture of *neder* and *nedavah* — which then entails all the complications previously described.

### Small/Big Animal

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא אִיפְּלִגוּ בַּהּ חֲדָא זִמְנָא, דִּתְנַן: קָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל – יָצָא, רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: לֹא יָצָא.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But didn't they** already **disagree about this** topic **once? As we learned** in a mishna (107b) that if one said: It is incumbent upon me to bring **a small** bull, **and he brought a large** bull instead, **he has fulfilled** his obligation, as the value of a small bull is included in the value of a large bull. **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He has not fulfilled** his obligation, as the offering that he brought did not correspond to his vow.

*Rabbanan savri katan vehevi gadol yatza* — the *Chachamim* hold that if one vowed to bring a small animal and brought a large one, one has fulfilled the obligation. In other words, the vow sets a minimum: anything greater or equal satisfies.

### Exact Amount Only

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** צְרִיכָא, דְּאִי אִיתְּמַר בְּהָא – בְּהָא קָא אָמְרִי רַבָּנַן, מִשּׁוּם דְּאִידֵּי וְאִידֵּי קוֹמֶץ הוּא; אֲבָל הָתָם, דְּקָא נְפִישִׁי אֵימוּרִין – אֵימָא מוֹדוּ לֵיהּ לְרַבִּי.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: It is **necessary** for the dispute to be mentioned with regard to both cases, **as had** their dispute **been stated** only **with regard to this** case of one who vows to bring a small meal offering and brings a large one instead, there would be room to reason that it is only **in this** case that **the Rabbis say** that he has fulfilled his obligation, **because** both **this** small meal offering **and that** large meal offering are identical with regard to the portion of the offering that is sacrificed on the altar; in both cases **it is a handful. But there,** in the case of one who vows to bring a small bull but brings a large one, **since the sacrificial portions are greater,** i.e., larger, there is room to **say** that the Rabbis **concede to Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi that he has not fulfilled his obligation.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאִי אִיתְּמַר בְּהַהִיא – בְּהַהִיא קָאָמַר רַבִּי, אֲבָל בְּהָא אֵימָא מוֹדֵי לְהוּ לְרַבָּנַן; צְרִיכָא.
>
> **Translation:** **And had** their dispute **been stated** only **in that** case of one who vows to bring a small bull and brings a big one, there would be room to reason that only **in that** case **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says** that the person has not fulfilled his obligation, because the sacrificial portions are larger. **But in this** case of bringing a large meal offering instead of a small one, there is room to **say** that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi **concedes to** the opinion of **the Rabbis.** Therefore, it is **necessary** for the mishna to teach both cases.

*V'Rebbi savar lo yatza* — Rebbi holds that a small animal plus a large animal does not satisfy the vow, because the vow was for something specific, and a fundamentally different-sized animal is not it.

Why does this matter for our *minchah* case? If Rebbi requires exact correspondence, then bringing sixty when one vowed, say, thirty does not fulfill the vow for the thirty — the sixty is not "thirty plus extra" in any meaningful sense for Rebbi. The thirty and the sixty are simply different things. One is therefore left with a mandatory portion (*neder*) and a separate voluntary portion (*nedavah*) that cannot simply be collapsed into the larger quantity. The *Gemara* then notes that this same debate — small animal and large animal — already appears explicitly in a later *mishna* in this *masechet*: *katan vehevi gadol yatza, Rabbi omer lo yatza*. The *Gemara* asks: is it necessary to state the dispute in both contexts? *Tzricha* — yes, both are needed. *De'i itmar beha beha qa'amri Rabbanan mishum de'eidi ve'eidi qomzo* — if we had only heard the ruling in the *minchah* case, we might say the *Rabbanan* permit it there because whether you bring three *esronim* or thirty, in either case a single *kometz* is offered on the *mizbe'ach*, and the offering is structurally identical regardless of size. We might then think that even the *Rabbanan* would agree with Rebbi in the animal case, since a large animal has considerably more *imurin* (the sacrificial fats and organs) to be placed on the *mizbe'ach* than a small one — they are qualitatively different offerings. *Eima modi leh leRabbi* — one might have said the *Rabbanan* agree with Rebbi there. Conversely, *v'i itmar beha baha ika le-meimar le-Rabbi* — had we only heard the ruling in the animal case, where Rebbi objects because a bigger animal places more *imurin* on the *mizbe'ach*, one might have said that in the *minchah* case — where it is all one *kometz* regardless — even Rebbi would concede. *Eima modu le-Rabbanan* — one might have thought Rebbi agrees to the *Rabbanan* there. Therefore both statements are necessary, as *tzricha* indicates: even in the *minchah* case, Rebbi maintains that the vowed amount is exact, not a minimum.

In sum, the *mishna*'s requirement that Rebbi bring sixty separate vessels stems from the conjunction of two principles: first, that a vowed amount is exact rather than a minimum; and second, that there are insurmountable difficulties — whether concerning oil proportions, the prohibition on mixing *chovah* and *nedavah*, or the inability to properly offer the second *kematz* — with placing more than the vowed amount in a single vessel.

## Non-Grain Vows

### Wood Vow: 2 Logs

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְנִי׳ הֲרֵי עָלַי עֵצִים – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִשְּׁנֵי גְזִירִין, לְבוֹנָה – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִקּוֹמֶץ.
>
> **Translation:** MISHNA: One who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to donate pieces of **wood** as fuel for the altar, **must** donate **no fewer than two logs.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to bring **frankincense, must** bring **no less than a handful.**

The next *mishna* opens: *harei alai etzim* — if someone obligates himself to bring wood to the Temple. As the *Gemara* will shortly establish, it is indeed possible to vow wood as a *korban*. *Lo yifchot mi-shnei gzirin* — he may not bring less than two logs. *Tosafot* notes: *davka ki amar etzim aval ki amar etz maytei gzir echad* — specifically when one says *etzim* (the plural form, "wood") is the minimum two logs; if one says *etz* (the singular, "a piece of wood"), a single log suffices.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חֲמִשָּׁה קְמָצִים הֵם: הָאוֹמֵר ״עָלַי לְבוֹנָה״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִקּוֹמֶץ, הַמְנַדֵּב מִנְחָה – יָבִיא עִמָּהּ קוֹמֶץ לְבוֹנָה, הַמַּעֲלֶה אֶת הַקּוֹמֶץ בַּחוּץ – חַיָּיב, שְׁנֵי בָּזִיכִין טְעוּנִין שְׁנֵי קְמָצִים.
>
> **Translation:** The mishna states tangentially: **There are five** *halakhot* pertaining to **handfuls. One who says:** It is incumbent **upon me** to bring an offering of **frankincense, may not** bring **less than a handful. One who pledges** to bring **a meal offering must bring with it a handful of frankincense. One who** intentionally **offers up a handful** of a meal offering **outside** the Temple courtyard is **liable** to receive excision from the World-to-Come [*karet*]. The **two bowls** of frankincense that accompany the shewbread are **required** to have **two handfuls** of frankincense.

The *mishna* continues: *harei alai levonah* — if one vows to bring *levonah* (frankincense), *lo yifchot mi-kometz* — the minimum is one *kometz*. The *mishna* then enumerates five contexts in which a *kometz* of *levonah* appears: *ha-omer harei alai levonah lo yifchot mi-kometz*; *ha-menadev minchah yavi ima kometz levonah* — when one brings a *minchah* as a donation, one brings with it a *kometz* of *levonah*; *ha-ma'aleh et ha-kometz ba-chutz chayav* — one who offers a *kometz* of *levonah* outside the Temple is liable; and *shnei bzichin te'unim shnei kmatzim* — the two golden dishes (*bzichin*) of *levonah* accompanying the *lechem ha-panim* each require a *kometz*, for a total of two *kmatzim* from those dishes. *Tosafot* notes that among the *chidushim* about *ma'aleh ba-chutz* is that even one who offers only one of the two *bzichin* outside is liable, despite it being only half the total *levonah* amount.

### Gold Dinar

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״הֲרֵי עָלַי זָהָב״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינַר זָהָב, ״כֶּסֶף״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינַר כֶּסֶף, ״נְחֹשֶׁת״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִמָּעָה כֶּסֶף. ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יְהֵא מֵבִיא עַד שֶׁיֹּאמַר ״לֹא לְכָךְ נִתְכַּוַּונְתִּי״.
>
> **Translation:** One who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to donate **gold** to the Temple treasury, **must** give **no less than a gold dinar.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **silver** to the Temple treasury, **must** give **no less than** the value of **a silver dinar.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **copper** to the Temple maintenance, **must** give **no less than** the value of **a silver *ma'a*.** One who says: **I specified** the amount of gold, silver, or copper, **but I do not know what I specified, must bring** the maximum amount of gold, silver, or copper, **until** it reaches an amount **where he says:** I am certain that **I did not intend to** donate **that** much.

*Harei alai zahav lo yifchot mi-dinar zahav* — one who vows gold may not bring less than one gold *dinar*.

### Silver Dinar

*Kesef lo yifchot mi-dinar kesef* — for silver, not less than one silver *dinar*.

### Copper Value

*Nechoshet lo yifchot mi-ma'ah kesef* — for copper, not less than a silver *ma'ah*. By way of orientation: a gold *dinar* is worth 25 silver *dinarin*, a silver *dinar* is six *ma'ah*, and a *ma'ah* is thus one-sixth of a silver *dinar*. So when one vows copper, one brings copper whose value is no less than one-sixth of a silver *dinar*.

If one said *perashti v'eini yodeia mah perashti* — I specified but do not remember what — *mevi ad she-yomar lo le-kach nitkavanti*: one brings progressively more until one can say, "I certainly did not intend more than this."

### Wood as Korban

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** גְּמָ׳ תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״קׇרְבָּן״ – מְלַמֵּד שֶׁמִּתְנַדְּבִין עֵצִים, וְכַמָּה? שְׁנֵי גְּזִירִין. וְכֵן הוּא אוֹמֵר: ״וְהַגּוֹרָלוֹת הִפַּלְנוּ עַל קֻרְבַּן הָעֵצִים״. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: עֵצִים קׇרְבָּן הֵם, טְעוּנִין מֶלַח וּטְעוּנִין הַגָּשָׁה.
>
> **Translation:** GEMARA: With regard to one who pledges to donate wood, **the Sages taught** in a *baraita*: The verse states: "And when one brings a meal offering [*korban minḥa*]**"** (Leviticus 2:1). The superfluous word "***korban***" **teaches that one can voluntarily give wood** as an offering for the altar. **And how much** wood must one bring if he does not specify an amount? **Two logs. And** the support for the fact that wood can be brought as a voluntary offering is **as** the verse **states: "And we cast lots for the wood offering"** (Nehemiah 10:35). **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says:** This voluntary donation of **wood is an offering** like a meal offering, **and** therefore it **requires salt and requires bringing** to the corner of the altar, like a meal offering.

The *Gemara* opens: *tanu Rabbanan* — from where do we know that wood may be dedicated as a *korban*? The verse *v'nefesh ki takriv korban minchah* uses the word *korban* before *minchah*, and from this additional term we derive *melamed she-mitnadvim etzim* — that one may bring a vow of wood. The required minimum is two logs, *shnei gezirim*. The *Gemara* adds: *vechen hu omer* — and so it says in Nechemiah: *vehagoralot hipallnu al korban ha-etzim* — "and we cast lots for the wood offering." The designation *korban etzim* — a wood *korban* — in Nechemiah confirms that wood was indeed brought as a formal offering, and this is read back into the *minchah* passage to establish the legal category.

### Rabbi on Wood

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רָבָא: וּלְדִבְרֵי רַבִּי, עֵצִים טְעוּנִין קְמִיצָה. אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא: לְדִבְרֵי רַבִּי, עֵצִים צְרִיכִין עֵצִים.
>
> **Translation:** **Rava says: According to the statement of Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi, **wood** donated in this manner **requires** the **removal of a handful;** just as in the case of a meal offering, a portion of the wood must be removed and sacrificed separately. **And Rav Pappa says** that **according to the statement of Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi, since it is an offering for the altar, the **wood** that is brought as an offering **needs** to be placed on other **wood** to burn, like any other offering that is burned on wood on the altar.

Rebbi derives a further implication: *etzim korban hem* — since wood is now designated a *korban* by virtue of that term, *te'unim melach* — they require salt, just as salt is required on everything called a *korban*. *Vete'unim hagasha* — and they require *hagasha*, the act of bringing the offering to touch the *mizbe'ach*, as is done with a *minchah* (since wood is, like a *minchah*, not an animal offering). *Amar Rava le-divrei Rabbi etzim te'unim kmitza* — Rava extends this further: according to Rebbi, wood also requires *kmitza* — one must take a handful of it, on analogy with the *minchah*. And *amar Rav Pappa le-divrei Rabbi etzim tzrichim etzim* — Rav Pappa pushes to the logical consequence: since the wood is being offered as a *korban* on the *ma'aracha* (the fire pile on the *mizbe'ach*), it must be burned on other wood — the dedicated wood requires additional wood to burn it. This is admittedly somewhat paradoxical, but it follows from treating the wood as a formal *korban* analogous to a *minchah*.

### Frankincense Kometz

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לְבוֹנָה – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִן הַקּוֹמֶץ. מְנָלַן? דִּכְתִיב: ״וְהֵרִים מִמֶּנּוּ בְּקֻמְצוֹ מִסֹּלֶת הַמִּנְחָה וּמִשַּׁמְנָהּ וְאֵת כׇּל הַלְּבֹנָה״. מַקִּישׁ לְבוֹנָה לַהֲרָמָה דְּמִנְחָה – מָה הֲרָמָה דְּמִנְחָה קוֹמֶץ, אַף לְבוֹנָה נָמֵי קוֹמֶץ.
>
> **Translation:** The mishna teaches: One who says: It is incumbent upon me to bring **frankincense, may not** bring **less than a handful.** The Gemara asks: **From where do we** derive this *halakha*? The Gemara answers: It is derived from a verse, **as it is written: "And he shall take up from it his handful, of the fine flour of the meal offering, and of its oil, and all the frankincense** which is upon the meal offering, and shall make the memorial part of it smoke upon the altar for an aroma pleasing to the Lord" (Leviticus 6:8). The Torah **juxtaposes** the **frankincense with the taking up** of a handful **of the meal offering.** This indicates that **just as** the **taking up from a meal offering** is referring to **a handful, so too,** the minimum size of **a frankincense** offering is **also a handful.**

The *Gemara* then asks: *minalan* — from where do we know that a vow of *levonah* is a minimum *kometz*? *Amar de-chtiv*: the verse states *veherim mimenu vekomzo misolet haminchah umishamnah ve-et kol halevona* — "and he shall raise from it a handful of the *minchah*'s flour and its oil, and all of the *levonah*." The *levonah* is juxtaposed with the *kemitza* of the *minchah*: *maquish levonah le-harama de-minchah* — the *levonah* is equated to the *harama* (the raising/offering) of the *minchah*. *Mah harama de-minchah kometz af levonah nami kometz* — just as the *harama* of the *minchah* is a *kometz*, so too the standard unit of *levonah* is a *kometz*. This is how we know that the minimum vowed *levonah* is one *kometz*.

## Altar Default Item

### Default: Frankincense

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״הֲרֵי עָלַי לַמִּזְבֵּחַ״ – יָבִיא לְבוֹנָה, שֶׁאֵין לְךָ דָּבָר שֶׁקָּרֵב לְגַבֵּי מִזְבֵּחַ אֶלָּא לְבוֹנָה. ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא מִכׇּל דָּבָר שֶׁקָּרֵב לַמִּזְבֵּחַ.
>
> **Translation:** § **The Sages taught** in a *baraita* which discusses vows to bring frankincense that one who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to bring **to the altar,** and does not specify what he will bring, **must bring frankincense, as you have nothing that is** entirely **sacrificed on the altar other than frankincense.** One who says: **I specified** what I intended to bring to the altar, **but I do not know what I specified, must bring** one **of everything that is sacrificed on the altar.**

The next *mishna* states: one who says *harei alai la-mizbe'ach* — "I obligate myself to the altar" — without specifying what he is bringing, *yavi levonah* — must bring *levonah* (frankincense). The reason: *she-ein lecha davar she-karav legabei mizbe'ach ella levonah* — there is nothing that is offered to the *mizbe'ach* exclusively except for *levonah*. At first glance this seems a strikingly sweeping claim, since many offerings go on the *mizbe'ach*. The *Gemara* therefore proceeds to eliminate the alternatives one by one.

### Not Olah

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְתוּ לֵיכָּא? וְהָא אִיכָּא עוֹלָה! אִיכָּא עוֹרָהּ לְכֹהֲנִים.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And is there nothing else** that is entirely sacrificed on the altar other than frankincense? **But isn't there a burnt offering,** which is entirely burned on the altar? The Gemara answers that **there is its hide,** which is given **to the priests** and not burned on the altar.

*Ve-ika olah* — what about an *olah*, which is entirely consumed on the *mizbe'ach*? The *Gemara* answers: *or le-kohanim* — the hide of the animal goes to the *kohanim*. Since the skin is not consumed on the *mizbe'ach*, an *olah* is not "entirely" a *mizbe'ach* item.

### Not Bird Olah

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא אִיכָּא עוֹלַת הָעוֹף, אִיכָּא מוּרְאָה וְנוֹצָה.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But isn't there a bird burnt offering,** which is entirely burned on the altar, including its skin? The Gemara answers: **There are** **the crop and feathers,** which are thrown on the ground next to the altar and are not burned.

*Ve-ika olat ha-of* — what about a bird *olah*, which has no hide? The *Gemara* answers: *ika mur'ah ve-notzah* — the crop and the feathers do not go up on the *mizbe'ach*; they are removed and discarded. So a bird *olah* is also not entirely consumed on the *mizbe'ach*.

### Not Libations

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָאִיכָּא נְסָכִים, לַשִּׁיתִין אָזְלִי.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But aren't there** wine **libations,** which are poured entirely onto the altar? The Gemara answers that the wine is not actually poured onto the altar; it is poured into ducts on the side of the altar and **goes** down **to the drainpipes.**

*Ve-ika nesachim* — what about wine libations, which are poured entirely in the Temple service? The *Gemara* answers: *le-shitin azli* — they are poured into the *shitin*, the underground funnels beneath the *mizbe'ach*. When someone says *la-mizbe'ach*, the assumption is that the item is burnt on the fire of the *mizbe'ach*, not poured into cavities beneath it.

### Not Mincha Type

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָאִיכָּא מִנְחַת נְסָכִים? כֵּיוָן דְּאִיכָּא מִנְחָה דְּאָכְלִי כֹּהֲנִים מִינַּהּ, לָא פְּסִיקָא לֵיהּ.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But isn't there a meal offering** brought with **libations,** which is entirely burned on the altar? The Gemara answers that when one says: It is incumbent upon me to bring to the altar, his intent is clearly to bring a type of offering that is only sacrificed on the altar. A meal offering brought with libations is a type of meal offering. Therefore, **since there are** other types of **a meal offering from which the priests eat,** after a handful has been removed from it and burned, a meal offering brought with libations **is not a clear** example of an offering that is sacrificed on the altar in its entirety, and certainly was not **his** intent. Therefore, his intent must have been to bring frankincense.

*Ve-ika minchat nesachim* — what about the *minchat nesachim* (the flour offering accompanying animal sacrifices), which is entirely burnt? The *Gemara* answers: *keivan de-ika minchah de-achli kohanim minah lo paskinah beh* — since there exists a category of *minchah* that is eaten by the *kohanim*, the word *minchah* in a person's mind is not associated with something entirely burnt on the *mizbe'ach*. A person saying *la-mizbe'ach* would not be thinking of a *minchah* as the paradigmatic item.

### Frankincense Unique

*Levonah* is therefore the one item that exclusively and entirely goes up on the *mizbe'ach* as a burnt offering, with no portion going to the *kohanim*, no part discarded, and no component poured elsewhere. It is the default when one says *la-mizbe'ach* without specification. *Tosafot* raises two additional candidates at the bottom of 106b: oil (for the position that one may dedicate oil alone) — rejected because oil is sometimes eaten by the *kohanim* when mixed with a *minchah*, so it does not have the exclusive *mizbe'ach* association; and wood, which Rebbi said is fully burnt on the *mizbe'ach* — but *levonah* remains the paradigmatic case. *Tosafot* also notes that the source for *levonah*'s being independently dedicable is not spelled out in the *Gemara* at this point; the focus here is on its uniqueness as the default *mizbe'ach* item and the minimum unit of one *kometz*.

## Metal Vow Details

### Gold Minimum

The *mishna* states *harei alai zahav* — "I hereby obligate myself to bring gold" — *lo yifchot mi-dinar zahav*: not less than a gold *dinar*.

### Gold Coin Standard

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְדִלְמָא נְסָכָא? אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר: דְּאָמַר ״מַטְבֵּעַ״. וְדִלְמָא פְּרִיטֵי? אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא: פְּרִיטֵי דְּדַהֲבָא לָא עָבְדִי אִינָשֵׁי.
>
> **Translation:** The Gemara challenges: **But perhaps** his intention in using the word gold is not to a coin at all, but to a small **piece [*naskha*]** of gold. **Rabbi Elazar said:** The case of the *baraita* is **where he said** the word **coin.** The Gemara challenges further: **But perhaps** his intention is not to a dinar, but to smaller coins, such as ***perutot*. Rav Pappa said: People do not make *perutot* of gold.** Therefore, it is unlikely that this was his intent.

The *Gemara* asks: *ve-dilma naska* — perhaps when one said gold, one meant a gold ingot or bar rather than a coin? *Amar Rabbi Elazar de-amar matbe'a* — Rabbi Elazar explains: the person said a coin (*matbe'a*). When someone mentions gold in the context of an obligation, it is understood as referring to the standard gold coin.

*Ve-dilma priti* — perhaps one meant small change of gold, gold pennies so to speak? *Amar Abaye priti de-dahava lo avdi inshi* — Abaye responds: people do not make gold small change. Gold's standard unit is the *dinar*, and there is no smaller denomination of gold coinage in common use, so when someone says gold, they unambiguously mean a gold *dinar*.

### Silver Standard

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״כֶּסֶף״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינָר. וְדִלְמָא נְסָכָא? אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר: דְּאָמַר ״מַטְבֵּעַ״, וְדִלְמָא פְּרִיטֵי? אָמַר רַב שֵׁשֶׁת: בְּאַתְרָא דְּלָא סָגוּ פְּרִיטֵי דְכַסְפָּא.
>
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches that one who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **silver** to the Temple maintenance, **must** donate **no less than** the value of a silver **dinar.** The Gemara challenges: **But perhaps** his intention in using the word silver is not to a coin at all, but to a small **piece** of silver. **Rabbi Elazar said:** The case of the *baraita* is **where he said** the word **coin.** The Gemara challenges further: **But perhaps** his intention is not to a dinar, but to smaller coins, such as ***perutot*. Rav Pappa said:** The *halakha* of the *baraita* is stated **with regard to a place where silver *perutot* do not circulate.**

*Kesef lo yifchot mi-dinar* — silver: not less than a silver *dinar*. *Ve-dilma naska* — perhaps a silver ingot? *Amar Rabbi Elazar de-amar matbe'a* — again, Rabbi Elazar: one said a coin. *Ve-dilma priti* — perhaps small change?

Unlike gold, silver small change does exist. *Amar Rav Sheshes be-asra lo sagi priti de-kaspa* — Rav Sheshes specifies that we are speaking of a locale where silver small change is not prevalent, where people use copper coins for small transactions rather than silver fractions. In such a context, when someone says silver, they mean a silver *dinar*, not a small silver coin. In a place where silver small change is common, the analysis might differ.

### Copper Minimum

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״נְחוֹשֶׁת״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִמָּעָה כֶּסֶף. תַּנְיָא: רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב אוֹמֵר – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִצִּינּוֹרָא קְטַנָּה שֶׁל נְחֹשֶׁת. לְמַאי חַזְיָא? אָמַר אַבָּיֵי: שֶׁמְּחַטְּטִין בָּהּ פְּתִילוֹת, וּמְקַנְּחִין בָּהּ נֵרוֹת.
>
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches that one who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **copper** to the Temple maintenance, **must** donate **no less than** the value of **a silver *ma'a*. It is taught** in a *baraita* that **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya'akov says: He must** donate **no less than** the amount needed to forge **a small copper hook.** The Gemara asks: **For what** use **is** that **suitable** in the Temple? **Abaye said: They scrape** the **wicks** from the Candelabrum **with it and clean** the **lamps** of the Candelabrum **with it.**

*Nechoshes lo yifchot mi-ma'ah kesef* — copper: not less than the value of a silver *ma'ah* (one-sixth of a silver *dinar*). A *baraita* presents an alternative formulation: *tanya Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov omer lo yifchot mi-tzinnora ketana shel nechoshes* — not less than a small copper implement of a particular type, rendered here as a kind of fork or tine.

*Le-mai chazei* — what is this implement used for? *Amar Abaye she-mechattin bo pesilos u-mekanchin bo neros* — Abaye explains: it is used to clean out lamps and wicks. *Tosafot* notes this is not specifically the Temple *menorah* but lamps in general. The implement is a small copper tine or probe used to adjust and clean wicks. This explains why the minimum for a copper vow is pegged to this practical object: copper, unlike gold and silver, is not primarily a coinage metal in this context, and its minimum unit is framed not as a coin of a certain denomination but as the smallest useful copper implement or as a quantity worth a *ma'ah*.

### Iron Spike

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בַּרְזֶל, תַּנְיָא, אֲחֵרִים אוֹמְרִים: לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִכָּלְיָה עוֹרֵב, וְכַמָּה? אָמַר רַב יוֹסֵף: אַמָּה עַל אַמָּה.
>
> **Translation:** The mishna discusses pledges of gold, silver, and copper. What is the *halakha* if one says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **iron? It is taught** in a *baraita* that **others say:** He **must** donate **no less than** the amount that can be made into a base and spike designed to **eliminate the ravens [*mikkalya orev*].** The Gemara asks: **And how much** is that? **Rav Yosef said:** One **cubit** in width **by** one **cubit** in length.

One who vows iron: *tanya hachi nami lo yifchot mi-kila orev* — not less than the amount needed to make a spike for keeping away ravens. The Temple roof was equipped with iron spikes to prevent ravens from landing and contaminating the area with droppings, attracted as they were by the smell of the meat offerings. *Ve-kamma* — how much is that? *Amar Rav Yosef amma al amma* — Rav Yosef says: an *amah* by an *amah* of iron. There is an alternative version of the text that renders this identically: *ika de-amri lo yifchot me-amma al amma*. *Le-mai chazei* — what is it used for? *Amar Rav Yosef le-kila orev* — for the raven spike on the Temple roof.
