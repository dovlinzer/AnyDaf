## Menorah Structure



### Lamps Not Attached

We begin at the very bottom of 88b, concluding a discussion about the מנורה, since one of the measures under consideration was the חצי לוג of oil for each lamp. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Karchah states: מנורה באה מן הכיכר ואין מלקחיה ומחתותיה ונרותיה באין מן הכיכר — the מנורה itself came from the כיכר, the talent of gold, but the tongs, the small shovels used for scraping and cleaning, and the lamps themselves did not come from the כיכר. The practical implication is significant: it is considerably easier to clean the lamps if they are not physically attached to the מנורה.

The reasoning flows from the way the תורה assigns the כיכר. The תורה says two things about the מנורה: that it requires a כיכר of gold, and that it is מקשה — beaten, formed as one unit. The assumption is that these two characteristics belong together. Rabbi Yehoshua ben Karchah therefore holds that only the מנורה proper was formed from the כיכר and was part of that unified structure, while the implements and the lamps were not.

### Menorah: One Gold Unit

What, then, does the phrase אלא מה אני מקיים את כל הכלים האלה — "what do I make of all these vessels" — mean, given that the תורה speaks of both the כיכר and all these vessels? The answer is שהיו כלי זהב — it means they are of gold, not that they are from the כיכר and not that they are attached. The גמרא objects: but that they are gold is already explicit, as the verse states ועשית את נרותיה שבעה... מלקחיה ומחתותיה זהב טהור. If the goldness of the implements is already stated explicitly, what purpose remains for the verse?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבִּי יְהוֹשֻׁעַ בֶּן קׇרְחָה אוֹמֵר: מְנוֹרָה בָּאָה מִן הַכִּכָּר, וְאֵין מַלְקָחֶיהָ וּמַחְתּוֹתֶיהָ וְנֵרוֹתֶיהָ בָּאָה מִן הַכִּכָּר. וְאֶלָּא מָה אֲנִי מְקַיֵּים אֵת ״כׇּל הַכֵּלִים הָאֵלֶּה״ – שֶׁהָיוּ כֵּלִים שֶׁל זָהָב.
> **Translation:** The Gemara resumes its citation of the *baraita*: **Rabbi Yehoshua ben Korḥa says:** Only the frame of the **Candelabrum was produced from the talent** of gold, **but its tongs and its pans and its lamps were not produced from the talent. Rather, how do I realize** the meaning of the verse: “Of a talent of pure gold it shall be fashioned **with all these vessels”?** It teaches only **that** all the **vessels** associated with the Candelabrum **were** made **of gold,** even though they were not fashioned from the same gold talent from which the Candelabrum and its lamps were.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** זָהָב בְּהֶדְיָא כְּתִיב בּוֹ, ״וְעָשִׂיתָ אֶת נֵרֹתֶיהָ שִׁבְעָה וְהֶעֱלָה אֶת נֵרֹתֶיהָ וְהֵאִיר אֶל עֵבֶר פָּנֶיהָ. וּמַלְקָחֶיהָ וּמַחְתֹּתֶיהָ זָהָב טָהוֹר״. לֹא נִצְרְכָא אֶלָּא לְפִי נֵרוֹת, סָלְקָא דַּעְתָּךְ אָמֵינָא: הוֹאִיל וּפִי נֵרוֹת אַשְׁחוֹרֵי מַשְׁחַר, הַתּוֹרָה חָסָה עַל מָמוֹנָן שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל,
> **Translation:** The Gemara questions the need for the derivation of the *baraita*: But the requirement that the vessels be made of **gold is explicitly written in** the verse: **“And you shall fashion its lamps seven, and they shall kindle its lamps, and it will enlighten toward its face. And its tongs and its pans shall be of pure gold”** (Exodus 25:37–38); why then is it necessary for the *baraita* to derive this from the phrase “with all these vessels”? The Gemara explains: This derivation of the *baraita* **is necessary only to** teach that the same applies **to** the **mouth of** the **lamps,** where the wicks rest. Otherwise, it might **enter your mind to say** that **since** the **mouth of** the **lamps blackens** and is damaged by the burning wick, therefore the principle that **the Torah spared the money of the Jewish people** should be applied,

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְלֶיעְבֵּד זָהָב כֹּל דְּהוּ, קָא מַשְׁמַע לַן.
> **Translation:** **and** consequently, **let** the mouth of the lamps **be fashioned from gold of any** quality, not necessarily from pure gold. Therefore, the *baraita* **teaches us** that even the mouth of the lamps must be fashioned from pure gold.

### Pure Gold Lips


The גמרא answers: לא נצרכה אלא לפי נרות — the verse is needed only for the lips of the lamps themselves. One might have thought — סלקא דעתך אמינא — that since the lips of the lamps become black from direct contact with fire, and since the תורה is concerned with the money of Israel, התורה חסה על ממונם של ישראל, they need not be made of pure gold. Rashi explains this to mean a gold alloy rather than pure gold, since pure gold would be damaged precisely where the fire burns. Therefore, קא משמע לן: the verse teaches that despite this concern, the lips too must be of pure gold.

This also concludes our מנורה discussion, though what remains especially interesting is the question raised in the previous session: whether the lamps were physically connected, and how the מנורה was relit if a lamp went out during the night. The simple reading of the גמרא suggested one position, while the Rambam's strikingly different reading — that both the cleaning and the relighting were performed twice daily, at the onset of night and again first thing in the morning — stands against the other ראשונים and many parallel גמרות.

## Todah Oil & Ratios



### Todah Half Log

At the top of 89a, the משנה states: חצי לוג שמן לתודה — a half לוג of oil for the תודה. The normal amount of oil accompanying a מנחה is a לוג per עשרון. The תודה, however, includes עשרה עשרונות for the ten loaves of חמץ and עשרה עשרונות for the thirty loaves of מצה. The חצי לוג under discussion here applies only to the מצה loaves, meaning a half לוג for thirty loaves — עשרה עשרונות. This yields a ratio of roughly one to twenty, far less oil than the standard one-to-one ratio of a לוג per עשרון.

### Double Oil Mention

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חֲצִי לוֹג שֶׁמֶן לְתוֹדָה.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches: **A half-*log* of oil** was used to measure oil **for the thanks offering.** The thanks offering was accompanied by four different types of loaves; three types were unleavened and one was leavened, as the verse states: “If he offers it for a thanks offering, then he shall offer with the sacrifice of thanks offering: Unleavened loaves mixed with oil; and unleavened wafers spread with oil; and poached fine flour, loaves mixed with oil. With loaves of leavened bread he shall present his offering, with the sacrifice of his peace offerings for a thanks offering” (Leviticus 7:12–13).

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תַּנְיָא: רַבִּי עֲקִיבָא אוֹמֵר: מָה תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ שְׁנֵי פְּעָמִים? אִילּוּ לֹא נֶאֱמַר אֶלָּא ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ אֶחָד, הָיִיתִי אוֹמֵר: הֲרֵי הוּא כְּכׇל הַמְּנָחוֹת לְלוֹג. עַכְשָׁיו שֶׁכָּתַב ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״, הָוֵי רִיבּוּי אַחַר רִיבּוּי, וְאֵין רִיבּוּי אַחַר רִיבּוּי אֶלָּא לְמַעֵט – מִיעֲטוֹ הַכָּתוּב לַחֲצִי לוֹג.
> **Translation:** It **is taught** in a *baraita*: **Rabbi Akiva says: Why** must **the verse state: “With oil,” “with oil,”** writing it **twice;** why was the first time not sufficient? Because **were** the term **“with oil” stated only once, I would have said** that, with regard to the amount of oil required, the meal offerings that accompany the thanks offerings **are like all** other **meal offerings to** the extent that they require one ***log*** of oil. **Now that** the verse **wrote “with oil,” “with oil,” it constitutes** one **amplification following** another **amplification, and** the principle is that one **amplification following** another **amplification** serves **only to restrict** the extent of the *halakha*. Accordingly, in this case **the verse restricted** the amount of oil used in the three types of unleavened meal offerings **to a half-*log*** for all of them together.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רִיבּוּי אַחַר רִיבּוּי?! חַד רִיבּוּי הוּא.
> **Translation:** The Gemara interjects the citation of the *baraita* to ask: Is this a case of one **amplification following** another **amplification?** It would appear that there is only **one amplification,** as according to the *baraita* the first mention of the term “with oil” is necessary to teach the basic requirement that the meal offerings of the thanks offering require oil, like other meal offerings. How then can it be considered an amplification?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא, אִילּוּ לֹא נֶאֱמַר ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ כׇּל עִיקָּר, הָיִיתִי אוֹמֵר הֲרֵי הוּא כְּכׇל הַמְּנָחוֹת לְלוֹג. עַכְשָׁיו שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״, הָוֵי רִיבּוּי אַחַר רִיבּוּי, וְאֵין רִיבּוּי אַחַר רִיבּוּי אֶלָּא לְמַעֵט, מִיעֲטוֹ הַכָּתוּב לַחֲצִי לוֹג.
> **Translation:** **Rather,** Rabbi Akiva’s statement should be emended, as follows: **Were** the term **“with oil,” not written at all, I would** still **have said** that the thanks offering meal offerings **are like all** other **meal offerings to** the extent that they require one ***log*** of oil. **Now that** the verse **wrote “with oil,” “with oil,” it constitutes** one **amplification following** another **amplification, and** the principle is that one **amplification following** another **amplification** serves **only to restrict** the extent of the *halakha*. Accordingly, in this case **the verse restricted** the amount of oil used in the three types of unleavened meal offerings **to a half-*log*** for all three together.

The ברייתא records Rabbi Akiva's derivation. The verse concerning the תודה reads: חלות מצות בלולות בשמן ורקיקי מצות משוחים בשמן וסולת מרבכת חלות בלולות בשמן — the word בשמן appears three times in connection with the three types of מצה loaves. Rabbi Akiva asks: why does the verse say בשמן twice? Had it said בשמן only once, I would have assumed it is like all other מנחות and receives a לוג — even though that is already a לוג for עשרה עשרונות, which is itself unusual. But since it says בשמן twice, this constitutes ריבוי אחר ריבוי, and ואין ריבוי אחר ריבוי אלא למעט — a doubled expansive formulation comes only to diminish. Hence, מיעטו הכתוב לחצי לוג — the verse reduces the amount to a half לוג.

The גמרא, however, raises a technical difficulty: is this truly ריבוי אחר ריבוי? The first בשמן is needed simply to indicate that oil is required at all — had it only said it was a מנחה, one would already know that oil is needed. So the first בשמן is already a ריבוי in its own right, and the second בשמן constitutes ריבוי אחר ריבוי, from which we derive the reduction.

### Three Oil Mentions

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** יָכוֹל יְהֵא חֲצִי לוֹג זֶה מִתְחַלֵּק לִשְׁלֹשֶׁת מִינִין, לַחַלּוֹת וְלָרְקִיקִין וְלָרְבִיכָה? כְּשֶׁהוּא אוֹמֵר ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ בָּרְבִיכָה, שֶׁאֵין תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר, רִיבָּה שֶׁמֶן לָרְבִיכָה. הָא כֵּיצַד? מֵבִיא חֲצִי לוֹג שֶׁמֶן וְחוֹצֵיהוּ [חֶצְיוֹ] לַחַלּוֹת וְלָרְקִיקִין, וְחֶצְיוֹ לָרְבִיכָה.
> **Translation:** The Gemara resumes its citation of the *baraita*: Once it has been established that the meal offerings require only a half-*log* of oil, one **might** have thought that **this half-*log* should be** equally **divided between** the **three types** of unleavened meal offerings, i.e., one-sixth of a *log* **for** the **loaves,** one-sixth **for** the **wafers, and** one-sixth **for** the **poached** loaves. **When** the verse **states: “With oil,” with regard to** the **poached** loaves, that is seemingly superfluous, **as** there is **no** need for **the verse to state** the requirement to use part of the half-*log* of oil, being that this requirement has already been established. It must therefore serve to **amplify** the amount of **oil** used **for a poached** loaf as opposed to the other two types. **How so? One brings a half-*log* of oil and divides it** equally into two. **Half of it** is further divided and used both **for** the ten regular **loaves and for** the ten **wafers, and** the other **half of it** is used entirely **for** the ten **poached** loaves.

From here, the גמרא considers the third mention of בשמן. One might think the half לוג is divided equally among the three types of מצה: the חלות, the רקיקין, and the רביכה. This would mean taking chatzi log and dividing it into three. But כשהוא אומר בשמן ברביכה — the fact that the verse says בשמן a third time by the רביכה specifically, where שאין תלמוד לומר — there was no need to state it, tells us שריבה שמן לרביכה — the רביכה receives more oil than the חלות and the רקיקין.

The practical calculation: מביא חצי לוג שמן וחוציהו — take the half לוג and divide it in half. חציו לחלות ורקיקין — one half, a רביעית, goes to the חלות and רקיקין together. וחציו לרביכה — and the other רביעית goes to the רביכה alone. A רביעית is roughly three and a half ounces; one might reflect on how small this amount is relative to the flour involved. The thirty מצה loaves are made from עשרה עשרונות — approximately ten five-pound bags of flour — and the entire oil allotment for all of them is a half לוג, amounting to about six ounces total.

The ten loaves of חמץ that are also part of the תודה have their own separate half לוג. And of the thirty מצה loaves, which are ten each of חלות, רקיקין, and רביכה, each group of ten is made from שלשה ושליש עשרונות. The twenty loaves of חלות and רקיקין share one רביעית, while the ten רביכה loaves receive the other רביעית — hence twice as much oil proportionally for the רביכה, though proportionally it remains tiny in comparison to the standard מנחה. As for which loaves precisely receive more based on the sequence of the verse, the גמרא does not resolve this definitively.

### Halacha L'Moshe

Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah responds to Rabbi Akiva with characteristic directness: עקיבא, אם אתה מרבה כל היום כולו בשמן בשמן איני שומע לך — you can multiply בשמנים all day long, I will not accept it. This is not how we know these things. Rashi understands Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah to be bothered specifically by the technical point the גמרא raised — that it is not truly ריבוי אחר ריבוי, since the first בשמן functions for its own sake. But the objection seems more fundamental: there is no reliable path from textual nuances of this kind to precise quantitative conclusions, and Rabbi Akiva's method of deriving exact שיעורים from the repetition of words is simply not accepted by all תנאים.

This exchange is part of a recognizable pattern in the גמרא. It is not always the same תנא who challenges Rabbi Akiva — sometimes it is Rabbi Yishmael, sometimes others — but Rabbi Akiva is consistently the one addressed. His approach, according to the גמרא, extended even to דרשת הקוצו של יוד, finding legal significance in the smallest textual details. Other תנאים did not reject his conclusions outright in all cases, but insisted that you cannot reach those conclusions through hermeneutics alone. Perhaps the ruling is known from tradition; perhaps it is learned elsewhere. But you will not get everything from textual derivation alone.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר לוֹ רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר בֶּן עֲזַרְיָה: עֲקִיבָא, אִם אַתָּה מְרַבֶּה כׇּל הַיּוֹם כּוּלּוֹ ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ ״בַּשֶּׁמֶן״ – אֵינִי שׁוֹמֵעַ לָךְ, אֶלָּא חֲצִי לוֹג שֶׁמֶן לְתוֹדָה, וּרְבִיעִית שֶׁמֶן לְנָזִיר, וְאַחַד עָשָׂר יוֹם שֶׁבֵּין נִדָּה לְנִדָּה – הֲלָכָה לְמֹשֶׁה מִסִּינַי.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya said to** Rabbi Akiva: **Akiva,** even **if you** were to **amplify** *halakhot* **the entire day** from the terms **“with oil,” “with oil,” I** would **not listen to you** and accept your claims. **Rather,** the *halakha* that **a half-*log* of oil** is required **for** the **thanks offering, and** similarly, the *halakha* that **a quarter-*log* of oil** is required **for** the loaves of **a nazirite, and** the *halakha* that a woman who experiences an emission of blood during the **eleven days that are between** one seven-day period of **menstruation** and the next period of **menstruation** is a *zava*, each of these is **a *halakha*** transmitted **to Moses from Sinai;** they are not derived from verses.

### Mesorah & Shiurim


Rabbi Elazar ben Azaryah then states what these שיעורים truly are: חצי לוג שמן לתודה — the half לוג for the תודה; ורביעית שמן לנזיר — the quarter לוג for the נזיר's מנחה, which is also far less than the standard לוג per עשרון; ואחד עשר יום שבין נדה לנדה — and the eleven days between נדה cycles. All of these are הלכה למשה מסיני. They come from tradition, not from hermeneutical derivation.

The inclusion of the eleven days warrants brief explanation, particularly since it will be treated at length in מסכת נדה. According to the תורה, when a woman bleeds during her menstrual cycle, she is a נדה for seven days, after which she immerses in the מקווה. If she bleeds outside her menstrual period — בלא עת נדתה — and does so for three consecutive days, she becomes a זבה, requiring seven clean days before immersion. The question is: what constitutes "outside her menstrual period"? The answer, transmitted as הלכה למשה מסיני, is that after the seven days of נדה, there is an eleven-day window during which any bleeding is considered non-menstrual. Any bleeding after those eleven days may again be menstrual. Together, this implies an assumed minimum menstrual cycle of eighteen days.

It is worth noting that there is indeed a ספרא that attempts to derive the eleven days from the פסוקים, but that discussion belongs to מסכת נדה.

The broader methodological point here is a rich one. There is a genuine debate, both among ראשונים and scholars generally, about whether the חכמים began with knowledge of the הלכה and worked backward to find textual support — אסמכתא — or whether they were actually deriving the law from the text. The truth is probably somewhere in between and varies by case. When a real מחלוקת turns on how to read a פסוק, one gets the sense of genuine derivation. In other cases, the sense is stronger that the conclusion was known and the verse is being recruited in its support.

The Rambam addressed this indirectly in his discussion of גזירה שוה. He was troubled by the fact that the גמרא requires a מסורה for a גזירה שוה to be used, yet the גמרא still entertains questions like "why derive it this way rather than that way?" — which would make no sense if the specific derivation were itself the מסורה. The Rambam's resolution is that the מסורה is not for the particular גזירה שוה, but for the principle that the law may be derived through גזירה שוה — while the specific construction of the derivation remains open to analysis. This represents a middle position between pure אסמכתא and genuine textual derivation: the conclusion is known, the general method of derivation is authorized, and the work of the תנאים is to identify the most coherent path between the text and the known result.

## Log Per Mincha



### Fixed Log Measure

The משנה states that one always measures with a לוג, even for a מנחה of sixty עשרונות — one does not use a larger measure but simply measures with a לוג sixty times. This parallels the rule for solid measures, where the only vessel is an עשרון: even for a large מנחה, one measures out sixty times.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בְּלוֹג הָיָה מוֹדֵד.
> **Translation:** § The mishna states that with the vessel of one ***log* one would measure** the oil for all the standard meal offerings. It then cites a dispute between the Rabbis, who hold that one *log* of oil is required for each tenth of an ephah of flour used, and Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov, who holds that each meal offering, irrespective of its size, requires only one *log* of oil.

### Metzora Paradigm


> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: בִּמְצוֹרָע עָנִי כְּתִיב ״עִשָּׂרוֹן ... בָּלוּל ... וְלֹג״, לִימֵּד עַל עִשָּׂרוֹן שֶׁטָּעוּן לוֹג, דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים.
> **Translation:** **The Sages taught** in a *baraita*: **With regard to a poor leper,** who cannot afford the standard offerings that are required as part of the purification process and is instead required to bring one lamb as a guilt offering and a tenth of an ephah of flour as a meal offering, **it is written:** “And **a tenth**-**part** of an ephah of fine flour **mixed** with oil for a meal offering, **and a *log*** of oil” (Leviticus 14:21). The verse juxtaposes the need for a *log* of oil with the fact the offering is made of a tenth of an ephah of flour. Accordingly, it **teaches about** each **tenth** of an ephah of flour **that it requires** one ***log* of oil;** this is **the statement of the Rabbis.**

The ברייתא establishes: במצורע עני כתיב עשרון סולת בלול בשמן ולוג שמן — it says of the poor מצורע, one עשרון of flour mixed with oil, and a לוג of oil. לימד על עשרון שטעון לוג — this teaches that each עשרון requires a לוג. That is the position of the חכמים.

The verse in question reads: ואם דל הוא ואין ידו משגת ולקח כבש אחד אשם לתנופה לכפר עליו ועשרון סולת אחד בלול בשמן למנחה ולג שמן — if he is poor, he brings one lamb for an אשם, one עשרון of flour mixed with oil for a מנחה, and a לוג of oil. It must be noted that this לוג of oil is not what is mixed into the מנחה; it is used for the ritual of the מצורע itself, applied to the ear and so on. Yet because the verse juxtaposes an עשרון with a לוג, we use it as the paradigm establishing a לוג per עשרון for all מנחות. This is not the plain meaning of the verse — it is a דרשה — but that is how the ratio is derived.

### Log Ratio Debate

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבִּי נְחֶמְיָה וְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר אוֹמְרִים: אֲפִילּוּ מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים אֵין לָהּ אֶלָּא לוּגָּהּ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״לְמִנְחָה וְלֹג שֶׁמֶן״.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Neḥemya and Rabbi Eliezer say:** Each meal offering, irrespective of its size, and **even a meal offering of sixty tenths** of an ephah of flour, requires **only its** single ***log*** of oil, **as it is stated** with regard to offering of a leper: “And a tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil **for a meal offering, and a *log* of oil.”** The juxtaposition of “a meal offering” with “a *log* of oil” teaches a principle for all meal offerings: Each offering requires only one *log* of oil.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְרַבִּי נְחֶמְיָה וְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב, הַאי ״עִשָּׂרוֹן בָּלוּל וְלֹג״ מַאי עָבְדִי לֵיהּ? הָהוּא לְגוּפֵיהּ, דְּקָא אָמַר רַחֲמָנָא: לַיְיתֵי חַד עִשָּׂרוֹן.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And** as for **Rabbi Neḥemya and Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov, what do they do with this** verse: “And **a tenth**-**part** of an ephah of fine flour **mixed** with oil for a meal offering **and a *log*** of oil,” which links the need for a *log* of oil with the fact the offering is made of a tenth of an ephah of flour? The Gemara explains: **That** verse is required **to** teach the matter **itself, as the Merciful One states: Let** a poor leper **bring** an offering of just **a tenth of an ephah** of flour. Accordingly, it cannot be used to teach a principle about meal offerings.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאִידָּךְ, לְגוּפֵיהּ לָא צְרִיךְ, מִדְּגַלִּי רַחֲמָנָא גַּבֵּי מְצוֹרָע שְׁלֹשָׁה קׇרְבָּנוֹת וּשְׁלֹשָׁה עֶשְׂרוֹנוֹת, הָכָא דְּחַד קׇרְבָּן – חַד עִשָּׂרוֹן.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And** as for **the other,** i.e., the Rabbis, how do they respond to this claim? They maintain that **it is not necessary to** teach the matter **itself,** as **from** the fact **that the Merciful One revealed with regard to a leper** who is not poor that he must bring **three** animal **offerings and** a meal offering of **three-tenths** of an ephah as part of his purification process, it may be inferred that **here,** with regard to a poor leper, **who** brings only **one offering,** that he similarly brings a meal offering of only **a tenth of an ephah.** Accordingly, the fact that the verse mentions that his offering is only a tenth of an ephah is superfluous and can teach a principle for all meal offerings.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאִידַּךְ – אִיצְטְרִיךְ, סָלְקָא דַּעְתָּךְ אָמֵינָא: הוֹאִיל וְחָס רַחֲמָנָא עֲלֵיהּ לְאֵתוֹיֵי בְּדַלּוּת, אֵימָא לָא לִיבְעֵי (מנה) [מִנְחָה] כְּלָל. וְאִידַּךְ – לִגְמָרֵי לָא אַשְׁכְּחַן.
> **Translation:** The Gemara continues to ask: **And** as for **the other,** Rabbi Neḥemya and Rabbi Eliezer, how would they respond to this claim? They maintain that it **was necessary** for the verse to state the size of his meal offering, as otherwise it might **enter your mind to say** that **since the Merciful One spared** the money of the poor leper by allowing him **to bring** an offering affordable **in poverty,** one might **say** that the Merciful One **does not require from him** to bring any meal offering **at all.** The Gemara asks: **And** as for **the other,** the Rabbis, how would they respond to this? They claim that **we do not find** that the Torah **entirely** exempts a poor person from the offerings of the purification process, only that it provides a less expensive way to complete that process.

Rabbi Nechemia and Rabbi Eliezer disagree: אפילו מנחה של ששים עשרונות אין לה אלא לוגה — even a מנחה of sixty עשרונות has only one לוג. This follows from reading the verse as למנחה ולוג שמן — not "one עשרון and a לוג" but "the מנחה and a לוג," meaning no matter the size of the מנחה, it receives one לוג.

The גמרא then asks: what do Rabbi Nechemia and Rabbi Eliezer do with the juxtaposition of עשרון and לוג? They respond that ההוא לגופיה — that mention of עשרון is needed to state the law of the מצורע itself: the תורה is telling us that the מצורע brings a מנחה of one עשרון. It is not teaching the לוג-per-עשרון ratio.

The חכמים counter: לגופיה לא צריך — the תורה did not need to specify the עשרון here. One could have derived it by analogy: the non-poor מצורע brings three animals and a מנחה of three עשרונות; the poor מצורע brings one animal of consequence — the אשם — and the others are replaced by birds. In a parallel fashion, the מנחה would reduce proportionally to one עשרון. Thus the תורה need not have stated עשרון, and the fact that it does so links עשרון to לוג as a general teaching.

Rabbi Nechemia and Rabbi Eliezer respond: איצטריך — the תורה did need to state the עשרון, because one might have thought that since הואיל וחס רחמנא עליה לאתויי בדלות — the תורה showed compassion on this poor מצורע by allowing him to bring less — אימא לא ליבעי מיניה כלל — perhaps the מנחה requirement is waived entirely. The חכמים rejoin: לגמרי לא אשכחן — we never find the תורה eliminating a requirement entirely; it only substitutes. Since the תורה replaced two sheep with two birds rather than eliminating them, it would never simply drop the מנחה.

This entire dispute thus turns on whether the word עשרון is redundant. If it is redundant, it serves to establish the universal ratio of לוג per עשרון. If it is not redundant — if it was needed to specify that the מצורע brings one עשרון at all — then it establishes only the local law of the מצורע and teaches nothing about the universal ratio.

### Derivation Methods

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְרַבָּנַן, הַאי ״לְמִנְחָה וְלֹג שֶׁמֶן״ מַאי עָבְדִי לֵיהּ? הָהוּא לַמִּתְנַדֵּב מִנְחָה, שֶׁלֹּא יִפְחוֹת מִדָּבָר הַטָּעוּן לוֹג, וּמַאי נִיהוּ? עִשָּׂרוֹן. וְאִידַּךְ, תַּרְתֵּי שָׁמְעַתְּ מִינַּהּ.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And** as for **the Rabbis, what do they do with** the juxtaposition in the end of **this** verse: **“For a meal offering and a *log* of oil,”** from which Rabbi Neḥemya and Rabbi Eliezer derive the principle that each meal offering requires only one *log* of oil? The Gemara explains: The Rabbis maintain **that** the juxtaposition teaches **about one who donates a meal offering,** without specifying its size, **that he should not** bring **less than an amount** of flour **that requires** one ***log*. And what is this** amount? **A tenth** of an ephah. The Gemara asks: **And** as for **the other,** Rabbi Neḥemya and Rabbi Eliezer, from where do they derive that *halakha*, as they maintain the verse teaches the principle for all meal offerings? The Gemara explains: They maintain that **two** *halakhot* **can be derived from** this verse.

This discussion opens onto a broader methodological question that is worth dwelling upon. As the text of the תורה does not provide a שיעור for how much oil goes into a מנחה — it simply says the flour is mixed with oil — the entire enterprise of establishing a שיעור requires a derivation, and that derivation comes from this one case of the מצורע, where the לוג is technically not the amount mixed into the מנחה at all, but is the separate לוג used for the ritual. The juxtaposition alone is the basis for establishing the ratio. Then there is a further dispute as to what that ratio even is.

One might reasonably conclude that in cases such as this, the חכמים knew the הלכה and were working to identify its textual grounding. This is the middle position the Rambam implies: not pure אסמכתא, in which the derivation is entirely post-hoc and decorative, and not pure textual derivation, in which the law is unknown until the verse is analyzed. Rather, there is a tradition that the law is derivable from the text, and the work of derivation — though not free-form — proceeds with a known destination in mind. The Rambam raises this tension in the specific context of גזירה שוה: the גמרא requires a מסורה for a גזירה שוה, yet it still entertains questions about why one formulates the גזירה שוה one way rather than another. His resolution is that the מסורה establishes not the specific גזירה שוה but only that the law is accessible through one — leaving the precise construction open to analysis. This captures the middle position well: one knows where one is going, and one knows there is a textual path, without the path itself being fixed in advance.

The גמרא's final statement here is worth noting: when the משנה says it teaches about the minimum size of a מנחה — that it cannot be less than an עשרון — this follows from למנחה ולוג: something that requires a לוג must by definition be at least an עשרון. Rabbi Nechemia and Rabbi Eliezer extract two teachings from this phrase simultaneously: the minimum size of a מנחה, and the rule that a מנחה of any size receives only one לוג.

## Nesachim Ratios



### Hin Conversion

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** שִׁשָּׁה לַפָּר, אַרְבָּעָה לָאַיִל, שְׁלֹשָׁה לַכֶּבֶשׂ. מְנָלַן? דִּכְתִיב: ״וְנִסְכֵּיהֶם חֲצִי הַהִין יִהְיֶה לַפָּר״.
> **Translation:** § The mishna lists the quantities of oil and wine that were required for the meal offerings and libations that accompanied the sacrifice of an animal. **Six** *log*, i.e., one-half of a *hin*, **for** those of **a bull; and four** *log*, i.e., one-third of a *hin*, **for** those of **a ram; and three** *log*, i.e., one-quarter of a *hin*, **for** those of **a lamb.** The Gemara asks: **From where do we** derive these quantities? We derive them from a verse, **as it is written: “And their libations shall be one-half of a *hin* of wine for a bull,** and one-third of a *hin* for the ram, and one-quarter of a *hin* for a lamb” (Numbers 28:14).

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** הִין – תְּרֵיסַר לוּגֵּי הָוַיִין, דִּכְתִיב: ״שֶׁמֶן זַיִת הִין״, וּכְתִיב: ״שֶׁמֶן מִשְׁחַת קֹדֶשׁ יִהְיֶה זֶה לִי לְדֹרֹתֵיכֶם״, זֶה בְּגִימַטְרִיָּא תְּרֵיסַר הָוַיִין.
> **Translation:** The Gemara elaborates: Now, one ***hin* is twelve *log*, as it is written: “And of olive oil a *hin*”** (Exodus 30:24), **and it is written** afterward in the same verse: **“Sacred anointing oil, this [*zeh*] shall be for Me, throughout your generations.”** The **numerical value [*gimatriyya*] of *zeh* is twelve.** Once it is established that one *hin* is twelve *log*, it is possible to calculate how may *log* are in one-half, one-third, and one-quarter of a *hin*.

The משנה taught: ששה לפר ארבעה לאיל שלשה לכבש — six לוגין for an ox, four for a ram, three for a lamb. These are derived from the rule that a הין is twelve לוגין: half a הין for a פר is six, a third for an איל is four, a quarter for a כבש is three. The question is: how do we know a הין is twelve לוגין?

The verse states שמן זית הין — the oil used for the שמן המשחה was a הין. A second verse states שמן משחת קודש יהיה זה לי לדורותיכם — this shall be the holy oil of anointing. The word זה בגימטריא תריסר הוי — the numerical value of זה is twelve (zayin = 7, heh = 5), hence a הין is twelve לוגין. One might ask why a scriptural verse is needed for what might seem to be a self-evident conversion between known measures, but the תורה mentions both לוג and הין without ever stating the conversion factor explicitly. Rather than rely on transmitted knowledge alone, the גמרא anchors the conversion in a textual derivation — though here too, one presumes this is effectively an אסמכתא.

### Menorah Night Oil

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** שְׁלֹשָׁה וּמֶחֱצָה לַמְּנוֹרָה, חֲצִי לוֹג לְכׇל נֵר. מְנָא הָנֵי מִילֵּי? דְּתָנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״מֵעֶרֶב עַד בֹּקֶר״ – תֵּן לָהּ מִדָּתָהּ שֶׁתְּהֵא דּוֹלֶקֶת וְהוֹלֶכֶת מֵעֶרֶב עַד בֹּקֶר. דָּבָר אַחֵר: ״מֵעֶרֶב עַד בֹּקֶר״ – אֵין לְךָ עֲבוֹדָה שֶׁכְּשֵׁירָה מֵעֶרֶב עַד בֹּקֶר אֶלָּא זוֹ בִּלְבַד. וְשִׁיעֲרוּ חֲכָמִים חֲצִי לוֹג מֵאוּרְתָּא וְעַד צַפְרָא.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches: **Three and a half** *log* of oil were required **for the Candelabrum,** as there were seven lamps and **a half-*log*** was required **for each lamp.** The Gemara asks: **From where are these matters** derived? They are derived from a verse, **as the Sages taught** in a *baraita*: The verse states with regard to the Candelabrum: “Aaron and his sons shall arrange it **from evening to morning,** before the Lord” (Exodus 27:21). This indicates that **you shall put into** each lamp **its** required **quantity** of oil **so that it will continue burning from evening until morning. Alternatively,** the phrase **“from evening to morning”** indicates that the mitzva is fulfilled throughout the night, and **you have no** other **rite that is valid from evening until morning except for this one alone. And the Sages calculated** that **a half-*log*** of oil for each lamp is necessary to ensure that they continue burning **from evening until morning.**


The משנה also taught: שלשה ומחצה למנורה חצי לוג לכל נר — three and a half לוגין for the מנורה, a half לוג per lamp. The ברייתא derives this from the verse מערב עד בוקר — the oil must be sufficient to burn from evening through the morning. The teaching is twofold: first, that the oil must be measured to last the full night; and second, that the lighting of the מנורה is אין לך עבודה שכשירה מערב עד בוקר אלא זו בלבד — the only עבודה that is valid specifically from evening through the night. ושיערו חכמים חצי לוג מאורתא ועד צפרא — the חכמים estimated that a half לוג per lamp, half a לוג from evening until morning, is the required amount.

### Estimation Methods

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אִיכָּא דְּאָמְרִי: מִלְּמַעְלָה לְמַטָּה שִׁיעֲרוּ, וְאִיכָּא דְּאָמְרִי: מִמַּטָּה לְמַעְלָה שִׁיעֲרוּ.
> **Translation:** How did the Sages reach the conclusion that a half-*log* of oil is needed? **There are** those **who say** that the Sages **calculated** it **by** initially using a large quantity of oil, more than necessary to burn throughout the night, and then **decreasing** the quantity by a small amount each night until they saw that at the end of the night there was no oil remaining. **And there are** those **who say** that **they calculated** it **by** initially using a small quantity of oil and then **increasing** the quantity each night until they saw that the quantity was sufficient to allow the lamps to burn throughout the night.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַאן דְּאָמַר מִמַּטָּה לְמַעְלָה שִׁיעֲרוּ – הַתּוֹרָה חָסָה עַל מָמוֹנָן שֶׁל יִשְׂרָאֵל, וּמַאן דְּאָמַר מִמַּעְלָה לְמַטָּה שִׁיעֲרוּ – אֵין עֲנִיּוּת בִּמְקוֹם עֲשִׁירוּת.
> **Translation:** The Gemara elaborates: The **one who said** that **they calculated** it **by increasing** the quantity each night holds that they did so in accordance with the principle that **the Torah spared the money of the Jewish people,** so the Sages wished to minimize the financial cost of their experimentation. **And** the **one who said** that **they calculated** it **by decreasing** the quantity each night holds that in the Temple one’s actions should not be motivated by a concern for the financial cost, as **in a place of wealth there is no poverty.**

The גמרא records two views on how this estimation was actually conducted. איכא דאמרי מלמעלה למטה שיערו — some say they began with a larger amount and reduced it incrementally, and איכא דאמרי מלמטה למעלה שיערו — others say they began with a smaller amount and increased it.

The rationale behind each approach reflects competing values. According to the view that they began with less and worked upward: התורה חסה על ממונן של ישראל — since the oil, once sanctified in a כלי שרת, could not be discarded if extra remained, the concern was not to waste consecrated oil, and therefore one would approach the minimum cautiously from below. According to the view that they began with more and worked downward: אין עניות במקום עשירות — one should not behave with a spirit of poverty in a place that is to express blessing and abundance. Rather than minimizing carefully, one expresses generosity and works down from abundance.

It is worth noting, as the ירושלמי records, that in the summer months when the nights are short, thicker wicks were used so the oil would not continue burning into the next day, while in the winter thinner wicks were used to ensure the oil lasted through the longer nights. The חכמים's estimation of the half לוג was thus calibrated for an average night, with the wick size adjusted seasonally.

### Tosafot Challenge

תוספות raises a pointed question on the phrase ושיערו חכמים: למה היה צריך לקדש השמן בכלי שרת? Why was it necessary to sanctify the מנורה oil in a כלי שרת at all? The גמרא had already assumed that the measuring vessels used for the מנורה oil served as כלי שרת, and that this was in fact the only reason a half-לוג measure was needed as a כלי שרת.

Tosafot's question cuts to something fundamental. Normally a כלי שרת is required because the object being sanctified is a קרבן — flour is offered, wine is poured, oil is mixed into a מנחה. But the oil of the מנורה is not being offered. One is not quantitatively sacrificing a certain amount; the goal is to achieve light. If it is a short night, less oil suffices. This is unlike a מנחה or a wine libation, where precise quantities are intrinsic to the act of offering. The proof is precisely the שיעור discussion: had this been an offering of a fixed quantity, the question of the night's length would be irrelevant.

This observation sharpens a broader distinction: the הדלקת המנורה is an עבודה performed in the מקדש, but it may not be a קרבן in the technical sense. This distinction has implications elsewhere — for example, in the discussion of why the principle of טומאה הותרה בציבור was not invoked to override the need for pure oil at the time of the miracle of Chanukah. If the מנורה is not a קרבן, the rules governing public offerings in a state of impurity may simply not apply. Tosafot's brief remark thus opens onto a significant question about the nature of the מנורה service.

## Mixing Minchot



### Par-Ayil Mix OK

The משנה taught: מערבין נסכי פר עם נסכי אילים — one may mix the מנחות of oxen with those of rams. This is because the ratio of flour to oil is consistent between them: three עשרונות for a פר with six לוגין of oil and wine, two עשרונות for an איל with four לוגין — both maintain a two-to-one ratio. If those מנחות get mixed together, the proportion of oil to flour has not changed, and one may offer them together on the מזבח.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְנִי׳ מְעָרְבִין נִסְכֵי פָרִים בְּנִסְכֵי אֵילִים, נִסְכֵי כְבָשִׂים בְּנִסְכֵי כְבָשִׂים, שֶׁל יָחִיד בְּשֶׁל צִיבּוּר,
> **Translation:** MISHNA: Many animal offerings are brought together with a meal offering and a wine libation. These additions are collectively referred to as libations. **One may mix together the libations of bulls with the libations of rams;** the meal offerings may be mixed as they both share the same ratio of flour to oil, i.e., two *log* of oil for each tenth of an ephah of flour. Likewise, one may mix together **the libations of lambs with the libations of** other **lambs,** as the meal offerings both share the same ratio of three *log* of oil for each tenth of an ephah of flour. And one may mix together the libations **of** the offering of **an individual with** those **of a communal** offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** שֶׁל יוֹם בְּשֶׁל אֶמֶשׁ.
> **Translation:** And one may mix together the libations **of** an offering brought on one **day with** those **of the day before,** if the meal offerings have the same ratio of oil to flour.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲבָל אֵין מְעָרְבִין נִסְכֵי כְבָשִׂים בְּנִסְכֵי פָרִים וְאֵילִים, וְאִם בְּלָלָן אֵלּוּ בִּפְנֵי עַצְמָן וְאֵלּוּ בִּפְנֵי עַצְמָן וְנִתְעָרְבוּ – כְּשֵׁרִים, אִם עַד שֶׁלֹּא בָּלַל – פָּסוּל.
> **Translation:** **But one may not mix together the libations of lambs with the libations of bulls or** the libations of **rams,** as the meal offerings have different ratios of oil to flour. **And** nevertheless, **if one intermingled** the flour and oil of **these** lamb offerings **by themselves and** the flour and oil of **these** bull or ram offerings **by themselves, and** only then **were they mixed together,** then they remain **fit** to be sacrificed. **If** they were mixed together **before** the oil and flour of each offering **were** independently **intermingled** to form the meal offering, then they are **disqualified.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** הַכֶּבֶשׂ הַבָּא עִם הָעוֹמֶר, אַף עַל פִּי שֶׁמִּנְחָתוֹ כְּפוּלָה – לֹא הָיוּ נְסָכָיו כְּפוּלִים.
> **Translation:** With regard to **the lamb** offering **that comes with the *omer*** meal offering, which is accompanied by another meal offering and a wine libation, **even though** the quantity of flour used in **its meal offering is doubled,** i.e., one uses twice the amount that is generally used for meal offerings that accompany the sacrifice of a lamb, **its** oil and wine **libations were not doubled;** rather, three *log* of oil and three *log* of wine were used, per the standard quantities used for a lamb.

### Keves Mix Forbidden


By contrast, the כבש receives three עשרונות of flour with three לוגין of oil and wine — a one-to-one ratio, rather than two-to-one. Mixing the מנחה of a כבש with that of a פר or איל disrupts the proportions: one becomes too oil-rich and the other too dry. Hence אבל אין מערבין נסכי כבשים בנסכי פרים ואילים — one may not mix the מנחה of lambs with that of oxen or rams.

The משנה also specifies that this applies regardless of whether the קרבן is an individual's or communal, and even when mixing offerings from different days — since the מנחה accompanying a קרבן does not need to be brought on the same day as the קרבן itself: מנחתם ונסכיהם אפילו מכאן ועד עשרה ימים, it may be brought up to ten days later. Provided the מנחות being mixed are of the same proportional type, they may be combined.

### Blila First

The משנה adds a crucial qualification: ואם בללם אלו בפני עצמן ואלו בפני עצמן ונתערבו כשרים — if one first mixed each type of dough separately — the flour with its appropriate oil — and they subsequently became intermingled, the result is כשר. But אם עד שלא בלל פסול — if the mixing occurred before the individual blendings, it is פסול.

The governing principle is that the מצוה of בלילה — the proper mixing of flour with oil in the correct proportions — must be fulfilled. Once each portion of dough has been mixed correctly, it is valid; the subsequent intermingling does not invalidate it even if proportions become confused, because the obligation has already been discharged. The practical distinction, then, is between two stages of preparation: before the actual mixing of flour and oil into dough, one has in effect only a pile of flour with oil poured over it but not yet worked in, and if two such unmixed piles of different proportions are combined at that point, the resulting dough cannot be correctly formed — the proportions have already been compromised. Once the dough has actually been worked together in the right proportions, however, the inadvertent combination of two already-valid portions does not undo what has been properly accomplished.

### Burn Separately

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** גְּמָ׳ וּרְמִינְהוּ:
> **Translation:** GEMARA: The mishna states that different types of libations may be mixed together provided that the meal offerings have the same flour to oil ratio. **And** the Gemara **raises a contradiction** to this from a *baraita* discussing the verse: “And he shall sacrifice, from the peace offerings, a fire to the Lord: The fat covering the innards, and all the fat that is on the innards, and the two kidneys with the fat that is on them, which is over the loins; and the diaphragm with the liver, with the kidneys, he shall take away. And Aaron’s sons shall burn it on the altar, apart from the burnt offering, which is on the wood that is on the fire; it is a fire of a pleasing aroma to the Lord” (Leviticus 3:3–5).

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״וְהִקְטִירוֹ״, שֶׁלֹּא יְעָרֵב חֲלָבִים בַּחֲלָבִים.
> **Translation:** The direct object of the term: **“And they shall burn,”** i.e., the pronoun “it,” is singular, despite referring to the many types of fats listed in the verse. This indicates **that one may not mix fats** of one offering **with the fats** of a different offering, but should burn the parts from each offering separately. Similarly, it follows that the libations accompanying different animal offerings should not be mixed together.

The גמרא raises a challenge. A ברייתא derives from the verse והקטירו — written in the singular — that שלא יערב חלבים בחלבים: one must not mix the fats of one sacrifice with those of another. More broadly, this teaches that anything burned on the מזבח must be burned separately. How, then, does our משנה permit mixing מנחות — which are ultimately burned — even when they are of the same proportional type?

Rabbi Yochanan suggests reading the permissive language of the משנה as אי נתערבו — referring to what happens if they become mixed, not to active mixing לכתחילה. However, the גמרא objects: if so, the prohibitive language later in the משנה — אין מערבין נסכי כבשים בנסכי פרים — would mean that if they do become mixed, they are invalid בדיעבד. But the end of the משנה shows otherwise: ונתערבו כשרים — they are כשר, precisely in the בדיעבד case of having been accidentally mixed. מכלל דרישא לכתחילה קאמר — the opening clause therefore must be stating a לכתחילה permission to mix. The contradiction with the principle of burning separately thus stands.

### Wine Mixing Criteria

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן: ״אִם נִתְעָרְבוּ״ קָא אָמַר.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Yoḥanan says:** The mishna **stated** only that **if** libations of different offerings **were mixed** together one may still sacrifice them, but not that this is permitted *ab initio*. Accordingly, there is no contradiction between the mishna and *baraita*.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אִי הָכִי, וְאֵין מְעָרְבִין נִסְכֵי כְבָשִׂים בְּנִסְכֵי פָרִים וְאֵילִים, וַאֲפִילּוּ נִתְעָרְבוּ נָמֵי לָא? וְהָא מִדְּקָתָנֵי סֵיפָא: בְּלָלָן אֵלּוּ בִּפְנֵי עַצְמָן וְאֵלּוּ בִּפְנֵי עַצְמָן וְנִתְעָרְבוּ כְּשֵׁרִין, מִכְּלָל דְּרֵישָׁא לְכַתְּחִלָּה קָא אָמַר!
> **Translation:** The Gemara infers: **If that is so,** then when the mishna states in its first clause: **And one may not mix together the libations of lambs with the libations of bulls or** the libations of **rams,** the intention is that **even** after the fact, if **they were mixed together,** they are **also not** valid. The Gemara challenges: **But from** the fact **that the latter clause teaches:** If **one intermingled** the flour and oil of **these** lamb offerings **by themselves and** the flour and oil of **these** bull or ram offerings **by themselves and** only then **were they mixed together** then they remain **fit** to be sacrificed, which is explicitly referring to the *halakha* after the fact, **it may be inferred that the first clause stated** the *halakha **ab initio*****.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר אַבָּיֵי: הָכִי קָאָמַר, מְעָרְבִין יֵינָן, אִם נִתְעָרֵב סׇלְתָּן וְשַׁמְנָן.
> **Translation:** **Abaye said** that **this** is what the mishna **is saying:** When different animal offerings are sacrificed, **their wine** libations **may be mixed together** *ab initio*, but only **if their fine flour and their oil** from their respective meal offerings **were** already **mixed together,** albeit improperly.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְיֵינָן לְכִתְחִלָּה לָא? וְהָתַנְיָא: בַּמֶּה דְּבָרִים אֲמוּרִים – בְּסֹלֶת וָשֶׁמֶן, אֲבָל יַיִן מְעָרְבִין.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And** is it correct that one may **not** mix together the **wine** libations of different offerings ***ab initio*** unless their flour and oil were already mixed? **But isn’t it taught** in the continuation of the *baraita* just cited: **In what** case **is this statement,** that one may not mix together parts from different offerings, **said?** It is said only **with regard to flour and oil. But one may mix together** the **wine** libations of different offerings. The implication is that this is the *halakha* even if their meal offerings had not been mixed together.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא אָמַר אַבָּיֵי: הָכִי קָאָמַר – הֵיכָא דְּהוּקְטַר סׇלְתָּן וְשַׁמְנָן, מְעָרְבִין יַיִן לְכַתְּחִלָּה. הֵיכָא דְּלָא הוּקְטַר – אִם נִתְעָרֵב סׇלְתָּן וְשַׁמְנָן, מְעָרְבִין נָמֵי יֵינָן, וְאִם לָאו – אֵין מְעָרְבִין, דִּלְמָא אָתֵי לְאִיעָרוֹבֵי סֹלֶת וְשֶׁמֶן לְכַתְּחִלָּה.
> **Translation:** **Rather, Abaye said** that **this** is what the mishna **is saying:** With regard to offerings of similar types of animals, **where their fine flour and their oil** from their respective meal offerings **have been burned** on the altar, **one may mix together** the **wine** libations ***ab initio*.** And even **where** their fine flour and their oil **have not been burned, if their fine flour and their oil have** at least **been mixed together, one may mix together their wine** libations *ab initio*. **But if** the fine flour and oil have **not** been mixed together, then **one may not mix** the libations **together.** The reason is that if one does mix the libations, **perhaps one will come to mix together** their **flour and oil *ab initio*,** which is prohibited.

Abaye resolves the difficulty with a reinterpretation of the משנה. The prohibition of והקטירו — burning separately — applies to הקטרה, the actual burning. Wine is poured out but not burned; therefore, wine from different sacrifices may be mixed without violating this principle. Oil and flour, however, are burned as part of the מנחה and must be kept separate.

In practice, one would want to maintain this distinction consistently: do not mix wines lest you come to mix flour and oil as well. But in two circumstances, this concern falls away. First: היכא דהוקטר סולת ושמן — once the flour and oil have already been burned, there is no longer any risk of coming to mix them, and the wine may therefore be mixed לכתחילה. Second: if the flour and oil happened to get mixed — and were of matching proportions and hence still valid — then mixing their wines is permitted as well.

Abaye thus reads the משנה as referring throughout to wine, not to the flour-and-oil מנחה itself. The active mixing permitted by the משנה is the mixing of wines, either after the מנחות have been burned or under the condition that the מנחות themselves have already gotten mixed and are כשר. Mixing the flour and oil מנחות actively and לכתחילה remains prohibited on account of the rule of הקטרה.

The intermingling of the second half of the משנה — where pre-mixed doughs that subsequently got mixed together are כשר — remains as a separate teaching about the timing requirement of בלילה: the key moment is when the dough is properly constituted, not when it is burned.

## Omer Lamb Offering



### Double Flour Only

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** כֶּבֶשׂ הַבָּא עִם הָעוֹמֶר, תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״וּמִנְחָתוֹ שְׁנֵי עֶשְׂרוֹנִים״, לִימֵּד עַל כֶּבֶשׂ הַבָּא עִם הָעוֹמֶר שֶׁמִּנְחָתוֹ כְּפוּלָה.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches: With regard to **the lamb** offering **that comes with the *omer*** meal offering, the quantity of flour used in its meal offering is doubled, i.e., one uses twice the amount that is generally used for meal offerings that accompany the sacrifice of a lamb, but the quantities of oil and wine are not doubled. **The Sages taught** in a *baraita*: The verse states with regard to the *omer* meal offering: **“And its meal offering shall be two tenths”** (Leviticus 23:13). This verse **teaches about** the **lamb that comes with the *omer* that** the size of **its meal offering is doubled.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** יָכוֹל כְּשֵׁם שֶׁמִּנְחָתוֹ כְּפוּלָה, כָּךְ יֵינוֹ כָּפוּל? תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר ״וְנִסְכּוֹ יַיִן רְבִיעִית הַהִין״. יָכוֹל לֹא יְהֵא יֵינוֹ כָּפוּל שֶׁאֵינוֹ נִבְלָל עִם מִנְחָתוֹ, אֲבָל יְהֵא שַׁמְנוֹ כָּפוּל שֶׁנִּבְלָל עִם מִנְחָתוֹ? תַּלְמוּד לוֹמַר ״וְנִסְכּוֹ״ – כׇּל נְסָכָיו לֹא יְהוּ אֶלָּא רְבִיעִית.
> **Translation:** One **might** have thought that **just as its meal offering is doubled, so** too **its wine** libation should be **doubled,** i.e., instead of using a quarter-*hin*, as is generally done for lambs, one should use a half-*hin*. To counter this, **the verse states,** in its continuation: **“And its libation shall be of wine, a quarter-*hin*”** (Leviticus 23:13). One **might** have thought that it is only **its wine** libation that **should not be doubled, as it is not intermingled with** the flour of **its meal offering, but its oil should be doubled, as it is intermingled with** the flour of **its meal offering.** To counter this, **the verse states: “And its libation** shall be of wine, a quarter-*hin*,” which teaches that **all of its libations shall be only a quarter**-***hin***, but no more.

The final section of the משנה concerns the lamb brought with the עומר. The תורה specifies שני עשרונים for this lamb's מנחה — double the normal amount of flour. The question is whether the oil and wine are similarly doubled. The ברייתא states: יכול כשם שמנחתו כפולה כך יינו כפול — perhaps since the flour is doubled, the wine is also doubled? The verse answers: ונסכו יין רביעית ההין — the wine is explicitly a quarter הין, which is three לוגין, the standard amount for a lamb. The wine is not doubled.

One might then propose: יכול לא יהיה יינו כפול שאין נבלל עם מנחתו — wine is not doubled because it is not blended with the flour; but שמנו כפול שנבלל עם מנחתו — the oil should be doubled since it is mixed with the flour, and the general principle of the חכמים is that more flour requires more oil. The verse answers again: ונסכו — the full לוג, all of its liquid measures, לא יהיה אלא רביעית — none of them are doubled; all remain at the standard רביעית ההין.

### Niskah-Nisko Equation

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַאי תַּלְמוּדָא? אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר: כְּתִיב ״וְנִסְכָּהּ״, וְקָרֵינַן ״וְנִסְכּוֹ״.
> **Translation:** The Gemara elaborates on the final proof in the *baraita*: **What is the** biblical **derivation** here? **Rabbi Elazar says:** There is an ambiguity as to whether the possessive pronoun in the term “and its libation” is referring to the lamb offering or the meal offering, both of which are mentioned previously in the verse. This is due to a disparity between the way the Hebrew word for the term is written and the way it is vocalized. **It is written** as ***veniskah*,** with the possessive pronoun in the feminine form. This would be referring to the meal offering [*minḥa*], which is a feminine noun. Accordingly, this means: The libation of the meal offering, and it is referring to the oil that is intermingled in the meal offering. **And we read it** as ***venisko*,** with the possessive pronoun in the masculine form. This would be referring to the lamb offering itself [*keves*], which is a masculine noun. Accordingly, this means: The libation of the lamb offering, which is a reference to the wine libation that accompanies the lamb offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** כֵּיצַד? ״נִסְכָּהּ״ דְּמִנְחָה כְּ״נִסְכּוֹ״ דְּיַיִן – מָה יַיִן רְבִיעִית, אַף שֶׁמֶן נָמֵי רְבִיעִית.
> **Translation:** **How** can this be explained? It teaches that **the libation of the meal offering,** i.e., its oil, **is like the libation of** the lamb **of wine: Just as** the quantity of **wine** used is **a quarter**-*hin*, **so too,** the quantity of **oil** used is **a quarter**-*hin* and no more.

The גמרא asks where this is derived. Rabbi Elazar states: כתיב ונסכה וקרינן ונסכו — the word is written with a ה but read with a ו. How are both readings meaningful? נסכה דמנחה כנסכו דיין — the נסכה of the מנחה, meaning its liquid component, is equated with the נסכו of the wine. Just as the wine libation is a רביעית ההין, so too the oil of the מנחה: מה יין רביעית אף שמן נמי רביעית — the oil, like the wine, remains at the standard quantity and is not doubled along with the flour.

## Sheelo Lishmo Status



### Asham Identity

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן: אֲשַׁם מְצוֹרָע שֶׁשְּׁחָטוֹ שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, טָעוּן נְסָכִים, שֶׁאִם אִי אַתָּה אוֹמֵר כֵּן – פְּסַלְתּוֹ.
> **Translation:** § The Gemara cites a related discussion: A guilt offering brought by a leper as part of his purification process is distinct from other guilt offerings in that there is an additional requirement that it must be brought together with a meal offering and a wine libation. **Rabbi Yoḥanan says:** In the case of **a guilt offering of a leper that one slaughtered not for its own sake,** although the leper can therefore no longer fulfill his obligation with it, the offering continues to be regarded as a guilt offering of a leper and still **requires libations,** i.e., a meal offering and a wine libation; **as if you do not say this, you have disqualified it.** To bring it without libations one would have to regard it as a standard guilt offering, but the *halakha* is that an offering may not be sacrificed for a different purpose than the one for which it was originally consecrated.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְקֵיף לַהּ רַב מְנַשְּׁיָא בַּר גַּדָּא: אֶלָּא מֵעַתָּה, כֶּבֶשׂ הַבָּא עִם הָעוֹמֶר שֶׁשְּׁחָטוֹ שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, תְּהֵא מִנְחָתוֹ כְּפוּלָה, שֶׁאִם אִי אַתָּה אוֹמֵר כֵּן – פְּסַלְתּוֹ.
> **Translation:** **Rav Menashya bar Gadda objects to this: If that is so,** that whenever an offering is consecrated for a specific purpose that requires various additional conditions to be fulfilled beyond those normally required for that type of offering, then even if it is then slaughtered not for its own sake those conditions must still be fulfilled in order for the offering to be valid; then with regard to the **lamb** offering **that comes with the *omer*** meal offering the *halakha* should likewise be that if **one slaughtered it not for its own sake,** the flour in **its meal offering should** still **be doubled** in quantity, as normally required for the *omer* meal offering, because it continues to be regarded as an *omer* meal offering, **as if you do not say this, you have disqualified it.**

Rabbi Yochanan states: אשם מצורע ששחטו שלא לשמו טעון נסכים — an אשם מצורע that was slaughtered שלא לשמו still requires its accompanying מנחה. The reasoning: שאם אי אתה אומר כן פסלתו — if you do not say this, you will have invalidated it. Since an אשם מצורע that is brought without its מנחה is invalid, and this sacrifice is still valid as a קרבן even though it does not discharge the owner's obligation, it must retain its defining identity as an אשם מצורע and therefore must be accompanied by its מנחה.

The underlying question is significant. Normally an אשם does not come with נסכים; the מנחה here is unique to the מצורע ritual, as we read earlier in the daf. When such a קרבן is brought שלא לשמו, it is valid but does not fulfill the owner's obligation. Does it then retain its original identity as an אשם מצורע, with all the attendant requirements? Or does it shed that identity and become a generic אשם — which would require no מנחה?

### Identity Retention Test

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְתָמִיד שֶׁל שַׁחַר שֶׁשְּׁחָטוֹ שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, יְהֵא טָעוּן שְׁנֵי גְזִירִין בְּכֹהֵן אֶחָד, שֶׁאִם אִי אַתָּה אוֹמֵר כֵּן – פְּסַלְתּוֹ. וְתָמִיד שֶׁל בֵּין הָעַרְבַּיִם שֶׁשְּׁחָטוֹ שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, יְהֵא טָעוּן שְׁנֵי גְזִירִין בִּשְׁנֵי כֹהֲנִים, שֶׁאִם אִי אַתָּה אוֹמֵר כֵּן – פְּסַלְתּוֹ.
> **Translation:** **And** similarly, with regard to the **daily morning offering,** if **one slaughtered it not for its own sake, it should** still **require** the arrangement of **two logs** of wood on the fire on the altar **by one priest,** because it continues to be regarded as a daily morning offering; **as if you do not say this, you have disqualified it. And** similarly, with regard to the **daily afternoon offering,** if **one slaughtered it not for its own sake, it should** still **require** the arrangement of **two logs** of wood on the fire on the altar **by two priests,** because it continues to be regarded as a daily afternoon offering; **as if you do not say this, you have disqualified it.** The fact that Rabbi Yoḥanan did not mention these *halakhot* suggests that he holds they are not correct, but then his opinion is logically inconsistent.

Rav Menashya bar Gadata challenges Rabbi Yochanan with a series of parallel cases. If the principle is that a קרבן brought שלא לשמו retains its full original identity and therefore all its requirements must be maintained — otherwise it would be invalidated — then consider: כבש הבא עם העומר ששחטו שלא לשמו תהא מנחתו כפולה — the lamb of the עומר brought שלא לשמו should require the doubled מנחה of שני עשרונות, for otherwise you would invalidate it. Similarly: תמיד של שחר ששחטו שלא לשמו יהא טעון שני גזירים ושני כהנים — the morning תמיד brought שלא לשמו should require the two logs of wood and two כהנים associated with the תמיד offering, since the גמרא assumes these logs of wood are an essential part of the תמיד. Likewise for the afternoon תמיד. Each case would equally have to retain its full original character, with all attendant requirements.

The force of the challenge is that it extends the principle to conclusions that seem difficult to sustain. If the identity is fully retained in every case, the consequences ramify broadly.

### Ein Hachi Nami

The initial response recorded in the גמרא is simply: אין הכי נמי — indeed, that is precisely the case. What appears to be a reductio ad absurdum is answered with a matter-of-fact affirmation: yes, even when a קרבן is brought שלא לשמו, it retains its full original identity, and all its requirements — the double מנחה for the עומר lamb, the logs of wood for the תמיד — must be met. If they are not, the קרבן is invalidated.

Tomorrow, Abaye will challenge this position and argue that a קרבן brought שלא לשמו does in fact shed its original identity, becoming generic in character. The tension between these two views — full retention of identity versus a shift to generic status — is the substantive question with which we close.
