## Chatas Pesukim



### Shechitah Source

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חַטָּאת מְנָלַן? דִּכְתִיב: ״וְשָׁחַט אֹתָהּ לְחַטָּאת״ – שֶׁתְּהֵא שְׁחִיטָה לְשֵׁם חַטָּאת.
> **Translation:** § The Gemara asks: **From where do we** derive that if **a sin offering** is sacrificed for the sake of the wrong type of offering or one other than its owner, it is disqualified? The Gemara answers: It is derived from this verse, **as it is written: “And slaughter it for a sin offering”** (Leviticus 4:33), teaching **that** the **slaughter must be** performed **for the sake of a sin offering.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַשְׁכְּחַן שְׁחִיטָה, קַבָּלָה מְנָלַן? דִּכְתִיב:
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **We found** a source with regard to **slaughter. From where do we** derive this with regard to **collection** of the blood? The Gemara answers: It is derived from this verse, **as it is written:**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״וְלָקַח הַכֹּהֵן מִדַּם הַחַטָּאת״ – שֶׁתְּהֵא קַבָּלָה לְשֵׁם חַטָּאת.
> **Translation:** **“And the priest shall take of the blood of the sin offering”** (Leviticus 4:25), teaching **that** the **collection** of the blood **must be** performed **for the sake of a sin offering.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַשְׁכְּחַן שְׁחִיטָה וְקַבָּלָה, זְרִיקָה מְנָלַן? דְּאָמַר קְרָא: ״וְכִפֶּר עָלָיו הַכֹּהֵן מֵחַטָּאתוֹ״ – שֶׁתְּהֵא כַּפָּרָה לְשֵׁם חַטָּאת.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And we found** a source for the *halakha* that the **slaughter and collection** must be performed for the sake of a sin offering. **From where do we** derive this *halakha* with regard to the **sprinkling** of the blood? The Gemara answers: It is derived from a verse, **as the verse states: “And the priest shall make atonement for him concerning his sin”** (Leviticus 4:26), teaching **that** the **atonement must be** effected **for the sake of a sin offering,** as atonement is effected by the sprinkling of the blood.

The eighth daf of Zevachim opens by pivoting from the previous discussion — which established that most *korbanos* must be performed *lishmah* and *l'shem ba'alim* at least *lechatchila*, though failure to do so renders them *kasher* while denying the owners fulfillment of their obligation — to the exceptions enumerated in the *mishnah*: *chatas* and *pesach*. When either of these is performed *shelo lishmo*, the *korban* is entirely *pasul* and cannot be offered on the *mizbeach*. The *gemara* now undertakes the task of locating the scriptural basis for both the *lechatchila* requirement and the *me'akev* status of *lishmah* by *chatas*.

The *gemara* begins with the question *chatas minalan* — where do we learn the law of *chatas*? The relevant *pasuk* states *v'shachat osah l'chatat*, "and he shall slaughter it for a *chatas*." The phrase *l'chatat* — for the purpose of being a *chatas* — establishes that the *shechitah* must be performed *l'shem chatas*. The principle derived is *she'te'hei shechitah l'shem chatat*: the slaughter must be done for the sake of the sin offering. This covers the first of the four *avodos*.

### Kabbalah Source

At the top of 8a, the *gemara* turns to the *kabbalah* — the receiving of the blood in a vessel. The *pasuk* reads *v'lakach hakohen midam hachatat*, "and the *kohen* shall take from the blood of the *chatas*." The formulation *hachatat* — the *chatas* — is a doubling of reference that could have been expressed more simply; the Torah's choice to specify *hachatat* teaches that *she'te'hei kabala l'shem chatat*, the receiving of the blood must also be for the sake of the *chatas*. The term *lakach* is classically understood to encompass both the *kabbalah* and the *holacha*, the carrying of the blood toward the *mizbeach*.

### Zerikah Source

For the *zerikah* — the throwing of the blood on the *mizbeach* — the *gemara* cites *v'chiper alav hakohen michatato*, "and the *kohen* shall atone for him from his sin." The act of atonement is the blood service on the *mizbeach*, and the formulation teaches *she'te'hei kapara l'shem chatat* — the atonement, too, must be performed for the sake of the *chatas*.

### Shinui Kodesh

Having established from these *pesukim* that all the *avodos* must be performed *l'shem chatas* at least *lechatchila*, the *gemara* notes: *ashkachan shinui kodesh* — we have now established the requirement regarding *shinui kodesh*, the case where the *avodah* is performed with intent for a different type of *korban*.

### Shinui Ba'alim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַשְׁכְּחַן שִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, שִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים מְנָלַן? אָמַר קְרָא: ״עָלָיו״ – עָלָיו וְלֹא עַל חֲבֵירוֹ.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **We found** a source for the prohibition against **deviation** from the type of **offering. From where do we** derive the prohibition against **deviation** with regard to the **owner?** The Gemara answers: The aforementioned **verse states:** “And the priest shall make atonement **for him,”** indicating that the intent must be **for him, and not for another.**

The *gemara* then asks *shinui ba'alim minalan* — where do we learn that *lechatchila* one must also have in mind the correct owner? The answer is found in the word *alav* from the same *pasuk*: *v'chiper alav hakohen* — "and the *kohen* shall atone for him." The precision of *alav v'lo l'chavero* — for him and not for another — establishes that the *avodah* must be performed with intent for the actual owner. And the *gemara* concludes: *ashkachan l'mitzva* — all of this establishes the *lechatchila* requirement.

## Me'akev Requirements



### Shinui Kodesh Gap

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַשְׁכְּחַן לְמִצְוָה, לְעַכֵּב מְנָלַן? כִּדְאָמַר רַב הוּנָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב יְהוֹשֻׁעַ: ״חַטָּאת״–״חַטָּאתוֹ״; הָכָא נָמֵי: ״חַטָּאת״–״חַטָּאתוֹ״.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **We found** a source **for** the **mitzva** to sprinkle the blood for the sake of the owner *ab initio*. **From where do we** derive that this is **indispensable** and disqualifies the offering if not fulfilled? The Gemara answers: It is **as Rav Huna, son of Rav Yehoshua, says** with regard to the sin offering of a nazirite: Since the verse could have simply stated: **Sin offering,** but instead stated: **“His sin offering”** (Numbers 6:16), it is inferred that the offering must be sacrificed for his, the owner’s, sake. **Here too,** since the verse could have simply stated **“sin,”** but states **“his sin,”** one infers that if the blood of the sin offering is not sprinkled for the sake of its owner, it is disqualified.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַשְׁכְּחַן מִצְוָה – בְּשִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, וּזְרִיקָה בְּשִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים – בֵּין לְמִצְוָה בֵּין לְעַכֵּב. לְעַכֵּב – בְּכׇל עֲבוֹדוֹת בְּשִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, וּבִשְׁאָר עֲבוֹדוֹת בְּשִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים – בֵּין לְמִצְוָה בֵּין לְעַכֵּב, מְנָלַן?
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **We found** a source for the **mitzva of** sacrificing a sin offering without **deviation** from the type of **offering** *ab initio*, **and** with regard to **sprinkling** its blood without **deviation** with regard to the **owner** concerning **both** its **mitzva** *ab initio* **and** its **indispensability. From where do we** derive that performing **all** sacrificial **rites** without **deviation** from the type of **offering is an indispensable** requirement, **and** that performing **all other rites,** besides sprinkling the blood, without **deviation** with regard to the **owner is both a mitzva** *ab initio* **and indispensable?**

Having established the *lechatchila* requirements for both *shinui kodesh* and *shinui ba'alim*, the *gemara* now confronts the harder question: *l'akev minalan?* How do we know that failure to comply renders the *korban pasul*?

The *gemara* reviews what has been established and what remains to be proven. The *pesukim* of *v'shachat osah l'chatat* and *v'lakach midam hachatat* establish *lechatchila* for *shinui kodesh* across the *avodos*. The *pasuk* of *v'chiper alav hakohen michatato* establishes both the *lechatchila* and the *me'akev* status — but only for *shinui ba'alim* by *zerikah*, because the word *michatato* ("from his *chatas*") reiterates the ownership requirement a second time in the same verse, and the doubling teaches that the *zerikah* with *shinui ba'alim* invalidates the *korban*.

The resulting picture is therefore incomplete in two respects. First, *shinui kodesh*: we know all the *avodos* must be performed *l'shem chatas*, but we do not yet know that failure to do so is *me'akev*. Second, *shinui ba'alim*: we know it is *me'akev* only by *zerikah*, but not by the other *avodos*. The question *l'akev b'chol avodos b'shinui kodesh*, and *b'she'ar avodos b'shinui ba'alim*, is therefore still open.

Rav Huna brei d'Rav Yehoshua had already made a *drasha* from the extra *vav* in the word *chatato* — the possessive suffix — arguing that the reiteration teaches that the ownership requirement is *me'akev*. The *gemara* notes that the same type of reasoning will be applied here: *hacha nami chatat chataso*, the expression *michatato* doubles down on the ownership requirement and teaches *me'akev* status. But this still only covers *zerikah*. The broader application to all *avodos* requires an additional source.

### Chatas Nazir

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹנָה: אָתְיָא מֵחַטַּאת נָזִיר, דִּכְתִיב: ״וְהִקְרִיב הַכֹּהֵן לִפְנֵי ה׳ וְעָשָׂה אֶת חַטָּאתוֹ וְאֶת עֹלָתוֹ״ – שֶׁיְּהוּ כׇּל עֲשִׂיּוֹתָיו לְשֵׁם חַטָּאת.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Yona says: It is derived from** the *halakha* concerning **the sin offering of a nazirite; as it is written** concerning a nazirite: **“And the priest shall bring them before the Lord, and shall offer [*ve’asa*] his sin offering, and his burnt offering”** (Numbers 6:16). The verse teaches **that all of its** sacrificial **actions [*asiyyotav*] must be** performed **for the sake of a sin offering.**

Rav Yona provides the answer: *atya m'chatat nazir* — one learns from the *chatas* of a *nazir*. The relevant *pasuk* states *v'hikriv hakohen lifnei Hashem v'asa et chatato v'et olato*, "and the *kohen* shall bring before God and perform his *chatas* and his *olah*." The word *v'asa* — "and he shall perform" — is generic and encompasses all the *avodos* at once. From *v'asa et chatato*, we learn *sheyaaseh kol asiyotav l'shem chatat* — all of the acts of the *avodah* must be performed for the sake of the *chatas*. This covers the *lechatchila* requirement for *shinui kodesh* across all *avodos*.

Regarding *shinui ba'alim*, the *gemara* adopts the *girsa* of Tosafot: since the *pasuk* of *v'asa et chatato* by *chatas nazir* is superfluous (the *lechatchila* requirement for *shinui kodesh* was already derived from the *pesukim* by *chatas chelev*), it is redirected — *im eino inyan l'shinui kodesh, tneihu inyan l'shinui ba'alim* — to teach that all the *avodos* must also be performed *l'shem ba'alim*.

### Doubling Teaches Me'akev

The *lechatchila* for *shinui ba'alim* across all *avodos* is now established, but *me'akev* remains elusive beyond *zerikah*. It is Rav Huna brei d'Rav Yehoshua who supplies the answer: the extra *vav* in *chatato* — appearing as *michatato* — reiterates the ownership requirement in a way that signals *me'akev* status. Once applied, this covers the *me'akev* requirement for both *shinui kodesh* and *shinui ba'alim* across all *avodos* of *chatas*.

## Me'akev Requirements (II)



### Chatas Chelev vs Nazir

At this point Ravina raises a sharp challenge. The entire structure rests on the assumption that one *chatas* can be learned from another — specifically, that the *pesukim* by *chatas nazir* can be applied to fill the gaps by *chatas chelev*. But this assumption does not go unchallenged. The *gemara* poses the question: *migmari mehadadi? chatas chelev mechatas nazir lo gamri* — can one really learn them from one another? Perhaps not.

---

## Chatas Metzora



### Ravina's Challenge


Ravina challenges the derivation: how can one learn *chatas chelev* from *chatas nazir*? Each *chatas* may have requirements unique to its own context. *Chatas chelev mechatas nazir lo gamri*, the *gemara* states, *sheken yesh immah damim acherim* — a *chatas nazir* comes together with other blood offerings, specifically an *olah*, and therefore one cannot assume that the demands applying to *chatas nazir* transfer to a standalone sin offering. Conversely, one cannot learn in the reverse direction either: *chatas nazir mechatas chelev lo gamar, sheken karet* — a *chatas chelev* is brought for a sin punishable by *karet*, which may entail heavier requirements. Each type of *chatas* carries a distinctive feature that might explain elevated demands, and therefore neither can be derived from the other.

### Minchah Nesachim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַשְׁכְּחַן שִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, שִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים מְנָלַן? (אִם אֵינוֹ עִנְיָן לְשִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, תְּנֵיהוּ עִנְיָן לְשִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים. אַשְׁכְּחַן לְמִצְוָה, לְעַכֵּב מְנָלַן?)
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **We found** a source for the *halakha* that a sin offering must be sacrificed without **deviation** from the type of **offering,** and that this requirement is indispensable. **From where do we** derive that sacrificing it without **deviation** with regard to the **owner** is also indispensable, even in rites other than the sprinkling of the blood?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַב הוּנָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַב יְהוֹשֻׁעַ: ״חַטָּאת״–״חַטָּאתוֹ״.
> **Translation:** **Rav Huna, son of Rav Yehoshua, says:** Since the verse concerning a nazirite could have simply stated: **Sin offering,** but instead states: **“His sin offering,”** one infers that the offering must be sacrificed for the owner’s sake.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְקֵיף לַהּ רָבִינָא: אֶלָּא מֵעַתָּה, ״עוֹלַת״–״עוֹלָתוֹ״ מַאי דָּרְשַׁתְּ בֵּיהּ?
> **Translation:** **Ravina objects to this: If that is so, what do you interpret** from the fact that the same verse could have stated: **Burnt offering,** but instead states: **“His burnt offering”?**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וּלְרָבִינָא, ״מִנְחָת״–״מִנְחָתוֹ״, ״נֶסֶךְ״–״נִסְכּוֹ״, מַאי דָּרְשַׁתְּ בְּהוּ?
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And according to Ravina, what do you interpret** from the subsequent verse: “The priest shall offer also his meal offering and his libation” (Numbers 6:17)? Why does Ravina not mention that this verse could have stated: **Meal offering,** but instead states: **“His meal offering,”** and that it could have stated: **Libation,** but instead states: **“His libation”?**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** הָהוּא מִבְּעֵי לֵיהּ: ״מִנְחָתָם וְנִסְכֵּיהֶם״ – בַּלַּיְלָה, ״מִנְחָתָם וְנִסְכֵּיהֶם״ – אֲפִילּוּ לְמָחָר. אֶלָּא ״עוֹלַת״–״עוֹלָתוֹ״, מַאי דָּרְשַׁתְּ בְּהוּ?
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: **That** language **is necessary;** it is just like the phrase: **“Their meal offering and their libations,”** which is mentioned many times in the portion of the Festival offerings (e.g., Numbers 29:37). It teaches that the meal offerings and libations that are brought with the additional animal offerings of the Festivals can be sacrificed even **at night** after the offering is brought. The phrase: **“Their meal offering and their libations,”** also teaches that they may be brought **even on the following day.** Likewise here, when stated with regard to a nazirite, the phrase teaches that they may be brought at night or the day after the offering is sacrificed. **But** there remains a difficulty: **What do you interpret** from the fact that the verse could have stated: **Burnt offering,** but instead states: **“His burnt offering”?**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְתוּ, מִי גָּמְרִי מֵהֲדָדֵי?! חַטַּאת חֵלֶב מֵחַטַּאת נָזִיר לָא גָּמְרָה – שֶׁכֵּן יֵשׁ עִמָּהּ דָּמִים אֲחֵרִים; חַטַּאת נָזִיר מֵחַטַּאת חֵלֶב לָא גָּמְרָה – שֶׁכֵּן כָּרֵת!
> **Translation:** The Gemara raises an additional objection to the derivation from a nazirite’s sin offering: **And furthermore, can** the *halakhot* of a standard sin offering and of a nazirite’s sin offering **be derived from one another?** The *halakhot* of **a sin offering** brought for a transgression, e.g., eating forbidden **fat, cannot be derived from** the *halakhot* of **a nazirite’s sin offering,** due to the unique element of stringency of the latter, **since there is other blood,** i.e., other offerings are brought, **with it.** Likewise, the *halakhot* of **a nazirite’s sin offering cannot be derived from** the *halakhot* of **a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat,** due to the unique element of stringency of the latter, **since** it atones for a transgression punishable by **excision from the World-to-Come [*karet*].**

At this point the *gemara* notes that Ravina challenged specifically the *vav* of *olaso* — the *olah* of the *nazir* — asking: if you are going to use the *vav* in *chatato* to teach *me'akev*, what will you do with the extra *vav* in *olaso*, since the *me'akev* requirement does not apply to an *olah* the way it does to a *chatas*? Why did Ravina not similarly challenge the extra *vavs* found in *minchaso* and *nisko* — his *minchah* and wine libation?

The *gemara* explains that those *vavs* are not problematic because they are needed for a separate *drasha*. The *pasuk* by *Sukkos* teaches, from the possessive form of *minchasam veniskeihem*, that the *minchah* and *nesachim* accompanying a *korban* may be brought even at night or even on the following day. Applying the same reading to the *korban* of the *nazir*, *minchaso venisko* teaches that a *nazir*'s *minchah* and *nesachim* may similarly be brought on a subsequent night or day. Since those *vavs* serve that separate purpose, Ravina's challenge was rightly directed only at *olaso*. And the challenge about *olaso* — paired with the structural objection that one *chatas* cannot be learned from another — leaves the derivation incomplete.

### Rava's Solution

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא אָמַר רָבָא: אָתְיָא מֵחַטַּאת מְצוֹרָע, דִּכְתִיב: ״וְעָשָׂה הַכֹּהֵן אֶת הַחַטָּאת״ – שֶׁיְּהוּ כׇּל עֲשִׂיּוֹתָיו לְשֵׁם חַטָּאת.
> **Translation:** **Rather, Rava says:** These *halakhot* are **derived from** the *halakha* of **the sin offering of a leper; as it is written: “And the priest shall offer [*ve’asa*] the sin offering”** (Leviticus 14:19), teaching **that all** sacrificial **actions of [*asiyyotav*]** a sin offering **must be** performed **for the sake of a sin offering.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַשְׁכְּחַן שִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ, שִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים מְנָלֵיהּ? אָמַר קְרָא: ״וְכִפֶּר עַל הַמִּטַּהֵר וְגוֹ׳״ – עַל הַמִּטַּהֵר זֶה, וְלֹא עַל הַמִּטַּהֵר חֲבֵירוֹ.
> **Translation:** **And we found** a source for the *halakha* that a sin offering must be sacrificed without **deviation** from the type of **offering,** and that this is indispensable. **From where do we** derive that sacrificing a sin offering without **deviation** with regard to the **owner** is also indispensable? **The verse states: “And make atonement for the one that is to be purified** of his impurity” (Leviticus 14:19), indicating that the sin offering must be sacrificed **for** the sake of **this** person **who is being purified, and not for another** person **who is to be purified.**

Rava proposes an alternative: *atya mechatas metzora* — learn not from *chatas nazir* but from the *chatas* of a *metzora*. The relevant *pasuk* states *ve'asah hakohen et hachatas*, "and the *kohen* shall perform the *chatas*." Once again the word *ve'asah* is generic and encompasses all the *avodos*, yielding the principle *sheyehu kol asiyosav leshem chatas* — all acts of the *avodah* must be for the sake of the *chatas*. And for *shinui ba'alim*, the *pasuk* adds *v'chiper al hamitaher* — he shall atone for the one becoming purified — from which one derives *al hamitaher zeh v'lo al mitaher chaveiro*: for this person and not for another's *metzora*. Combined with the *pesukim* already known by *chatas chelev*, the doubling would then signal *me'akev* status.

### Metzora Distinct

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַכַּתִּי, מִי יָלְפָא מֵהֲדָדֵי?! חַטַּאת חֵלֶב מֵחַטַּאת מְצוֹרָע לָא יָלְפָא – שֶׁכֵּן יֵשׁ עִמָּהּ דָּמִים אֲחֵרִים; חַטָּאת מְצוֹרָע מֵחַטַּאת חֵלֶב לָא גָּמְרָה – שֶׁכֵּן כָּרֵת!
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But still, can** the *halakhot* of a standard sin offering and a leper’s sin offering **be derived from one another?** This comparison is also problematic. The *halakhot* of **a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat cannot be derived from** the *halakhot* of **a leper’s sin offering, since there is other blood** that is brought **with** the latter, i.e., a burnt offering and a guilt offering; and the *halakhot* of **a leper’s sin offering cannot be derived from** the *halakhot* of **a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat, since** the latter atones for a transgression punishable by ***karet*.**

The *gemara* immediately raises the same objection against Rava's solution. *Chatas chelev mechatas metzora lo yalfei, sheken yesh imom damim acherim* — a *chatas metzora* comes alongside an *olah* and an *asham* and other *korbanos*, so its demands may be heavier and cannot be assumed to apply to a standalone *chatas chelev*. And in the reverse, *chatas metzora mechatas chelev lo gamar, sheken karet* — the demands of a *chatas* brought for a *karet* offense may be more stringent than those applicable to a *metzora*. Once again, the two *chatasos* are too distinct to allow learning one from the other.

## Tzad HaShaveh



### Learn One from Two

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מֵחֲדָא לָא אָתְיָא, תֵּיתֵי חֲדָא מִתַּרְתֵּי.
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: Indeed, the *halakhot* of a sin offering **cannot be derived from** the *halakhot* of **one** other type of sin offering alone. But one can **derive** the *halakhot* of **one** type of sin offering **from** the *halakhot* of the other **two.** The verses concerning a standard sin offering, that of a leper, and that of a nazirite all contain elements that indicate that the offering must be sacrificed for its own sake. Two of them together are sufficient to teach that this *halakha* applies to the third case. The third case is therefore apparently extraneous, and it is understood to teach that proper intent in this regard is indispensable for all the offering’s rites.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בְּהֵי לָא לִכְתּוֹב? [לָא לִכְתּוֹב] רַחֲמָנָא בְּחַטַּאת חֵלֶב, וְתֵיתֵי מֵהָנָךְ?! מָה לְהָנָךְ, שֶׁכֵּן יֵשׁ עִמָּהֶם דָּמִים אֲחֵרִים!
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **With regard to which** type of sin offering **should** the Torah **not have written** this *halakha*, i.e., which of the three cases should be regarded as the extraneous case? If you say: **Let the Merciful One not write** it **with regard to a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat, and** one can **derive** it **from these** other sin offerings, i.e., those of a nazirite and a leper, this can be refuted: **What** is notable **about these** other sin offerings? They are notable **in that there is other blood** brought **with them.** Therefore, the *halakha* should apply only to similar sin offerings.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לָא לִכְתּוֹב רַחֲמָנָא בְּחַטַּאת נָזִיר, וְתֵיתֵי מֵהָנָךְ?! מָה לְהָנָךְ, שֶׁכֵּן אֵין לָהֶן שְׁאֵלָה!
> **Translation:** If you say: **Let the Merciful One not write** it **with regard to a nazirite’s sin offering, and** one can **derive** it **from these** other sin offerings, those of a leper and a sinner, this can also be refuted: **What** is notable **about these** other sin offerings? They are notable **in that** one **does not have** the option of obtaining an exemption through **a request** to a halakhic authority. A nazirite, by contrast, can request of a halakhic authority to dissolve his vow of naziriteship, thereby nullifying his obligation.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לָא לִכְתּוֹב רַחֲמָנָא בְּחַטַּאת מְצוֹרָע, וְתֵיתֵי מֵהָנָךְ?! מָה לְהָנָךְ, שֶׁכֵּן אֵין בָּאִין בְּדַלּוּת!
> **Translation:** If you say: **Let the Merciful One not write** it **with regard to a leper’s sin offering, and** one can **derive** it **from these** other sin offerings, those of a nazirite and a sinner, this can also be refuted: **What** is notable **about these** other sin offerings? They are notable **in that they cannot be brought** from doves instead of sheep if the owner is **poor,** unlike a leper’s sin offering (see Leviticus 14:21). Therefore, the *halakhot* of these different sin offerings cannot be derived from one another.


*Chada mechada lo asya* — one cannot be learned from the other alone, since each has a distinctive feature that might account for its requirements. But the *gemara* proposes a way forward: *teisi chada mitartei* — why not learn one from two? We now have three sets of *pesukim* requiring *lishmah* by *chatas*: by *chatas chelev*, by *chatas nazir*, and by *chatas metzora*. If any two of them together would suffice to establish the requirement in the third, then the *pesukim* by the third are superfluous — and that surplus teaches not merely *lechatchila* but *me'akev*.

The *gemara* tests each possible configuration. First: why not omit the *lishmah* requirement from *chatas chelev* and learn it from *chatas nazir* and *chatas metzora*? No — *mah lehanach sheken yesh imom damim acherim*, both of those come in a cluster of *korbanos*, and their combined demands might not transfer to a standalone *chatas chelev*. Second: why not omit it from *chatas nazir* and learn from *chatas chelev* and *chatas metzora*? No — *mah lehanach sheken ein lahem she'eilah*, a *nazir* can in principle approach a *chacham* to have the *nezirus* annulled, so the demands applying to the irreversible *chatas chelev* and *chatas metzora* might not apply to *chatas nazir*. Third: why not omit it from *chatas metzora* and learn from *chatas chelev* and *chatas nazir*? No — *mah lehanach sheken ein bam bedalut*, those two have no *oleh v'yored* option for the poor, while *chatas metzora* does allow a lesser offering for someone impoverished; perhaps that leniency means *chatas metzora* carries lighter requirements.

In every configuration, the third *chatas* cannot be learned from the other two, and therefore every one of the three needed its own *pesukim* to establish the *lishmah* requirement independently. This means none of the *pesukim* are superfluous, and therefore none of them can be used to teach *me'akev*.

### Shelamim Connection

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא אָמַר קְרָא: ״זֹאת הַתּוֹרָה לָעֹלָה וְלַמִּנְחָה וְגוֹ׳״ – הִקִּישׁוֹ הַכָּתוּב לִשְׁלָמִים; מָה שְׁלָמִים – בֵּין שִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ בֵּין שִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים, בָּעֵינַן לִשְׁמָן לְמִצְוָה; אַף חַטָּאת – בֵּין שִׁנּוּי קֹדֶשׁ בֵּין שִׁנּוּי בְּעָלִים, בָּעֵינַן לִשְׁמָן לְמִצְוָה.
> **Translation:** **Rather,** the *halakha* is derived from that which **the verse states: “This is the law of the burnt offering, and of the meal offering,** and of the sin offering, and of the guilt offering, and of the consecration offering, and of the sacrifice of peace offerings” (Leviticus 7:37). **The verse juxtaposes** a sin offering **with a peace offering,** indicating that **just as** for **a peace offering we require** intent **for its sake, as a mitzva** *ab initio*, **both** concerning the **deviation** from the type of **offering and** concerning **deviation** with regard to the **owner, so too,** for **a sin offering we require** intent **for its sake as a mitzva, both** concerning **deviation** from the type of **offering and** concerning **deviation** with regard to the **owner.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** הִילְכָּךְ, מִצְוָה – מִשְּׁלָמִים, וְהָנָךְ קְרָאֵי – לְעַכֵּב.
> **Translation:** **Therefore,** the **mitzva** *ab initio* is derived **from** the *halakha* of **a peace offering; and those verses** mentioned earlier, from which this requirement is derived with regard to each type of sin offering separately, repeat the *halakha* **to** teach that it is **indispensable.**

The *gemara* resolves the impasse by returning to a *pasuk* cited earlier: *zos hatorah la'olah u'laminchah v'lachatat v'la'asham v'lamiluim u'lizevach hashelamim* — this is the teaching for the *olah*, the *minchah*, the *chatas*, the *asham*, the *miluim*, and the *zevach hashelamim*. *Hikishu hakasuv leshelamim* — the verse compares *chatas* to *shelamim*. Just as *shelamim* requires *lishmah* and *l'shem ba'alim* at least *lechatchila* for both *shinui kodesh* and *shinui ba'alim* — as established in earlier discussions — the same applies to *chatas*. This *hekesh* to *shelamim* is the foundation that all *korbanos*, including *chatas*, must be performed *lishmah* and *l'shem ba'alim* at least *lechatchila*. That is our starting point.

### Extra Pesukim Teach

Once that baseline is established through the connection to *shelamim*, the individual *pesukim* by each *chatas* category — *chatas chelev*, *chatas nazir*, and *chatas metzora* — are no longer needed to teach the *lechatchila* requirement, since that is already known. Each of those sets of *pesukim* is now available to teach something additional: *hanach kra'ei l'akeiv* — those verses come to teach that the requirement is *me'akev*. If you fail to perform the *avodah* *lishmah* or *l'shem ba'alim* in any of these *chatas* categories, the *korban* is *pasul*.

### All Five Categories

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַשְׁכְּחַן חַטַּאת חֵלֶב, דִּכְתִיב בָּהּ ״לַחַטָּאת״;
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And we found** a source for these *halakhot* with regard to **a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat, as it is written with regard to it:** “And slaughter it **for a sin offering”** (Leviticus 4:33), indicating that it must be slaughtered for the sake of a sin offering. Likewise, sources for this *halakha* with regard to the sin offerings of a nazirite and a leper have also been cited.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חַטָּאת דַּעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה, דִּשְׁמִיעַת הַקּוֹל, וּבִטּוּי שְׂפָתַיִם, וְטוּמְאַת מִקְדָּשׁ וְקָדָשָׁיו – דְּלָא כְּתִיב בְּהוּ; מְנָלַן?
> **Translation:** **From where do we** derive these *halakhot* with regard to **a sin offering** brought **for idol worship,** which has unique *halakhot*, as detailed in Numbers 15:27–31, and with regard to which the term: For a sin offering, is not written? Likewise, from where do we derive them with regard to the sin offerings that are brought **for** the **hearing** of **the voice, and** for **an utterance of the lips, and** for the **defiling of** the **Temple or its sacrificial** foods, which also have unique *halakhot* (Leviticus 5:1–13), and **with regard to which** the term: For a sin offering, **is not written** either?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חַטָּאת דַּעֲבוֹדָה זָרָה – אָתְיָא מֵחַטַּאת חֵלֶב, שֶׁכֵּן כָּרֵת כְּמוֹתָהּ; וְהָנָךְ כּוּלְּהִי – אָתְיָין בְּמָה הַצַּד.
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: With regard to **a sin offering** brought **for idol worship,** these *halakhot* are **derived from** the *halakhot* of **a sin offering** brought for eating forbidden **fat, as** idol worship is also punishable by ***karet*, similar to** consumption of forbidden fat. **And** with regard to **all these** other sin offerings, which are brought for transgressions not punishable by *karet*, these *halakhot* **are derived by** analogy from **the common element** of the aforementioned three sources, namely, a standard sin offering, the sin offering of a nazirite, and the sin offering of a leper.

The *gemara* now traces how all five categories of *chatas* are covered. *Chatas chelev* — the standard sin offering — has its own *pesukim* stating *l'chatat*, which now teach *me'akev*. *Chatas nazir* and *chatas metzora* similarly have their own *pesukim* teaching *me'akev*. *Chatas avodas kochavim* — the *chatas* brought for idolatry — can be learned directly from *chatas chelev*, since both involve a *karet*-level transgression; if it is *me'akev* by *chatas chelev*, it is likewise *me'akev* for the *chatas* of *avodas kochavim*.

The remaining category — the *chatas oleh v'yored* found at the end of *Parshas Vayikra*, covering cases such as a false oath, failure to testify, or *tumah* in the *Beis HaMikdash* — does not have its own explicit *pesukim* establishing *me'akev*. Here the *gemara* applies a *tzad hashaveh*: since across all the other *chatas* categories — *chatas chelev*, *chatas nazir*, *chatas metzora*, and *chatas avodas kochavim* — *lishmah* is *me'akev*, one can infer by logical equivalence that the same applies to the *chatas oleh v'yored* as well. *Kulhu asyan b'ma hatzad* — all of them together support the inference.

There is also an underlying logic to why *me'akev* applies specifically to *chatas*: the *chatas* is inherently bound to its owner and their particular sin. The mechanism of *lo olah l'ba'alim l'shem chovah* — which ordinarily allows a *korban* performed *shelo lishmah* to be offered even if it doesn't count toward the owner's obligation — has no meaningful application to a *chatas*, since a *chatas* disconnected from its owner and their transgression loses its essential character entirely. This is also the explanation behind the category of *chatasos meisos* — *chatasos* that must be left to die once atonement has been achieved through other means — and it explains why a *chatas* performed *shelo lishmah* is not merely unhelpful but fully *pasul*.

## Pesach Overview



### Pesach Mishnah

The *gemara* now turns to the second exception identified in the *mishnah*: the *pesach* offering. A *baraisa* is cited: *tanu rabbanan*, *pesach bizmano lishmo kasher shelo lishmo pasul* — the *pesach* slaughtered at its proper time, from *chatzos* on the fourteenth of *Nissan*, if done *lishmo* is *kasher*, and if done *shelo lishmo* it is *pasul*.

This parallels *chatas*: when the *pesach* is performed at the correct time but with the wrong intent — for a different *korban* — it is entirely invalid. The *Torah* repeats the requirement multiple times that the *korban pesach* must be slaughtered on the fourteenth after *chatzos*; taking something sanctified as a *korban pesach* and slaughtering it as a *korban pesach* at the wrong time is therefore simply invalid. But when it is the right time and the animal is slaughtered *shelo lishmo*, that ruling is established — the question the *gemara* will now focus on is the source that makes *shelo lishmah* by *pesach* at its proper time *pasul* in the same way it is for a *chatas*.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: פֶּסַח בִּזְמַנּוֹ – לִשְׁמוֹ כָּשֵׁר, שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ פָּסוּל; וּבִשְׁאָר יְמוֹת הַשָּׁנָה – לִשְׁמוֹ פָּסוּל, שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ כָּשֵׁר.
> **Translation:** § **The Sages taught:** When **a Paschal offering** is sacrificed **at its** appointed **time,** on Passover eve after noon, if it is sacrificed **for its sake,** it is **fit;** if it is sacrificed **not for its sake,** it is **unfit,** as stated in the mishna. **And** when an offering consecrated as a Paschal offering is sacrificed **during the rest of the days of the year,** if it is sacrificed **for its sake,** it is **unfit;** if it is sacrificed **not for its sake,** it is **fit.**

### Pesach Becomes Shelamim


But the *mishnah* introduces a further ruling that applies when it is *not* the proper time — any time other than the fourteenth from *chatzos* onward. *Uv'she'ar yemos hashanah lishmo pasul shelo lishmo kasher* — during the rest of the year, the reverse holds. If it is slaughtered as a *pesach*, it is *pasul*, since the *Torah* repeats multiple times that the *pesach* must be slaughtered on the fourteenth after *chatzos*, and bringing it as a *pesach* at the wrong time is simply invalid. But if it is slaughtered for something other than *pesach* — thereby *oker hashem*, uprooting its *pesach* status — then it is *kasher*, and it becomes a *shelamim*.

## Pesach to Shelamim



### V'Im Pasuk

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** (של״ו קבאיצ״ן ממה״ר בצ״א ב״א סִימָן)
> **Translation:** The Gemara presents **a mnemonic** for the names of the *amora’im* mentioned in this discussion: ***Shin*, *lamed*, *vav*; *kuf*, *beit*, *alef*, *yod*, *tzadi*, *nun*; *mem*, *mem*, *heh*, *reish*; *beit*, *tzadi*, *alef*; *beit*, *alef*.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מְנָא הָנֵי מִילֵּי? אָמַר אֲבוּהּ דִּשְׁמוּאֵל, אָמַר קְרָא: ״וְאִם מִן הַצֹּאן קׇרְבָּנוֹ לְזֶבַח שְׁלָמִים לַה׳״ – דָּבָר הַבָּא מִן הַצֹּאן יְהֵא לְזֶבַח שְׁלָמִים.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **From where are these matters** derived, i.e., that if a Paschal offering is sacrificed during the rest of the year it is fit if sacrificed not for its sake? **Shmuel’s father says: The verse states: “And if his offering for a sacrifice of peace offerings unto the Lord is of the flock”** (Leviticus 3:6), indicating that an offering **that is brought** only **from the flock will be a sacrifice of peace offerings.** The Paschal offering fulfills this criterion.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֵימָא שְׁלָמִים אִין, מִידֵּי אַחֲרִינָא לָא! אָמַר רַבִּי אִילָא אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן: ״לְזֶבַח״ – לְרַבּוֹת כׇּל זֶבַח.
> **Translation:** The Gemara challenges: **Say** that if it is sacrificed for the sake of **a peace offering, yes,** it is fit, but if it is sacrificed for the sake of **some other** offering, it is **not** fit. **Rabbi Ila says** that **Rabbi Yoḥanan says:** The redundant expression **“for a sacrifice”** in Leviticus 3:6 is stated **to include any** type of **sacrifice.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֵימָא כֹּל דְּשָׁחֵיט – לֶהֱוֵי כְּמוֹתָהּ!
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: If so, **say** that a Paschal offering **should be** rendered **as any** offering for **which** one **slaughters** it; for example, if it is slaughtered for the sake of a burnt offering, it should be rendered a burnt offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אִי הֲוָה כְּתִיב ״לִשְׁלָמִים וְזֶבַח״ – כִּדְקָאָמְרַתְּ; הַשְׁתָּא דִּכְתִיב ״לְזֶבַח שְׁלָמִים״ – לְכֹל דְּשָׁחֵיט לֵיהּ, שְׁלָמִים לֶהֱוֵי.
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: **If** the phrase: **For peace offerings and a sacrifice, was written** in the verse, it would be **as you say. Now that it is written: “For a sacrifice of peace offerings,”** it indicates that **for whatever** type of offering one **slaughters it, it will be a peace offering.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֵימָא: ״לְזֶבַח״ – כָּלַל, ״שְׁלָמִים״ – פָּרַט; כְּלָל וּפְרָט – אֵין בַּכְּלָל אֶלָּא מַה שֶׁבַּפְּרָט; שְׁלָמִים אִין, מִידֵּי אַחֲרִינָא לָא!
> **Translation:** The Gemara challenges: **Say** that: **“For a sacrifice,”** is **a generalization** and: **“Of peace offerings,”** is **a detail,** and according to the principles of biblical hermeneutics, wherever there is **a generalization and a detail, the generalization includes only that which is** specified **in the detail.** Accordingly, if the offering is sacrificed for the sake of **a peace offering, yes,** it is fit; but if it is sacrificed for the sake of **another** offering, it is **not** fit.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״לַה׳״ – הָדַר וְכָלַל.
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: With the term: **“Unto the Lord,”** the verse **then generalized** again. Therefore, the verse is formulated as a generalization, a detail, and a generalization, as the Gemara will explain.

The *gemara* asks: *minalan hanei mili* — where do we learn that when a *pesach shelo bizmano* is slaughtered *shelo lishmo* it is *kasher*? The father of Shmuel answers that the *pasuk* states *v'im min hatzon korbano l'zevach shelamim la'Hashem*, "and if from the sheep is his offering as a *zevach shelamim* to God." This appears at the beginning of *Vayikra* in the context of *shelamim* offerings. The *drasha* proceeds: *davar haba min hatzon* — a type of *korban* that comes exclusively from sheep — this is the *pesach*, which is restricted to *tzon* (sheep or goats). *Yehei l'zevach shelamim* — such a *korban* brought outside its proper time shall be a *shelamim*. Since a *pesach* cannot be a *shelamim* on the fourteenth of *Nissan* after *chatzos*, the verse must refer to a *pesach* brought at any other time of year.

### Kavana Scope

One might object: perhaps the verse means only that if one explicitly had *kavana* for *shelamim*, it becomes a *shelamim*, but not if one had *kavana* for something else entirely. If one slaughtered the *pesach* with intent for an *olah* or a *chatas*, perhaps it should be *pasul*?

This question is not merely technical. The underlying mechanism would be: intent for something other than *pesach* uproots its *pesach* status, and because of its fundamental *kedusha* as something eaten by the owners in *Yerushalayim*, it gravitates toward becoming a *shelamim*. But that mechanism is not obvious; one might assume that only explicit *kavana* for *shelamim* could produce this result.

### Grammar L'Zevach

Rav Ila in the name of Rabbi Yochanan resolves this: *l'zevach l'rabot kol zavach* — the word *l'zevach* functions as a *ribuy*, an expansion, including any type of *zevach*. The full verse then reads: if it is a *korban* of sheep (*i.e.*, a *pesach*) — *l'zevach*, for any *zevach* — *shelamim*, it becomes a *shelamim*. Any form of *shelo lishmah* intent transforms the *pesach* into a *shelamim*.

However, the *gemara* presses further. If *l'zevach* is read as a *ribuy*, one might ask: why does it become specifically a *shelamim*? Why not become whatever *korban* the *shochet* had in mind — an *olah* if intent was for *olah*, a *chatas* if intent was for *chatas*? The *gemara* considers whether *l'zevach shelamim* should be read as *klal u'prat* — a general statement (*l'zevach*) followed by a specific (*shelamim*) — in which case the rule *ein baklal ella mah shebaprat* would restrict the result to *shelamim* only when one explicitly intended *shelamim*.

The *gemara* responds to this by pointing to the word *l'Hashem* later in the verse, which restores the broadening: the structure is now *l'zevach* — a *klal*, *shelamim* — a *prat*, *l'Hashem* — a second *klal*. This is a *klal u'prat v'klal*, in which the *prat* does not collapse the scope entirely but rather characterizes the kind of intent that matters. The *shelamim* in the middle tells you what result follows, but the surrounding *klalim* tell you that any intent — any *kavana* for a *zevach* brought to God — is sufficient to trigger that result. The grammar of the verse is doing two things simultaneously: *lezavach* and *l'Hashem* open up the scope of qualifying intent, while *shelamim* anchors the outcome. It is a genuinely elegant *drasha*.

### Klal U'Prat

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְקֵיף לַהּ רַב יַעֲקֹב מִנְּהַר פְּקוֹד: הָא לָא דָּמֵי כְּלָלָא בָּתְרָא לִכְלָלָא קַמָּא; כְּלָלָא קַמָּא – מְרַבֵּי זְבָחִים וְתוּ לָא; כְּלָלָא בָּתְרָא – ״לַה׳״ כֹּל דְּלַה׳, וַאֲפִילּוּ לְעוֹפוֹת וַאֲפִילּוּ לִמְנָחוֹת!
> **Translation:** **Rav Ya’akov of Nehar Pekod objects to this** suggestion: **But the latter generalization is dissimilar to the former generalization. The former generalization:** “For a sacrifice,” **includes** only **slaughtered offerings and nothing more,** whereas **the latter generalization: “Unto the Lord,”** includes **any** offering **unto the Lord. And** accordingly, **even** if the Paschal offering was sacrificed **for** the sake of **bird** offerings, **or even for** the sake of **meal offerings,** it should be fit, as these are brought “unto the Lord.”

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** הָא תַּנָּא דְּבֵי רַבִּי יִשְׁמָעֵאל, בִּכְלָלֵי וּפְרָטֵי דָּרֵישׁ כִּי הַאי גַוְונָא:
> **Translation:** The Gemara responds to this objection: **The school of Rabbi Yishmael taught generalizations and details** even in examples **like this case.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** כְּלָל וּפְרָט וּכְלָל – אִי אַתָּה דָן אֶלָּא כְּעֵין הַפְּרָט; מָה הַפְּרָט מְפוֹרָשׁ – שֶׁהוּא שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, וְכָשֵׁר; אַף כֹּל – שֶׁהוּא שֶׁלֹּא לִשְׁמוֹ, וְכָשֵׁר.
> **Translation:** The Gemara explains: Since “unto the Lord” is a second generalization, the verse is formulated as **a generalization, and a detail, and a generalization,** and **you may deduce** that the verse is referring **only** to items **similar to the detail. Just as** the items mentioned in **the detail** are clearly **defined as** a case where a Paschal offering was sacrificed **not for its own sake, and** nevertheless it is **fit, so too,** in **any** case **where it** was sacrificed **not for its sake but** for the sake of another offering, it is **fit.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אִי – מָה הַפְּרָט מְפוֹרָשׁ, דָּבָר הַבָּא בְּנֶדֶר וּבִנְדָבָה; אַף כׇּל הַבָּא בְּנֶדֶר וּבִנְדָבָה – עוֹלָה וּשְׁלָמִים אִין, חַטָּאת וְאָשָׁם לֹא!
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **If** the verse is referring only to actions similar to the detail, then **just as** the items mentioned in **the detail** are clearly **defined** as a peace offering, which is **a matter that is brought as a vow** offering **or as a gift** offering, **so too,** a Paschal offering should be fit only if it was sacrificed for the sake of **any** offering **that is brought as a vow** offering **or as a gift** offering. Accordingly, if it was sacrificed for the sake of **a burnt offering or a peace offering, yes,** it is fit, as these are brought as a vow offering or as a gift offering; but if it was sacrificed for the sake of **a sin offering or a guilt offering,** it is **not** fit, as these are not brought as a vow offering or as a gift offering.


Rav Yaakov miNehar Pekod challenges the *klal u'prat v'klal* reading on its own terms. A valid *klal u'prat v'klal* requires that the two *klalim* be of comparable scope, but here they are not. The first *klal*, *l'zevach*, encompasses only *zvachim* — slaughtered mammals. The second *klal*, *l'Hashem*, is far broader, potentially including *olos ha'of*, *menachos*, and any form of *korban* brought to God. If the two *klalim* are of unequal scope, the *klal u'prat v'klal* analysis does not function properly.

The *gemara* responds that this *tanna* follows the school of Rabbi Yishmael, which takes a looser approach to *klal u'prat* and allows the inference even when the *klalim* are not precisely matched.

### Ribuy Resolution

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא ״לְזֶבַח״ – רִבּוּיָא הוּא.
> **Translation:** The Gemara therefore rejects the exegetical method of generalizations and details in favor of that of amplifications and restrictions: **Rather,** the term: **“For a sacrifice,” is** not a generalization, but **an amplification,** which includes any type of offering. Therefore, if a Paschal offering is sacrificed for the sake of any type of offering during the rest of the year, it is fit.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֵימָא לְכֹל דְּשָׁחֵיט לֵיהּ – לֶהֱוֵי כְּוָותֵיהּ!
> **Translation:** The Gemara challenges: If so, **say** that it adds that a Paschal offering **should be** rendered **as any** offering for **which** one **slaughters** it. Why does Shmuel’s father interpret that it is rendered a peace offering specifically?

Ultimately, the *gemara* sets aside the *klal u'prat v'klal* analysis and returns to the reading of *l'zevach* as a *ribuy*. As Rashi explains, whether a term functions as a *klal* or as a *ribuy* depends on the linguistic context and one's hermeneutical methodology. Here, reading *l'zevach* as a *ribuy* — as simply expanding the scope of which *zevach* intent suffices — the verse teaches that any *zevach* intent transforms the *pesach*. And *shelamim* specifies what it becomes.

The *gemara* asks one final question on this reading: if *l'zevach* is a *ribuy* and no longer part of a structured *klal u'prat v'klal* sentence, how do we still know that the result is *shelamim* rather than whatever *korban* the *shochet* intended? The answer to this question is provided in the next section.

## Why Shelamim



### Kodshim Kalim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי אָבִין:
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Avin says:**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִים אֵצֶל קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִין, וְאֵין דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִין אֵצֶל קָדָשִׁים שֶׁאֵינָן נֶאֱכָלִין.
> **Translation:** **Offerings that are eaten,** in this case the Paschal offering, **are diverted** when slaughtered not for their sake **to** serve as other types of **offerings that are eaten,** such as peace offerings. **But offerings that are eaten are not diverted to** serve as **offerings that are not eaten,** such as burnt offerings.

Rav Avin supplies the principle: *dochin kodshim ne'echalim etzel kodshim ne'echalim v'ein dochin kodshim ne'echalim etzel kodshim she'einam ne'echalim* — *kodshim* that are eaten are pushed toward other *kodshim* that are eaten, and they are not pushed toward *kodshim* that are not eaten. A *pesach* is eaten by its owners; an *olah* is entirely consumed on the *mizbeach* and not eaten by people. Therefore, even if one had *kavana* for an *olah*, the *pesach* will not become an *olah* — it will gravitate toward something that is eaten, namely *shelamim*.

Rabbi Yose berebi Avin states the principle more precisely: *dochin kodshim kalim etzel kodshim kalim v'ein dochin kodshim kalim etzel kodshei kodshim* — items of *kodshim kalim* status (eaten throughout *Yerushalayim*) are pushed toward other *kodshim kalim*, and not toward *kodshei kodshim*. A *pesach* is *kodshim kalim*, and *shelamim* is *kodshim kalim*, and therefore the *pesach* becomes a *shelamim*.

### Maaser Objection


Rabbi Yitzchak berebi Svara raises an objection: *eima shachatei l'sheim Maaser l'havei Maaser* — why not say that if one slaughtered the *pesach* with intent for *maaser*, it should become *maaser*? *Maaser* is also *kodshim kalim*, eaten by the owners in *Yerushalayim*. And the distinction matters practically: if treated as *maaser*, it would not require *nesachim* — the accompanying wine libations — and one would receive *malkus* for selling it, given the specific *lav* against redeeming *maaser* improperly.

### Ha'Asiri Answer

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אַטּוּ חַטָּאת וְאָשָׁם מִי לָא מִיתְאַכְלִי?!
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **Is that to say** that **a sin offering and a guilt offering are not eaten?** Why does a Paschal offering receive the status of a peace offering and not that of a sin offering or a guilt offering, which are eaten by the priests?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֶלָּא דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִין לְכׇל אָדָם אֵצֶל קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִין לְכׇל אָדָם, וְאֵין דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים הַנֶּאֱכָלִין לְכׇל אָדָם אֵצֶל קָדָשִׁים שֶׁאֵין נֶאֱכָלִין לְכׇל אָדָם.
> **Translation:** **Rather,** the answer should be emended: **Offerings that are eaten by every** Jewish **person are diverted to** serve as **offerings eaten by every** Jewish **person,** i.e., peace offerings. **But offerings eaten by every** Jewish **person are not diverted to** serve as **offerings not eaten by every** Jewish **person,** i.e., sin offerings and guilt offerings, which are eaten only by priests.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבִּי יוֹסֵי בְּרַבִּי אָבִין אָמַר: דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים קַלִּים אֵצֶל קָדָשִׁים קַלִּים, וְאֵין דּוֹחִין קָדָשִׁים קַלִּים אֵצֶל קׇדְשֵׁי קָדָשִׁים.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Yosei, son of Rabbi Avin, says** that there is a different answer: **Offerings of lesser sanctity are diverted to** serve as **offerings of lesser sanctity,** i.e., peace offerings. **But offerings of lesser sanctity are not diverted to** serve as **offerings of the most sacred order,** i.e., burnt offerings, sin offerings, or guilt offerings.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְקֵיף לַהּ רַב יִצְחָק בְּרַבִּי (סַבְרִיוּ) [סַבְרִין], אֵימָא: שַׁחְטֵיהּ לְשֵׁם מַעֲשֵׂר – לֶיהֱוֵי מַעֲשֵׂר! לְמַאי הִילְכְתָא? דְּלָא לִיבְעֵי נְסָכִים, וּלְמִלְקֵא עֲלֵיהּ (בְּלֹא יִמָּכֵר) בְּ״לֹא יִגָּאֵל״.
> **Translation:** **Rav Yitzḥak, son of Rabbi Savriyu, objects to this** answer: If so, **say** that if one **slaughtered** a Paschal offering **for the sake of** the animal **tithe, let it be** rendered animal **tithe,** which, like a Paschal offering, is an offering of lesser sanctity. The Gemara adds: **For what *halakha*** does it matter, practically speaking, that a Paschal offering has been rendered an animal tithe rather than a peace offering? It matters **because** if it is rendered an animal tithe, **it should not require libations. And** furthermore, if one sells it, he **should be flogged for** transgressing the prohibition: **“It shall not be redeemed”** (Leviticus 27:33), which applies to the animal tithe even after its slaughter.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר קְרָא: ״הָעֲשִׂירִי יִהְיֶה קֹדֶשׁ״ – זֶה מַעֲשֵׂר, וְאֵין אַחֵר מַעֲשֵׂר.
> **Translation:** The Gemara responds: **The verse states** with regard to the animal tithe: **“The tenth shall be sacred”** (Leviticus 27:32). The definite article indicates that **this,** the tenth animal, is the **tithe, but another** animal slaughtered for its sake is **not a tithe.**

The *gemara* answers by citing the *pasuk* *ha'asiri yihiyeh kodesh* — the tenth shall be holy. The *drasha* is: *zeh Maaser v'ein acher Maaser* — only the item that is literally the tenth in a sequence acquires *maaser* status. Something like a *korban pesach*, which does not emerge from a tithing process, cannot spontaneously become *maaser* regardless of the *kavana* at the time of *shechitah*. Therefore, the only available destination for the displaced *pesach* is *shelamim*.
