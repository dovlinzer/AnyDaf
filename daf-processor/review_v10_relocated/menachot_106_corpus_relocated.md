## Unspecified Mincha



### Mishnah Setup

Today's *daf* is *Menachot* 106. We pick up on 106a about halfway down the page, at the words *perashti minchah shel esronim* — "I designated a *minchah* of *esronim*." The case is one where a person vowed to bring a *minchah* of *esronim* but cannot remember how many *esronim* he specified. Because he said a *minchah* — singular — of *esronim*, the assumption is that it must be one *minchah* of X *esronim*. The *mishna* resolves the uncertainty by bringing sixty, since that is the maximum number of *esronim* a single *minchah* can contain. According to the *Chachamim*, he stipulates that whatever amount satisfies his *neder* is his *neder*, and the remainder is a *nedavah* — a free-will offering. According to *Rebbi*, however, the amount must be exact: since he said a specific number, he must bring every possible amount from one to sixty, each in its own vessel, covering all possibilities.

### Tanu Rabbanan

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי מִנְחָה, וְקָבַעְתִּי בִּכְלִי אֶחָד שֶׁל עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: יָבִיא מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל עֶשְׂרוֹנִים מֵאֶחָד וְעַד שִׁשִּׁים, שֶׁהֵן אֶלֶף וּשְׁמוֹנֶה מֵאוֹת וּשְׁלֹשִׁים.
> **Translation:** **The Sages taught** in a *baraita*: If one says: **I specified** that I would bring **a meal offering, and I established** that they must be brought **in** one **vessel of tenths** of an ephah, **but I do not know what** number of tenths **I specified, he must bring** one **meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah. This is **the statement of the Rabbis. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He must bring** sixty **meal offerings of tenths** in sixty vessels, each containing an amount **from one**-tenth **until sixty**-tenths, **which are** in total **1,830** tenths of an ephah.

The *Gemara* now cites a *baraita* that elaborates on the *mishna*: *Tanu Rabanan* — the Rabbis taught: *Perashti minchah v'kavati b'kli echad shel esronim v'eini yodeia mah perashti* — "I designated a *minchah* and specified it in one vessel of *esronim*, and I do not know how many *esronim* I specified." When the person says a *minchah* singular, it is either explicit or implicit that the entire amount is in one vessel — as opposed to the possibility discussed earlier of bringing a *minchah* across multiple vessels.

*Yavi minchah she-shishim esronim Divrei Chachamim* — according to the *Chachamim*, one brings sixty *esronim* in a single vessel and stipulates: whatever is needed for my *neder* is the *neder*, and the rest is a *nedavah*, an additional *minchah* brought as a free-will offering. *Rebbi omer yavi menachot shel esronim me-echad v'ad shishim* — *Rebbi* says one brings separate *menachot* of *esronim* from one through sixty. And if the person also does not know which of the five types of *minchah* he vowed, or how many *esronim* he intended — *v'eini yodeia mah perashti v'eizo mehem perashti v'eini yodeia kamah perashti* — the phrase *kamah perashti* addresses how many, *eizo mehem* addresses what type, and *mah perashti* encompasses the whole uncertainty — then *yavi chamesh menachot she-shishim esronim Divrei Chachamim*: the *Chachamim* require five sets of sixty *esronim*, one for each type of *minchah*, and *Rebbi omer yavi chamesh menachot she-shishim esronim me-echad v'ad shishim*: *Rebbi* requires bringing all five types, each multiplied across all amounts from one to sixty.

### Sum 1-60

This raises the arithmetic: what is the total number of *esronim* according to *Rebbi*? Adding all integers from one to sixty yields 1,830 — *she-hen elef u-shmoneh me'ot u-shloshim*. *Tosafot* provides an elegant method for this calculation: *keitzad kach b'yadcha me-echad v'ad shishim v'tzaref techilatan v'sofan ad ha-emtza k'gon echad v'shishim hen shishim v'echad* — take the numbers from one to sixty and pair each from the beginning with one from the end: one plus sixty equals sixty-one, two plus fifty-nine equals sixty-one, and so on, until the middle pair of thirty and thirty-one. That yields thirty pairs of sixty-one, and thirty times sixty-one is 1,830. This same method is attributed to the mathematician Carl Gauss, who reportedly discovered it as a young boy when a teacher assigned him to sum all integers from one to one hundred. Whatever the history, *Tosafot* records the identical insight centuries earlier.

### Rabbi's Count

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי, וְאִי זוֹ מֵהֶן פֵּירַשְׁתִּי, וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ כַּמָּה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא חָמֵשׁ מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים, שֶׁהֵן שְׁלֹשׁ מֵאוֹת, דִּבְרֵי חֲכָמִים. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: יָבִיא חָמֵשׁ מְנָחוֹת שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עֶשְׂרוֹנִים מֵאֶחָד וְעַד שִׁשִּׁים, שֶׁהֵן תִּשְׁעָה אֲלָפִים וּמֵאָה וַחֲמִשִּׁים.
> **Translation:** The *baraita* continues: If one said: **I specified** a certain type of meal offering with a certain number of tenths of an ephah to be brought in one vessel, **but I do not know what I specified, or which** type **of** meal offering **I specified, and I do not know how many** tenths of an ephah **I specified, he must bring** the **five** different types **of meal offerings,** and each one must contain **sixty-tenths** of an ephah, **which are** in total **three-hundred-**tenths of an ephah. This is **the statement of the Rabbis. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He must bring five** different types of **meal offerings,** and for each type he must bring **sixty** meal offerings, each with a different number of tenths, **from one until sixty, which are** in total **9,150** tenths of an ephah.


According to *Rebbi*, if the person also forgot the type of *minchah*, he must bring all five types, each covering amounts from one to sixty. The formula here follows from the phrase *eini yodeia mah perashti v'eizo mehem perashti v'eini yodeia kamah perashti* — I do not know what I specified, which type among them I specified, or how much I specified. It is worth pausing on the three-part structure: *kamah perashti* addresses the quantity, *eizo mehem* addresses the type, and *mah perashti* appears to function as the global statement — I simply do not know what I vowed — which subsumes the other two uncertainties. There is in fact a textual question about whether *mah perashti* is a genuine third term or a redundant framing, and some authorities attempt to remove it, but its presence in the text is what it is. The practical consequence, in any case, is that the five types of *minchah* each require their own full sequence from one to sixty *esronim* per *Rebbi*'s requirement that each amount occupy its own vessel. The total is therefore 1,830 multiplied by five: *she-hem tishat alafim u-me'ah va-chamishim* — nine thousand one hundred and fifty *esronim*.

### Core Debate

Now that the arithmetic is clear, the *Gemara* asks: *b'mai kamifligi* — what is the underlying dispute? Why can *Rebbi* not accept the *Chachamim*'s solution? If everything must be in one vessel, why not place sixty *esronim* in that vessel, stipulate that whatever amount corresponds to the *neder* is the *neder*, and treat the remainder as a *nedavah*? What precisely prevents mixing the obligatory portion with an additional free-will offering in the same vessel?

## Chullin in Azarah



### Chullin Entry

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בְּמַאי קָא מִיפַּלְגִי? אָמַר רַב חִסְדָּא: בְּמוּתָּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה קָא מִיפַּלְגִי, רַבִּי סָבַר: אָסוּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה, וְרַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: מוּתָּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **With regard to what** principle **do** the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi **disagree? Rav Ḥisda said: They disagree with regard to** whether it is **permitted to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard. Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that **it is prohibited to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard.** Therefore, it is necessary to bring sixty meal offerings of each type, as if one merely brings one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah in a single vessel, he may be in violation of this prohibition, as it is possible that he vowed to bring less than that amount, and the surplus amount is non-sacred. **And the Rabbis hold** that **it is permitted to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard.** Therefore, even if the meal offering is of a greater volume than necessary, it is inconsequential.

*Rav Chisda* proposes the following framework: *b'muttar lehachnis chullin la'azarah kamifligi* — the dispute concerns whether one is permitted to bring non-sanctified matter, *chullin*, into the Temple courtyard, the *azarah*. The logic, strange as it sounds, is that the extra flour beyond the amount of the *neder* would not be designated as a *nedavah* but would simply remain *chullin*. The question would then be whether a vessel can be brought into the *azarah* when part of its contents may be *chullin*.

### Rabbi Forbids

According to *Rav Chisda*'s initial framing, *Rebbi savar assur lehachnis chullin la'azarah* — *Rebbi* holds that it is forbidden to bring *chullin* into the *azarah*, and therefore each amount must be placed in its own separate vessel. In a separate vessel, the donor can designate the exact sanctified amount as the *neder* and anything additional as a *nedavah*, without the problem of *chullin* in the *azarah*.

### Chachamim Allow

By contrast, *v'rabbanan savri muttar lehachnis chullin la'azarah* — the *Chachamim* permit bringing *chullin* into the *azarah*, and therefore have no objection to a single vessel containing both the obligatory and the possibly non-sanctified portions. This position is admittedly difficult to understand: if some of the flour is *chullin*, a handful will need to be taken and the offering placed on the altar — it is hard to see why the *Gemara* would initially prefer this arrangement over simply designating the extra as a *nedavah*.

### Rava Corrects

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רָבָא אָמַר: דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא אָסוּר לְהַכְנִיס חוּלִּין לָעֲזָרָה, וְהָכָא בְּמוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה קָא מִיפַּלְגִי.
> **Translation:** **Rava said** that the dispute between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi is with regard to a different issue: **Everyone** agrees that **it is prohibited to bring non-sacred** items **into the** Temple **courtyard, and here they disagree with regard to** whether it is permitted **to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering.

*Rava* rejects this framework entirely: *dechulei alma assur lehachnis chullin la'azarah* — obviously everyone agrees that *chullin* may not be brought into the *azarah*. The initial framing was misguided; no one would wish to have *chullin* mixed with a *korban*. The true dispute is something else: *vehacha bemuttar le'arev chovah binedava kamifligi* — here they disagree about whether it is permitted to mix an obligatory offering, the *chovah*, with a free-will offering, a *nedavah*, in a single vessel. *Rabbanan savri muttar le'arev chovah binedava* — the *Chachamim* permit combining them. *Rebbi savar assur le'arev chovah binedava* — *Rebbi* forbids it. It is striking that the *Gemara* initially seemed more comfortable with the idea of *chullin* in the *azarah* than with mixing *chovah* and *nedavah*; *Rava* now corrects course and makes clear that the real issue is the latter.

## Kometz Mixture



### Two Kematzim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וְרַבִּי סָבַר: אָסוּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה.
> **Translation:** **The Rabbis hold** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and therefore one may bring sixty-tenths in one vessel and stipulate that the amount beyond his obligation will be a gift offering. **And Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that **it is prohibited to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and therefore one cannot make such a stipulation; every possible meal offering requires its own vessel. Concerning each vessel, he stipulates that if this one can fulfill his vow, it should count as fulfillment of his vow, and otherwise it should serve as a gift offering.


*Abaye* now raises a practical difficulty with the *Chachamim*'s position to *Rava*. If *chovah* and *nedavah* are mixed together in one vessel, *le'Rabbanan de'amri muttar le'arev chovah binedava*, then you have a problem: *ha ba'inan sheni kematzim* — you need two *kematzim*, one for the *neder* and one for the *nedavah*. You cannot know in advance whether the sixty *esronim* are entirely a *chovah* (if you vowed sixty) or partly a *chovah* and partly a *nedavah* (if you vowed less). The *Gemara* answers: *dekometz vehadar kometz* — fine, you take two *kematzim*, one after the other.

### Mixed Kematzim

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ אַבָּיֵי לְרָבָא: לְרַבָּנַן דְּאָמְרִי מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, הָא בָּעֵינַן שְׁנֵי קְמָצִים? דְּקָמֵיץ וַהֲדַר קָמֵיץ.
> **Translation:** **Abaye said to Rava: According to the Rabbis, who say** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, and one vessel with sixty-tenths of an ephah can serve in part to fulfill the obligation and in part as a gift offering, there is a difficulty: **Don’t we require** the removal of **two handfuls,** one for the obligatory meal offering and one for the gift offering? Rava answered: According to the Rabbis, it is required **that** the priest **removes a handful and again removes a handful.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא קָא קָמֵיץ מֵחוֹבָה אַנְּדָבָה וּמִנְּדָבָה אַחוֹבָה!
> **Translation:** Abaye said to Rava: **But** if so, since the tenths that fulfill an obligation and the tenths that are a gift are mixed together in a single vessel, when the priest removes a handful, **isn’t he removing a handful from** tenths that fulfill **an obligation** to account **for** tenths that are **a gift** offering, **and** removing a handful **from** tenths that are **a gift** offering to account **for** tenths that fulfill **an obligation?**

But a further problem immediately arises: *veha kometz mechovah anedava uminedava achovah* — when you take each handful, it will contain a mixture drawn from both the *chovah* portion and the *nedavah* portion, since everything is in one vessel and the two are not physically separated. How then can the first *kematz* serve properly as the *kematz* of the *chovah* and the second as the *kematz* of the *nedavah*?

### Kohen's Intent

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** דְּתָלֵי לֵיהּ בְּדַעַת כֹּהֵן, דְּאָמַר: כֹּל הֵיכָא דְּמָטְיָא יְדָא דְּכֹהֵן הַשְׁתָּא – חוֹבָה, וּלְבַסּוֹף – נְדָבָה.
> **Translation:** Rava answered Abaye: This is not difficult, **as** the one who brings the meal offering **renders it dependent on the intent of the priest, as he says: Wherever the priest’s hand reaches now,** when he removes the first handful, shall be the location of the tenths that fulfill my **obligation, and** wherever his hand reaches **at the end,** when removing the second handful, shall be **a gift** offering.

The *Gemara* resolves this: *detali lei bedaat kohen* — the matter is made to depend on the intention of the *kohen*. The stipulation is formulated as follows: *kol heicha dematya yadeih dekohen hashta chovah* — wherever the *kohen*'s hand reaches now as he takes his first handful, that section of the flour is designated as the *chovah*. He is thus taking entirely from the *chovah* portion. Then, assuming there is a *nedavah* portion — meaning the person vowed less than sixty — the second section into which the *kohen* reaches is designated as the *nedavah*: *hashta chovah ulevasof nedavah*. The two *kematzim* are thereby kept distinct through designation, even though the physical flour is intermingled.

### No Nedava Problem

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאַקְטוֹרֵי הֵיכִי מַקְטַר? לַיקְטַר נְדָבָה בְּרֵישָׁא – דְּחוֹבָה הֵיכִי מַקְטַר לַהּ? דִּלְמָא כּוּלַּהּ חוֹבָה הִיא, וְחָסְרוּ לְהוּ שִׁירַיִם.
> **Translation:** Abaye asked Rava: **But how does** the priest **burn** the handful upon the altar? If the priest **will burn** the handful from the **gift** offering **first, how can he** then **burn** the handful **of the** tenths that fulfill the **obligation? Perhaps the entire** meal offering **is** for the **obligation,** as the vow was to bring sixty-tenths of an ephah, and everything apart from the first handful, including the second handful, is the remainder, **and** by burning part of it the priest causes **the remainder to be lacking.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאָמַר מָר: שִׁירַיִם שֶׁחָסְרוּ בֵּין קְמִיצָה לְהַקְטָרָה – אֵין מַקְטִיר קוֹמֶץ עֲלֵיהֶן!
> **Translation:** **And the Master said:** With regard to the **remainder** of a meal offering **that became lacking between the removal of the handful and the burning** of the handful upon the altar, the *halakha* is that **one does not burn the handful on its** account, as it is not considered a valid meal offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לַיקְטַר חוֹבָה בְּרֵישָׁא – דִּנְדָבָה הֵיכִי מַקְטַר לַהּ?
> **Translation:** Alternatively, if the priest **will burn** the handful that fulfills the **obligation first, how can he burn** the handful **of the gift** offering?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְדִלְמָא כּוּלַּהּ חוֹבָה הִיא, וְכֹל שֶׁהוּא מִמֶּנּוּ לָאִישִּׁים הֲרֵי הוּא בְּ״בַל תַּקְטִירוּ״.
> **Translation:** **But perhaps** the vow was to bring sixty-tenths of an ephah, in which case **the entire** meal offering **is** necessary to fulfill the **obligation.** Consequently, only one handful may be removed, and all the rest is considered the remainder. **And** there is a principle: **Whatever is partly** burned **in the fire** on the altar **is subject to** the prohibition of: **You may not make as an offering** (see Leviticus 2:11). This principle states that if part of an item, such as the blood of an animal offering or the handful of a meal offering, has been sacrificed, one who sacrifices any other part of it that is not designated for sacrifice has violated a prohibition. In this case, the second handful is considered part of the remainder of the meal offering, and burning it is prohibited.

However, a more serious difficulty now presents itself. If the person did in fact vow sixty *esronim*, then the entire sixty constitutes the *chovah* — there is no *nedavah* at all. Yet a second *kematz* will have been taken. The *Gemara* asks: *veidach heichi maktar* — how do you then burn this second *kematz*? The *Gemara* explores the possible order of operations. *Liktar nedavah bereisha* — should you burn the second *kematz* first, treating it as the *nedavah*? The *Gemara* objects: *dechovah heichi maktar lah* — how would you then subsequently burn the first *kematz* as the *chovah*? *Dilma kullah chovah hi* — perhaps the entire sixty is the *chovah*, in which case offering that first *kematz* would be burning the *kometz* after you have already removed and burned what might be the *shirayim* — the remnant. And the rule is: *shirayim shechasru bein kemitzah lehaktarah ein maktir kometz aleihem* — if the *shirayim* were diminished between the taking of the *kematz* and the burning of it, you cannot then burn the *kometz*.

The *Gemara* therefore reverses the order: *liktar chovah bereisha* — burn the first *kematz*, the one designated as the *chovah*, first. But then the difficulty shifts: *denedavah heichi maktir lei* — how do you subsequently burn the second *kematz*, the one designated as the *nedavah*? *Vedilma kullah chovah hi* — perhaps the entire sixty was the *chovah*, and there is no *nedavah* here at all. *Vechol shehu mimenu leishim harei hu beval taktir* — and the rule derived from the verse *ki kol seor vechol devash lo taktiru mimenu isheh l'Hashem* is that anything from which a portion has already been offered on the altar, its remainder may not itself be offered on the altar. If there is no *nedavah*, that second *kematz* consists entirely of *shirayim* of the *chovah*, and burning it on the altar violates this prohibition.

### Fuel Solution

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רַבִּי יְהוּדָה בְּרֵיהּ דְּרַבִּי שִׁמְעוֹן בֶּן פַּזִּי: דְּמַסֵּיק לְהוּ לְשׁוּם עֵצִים, וּכְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר. דְּתַנְיָא, רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר אוֹמֵר: לְרֵיחַ נִיחוֹחַ אִי אַתָּה מַעֲלֶה, אֲבָל אַתָּה מַעֲלֶה לְשׁוּם עֵצִים.
> **Translation:** **Rabbi Yehuda, son of Rabbi Shimon ben Pazi, said:** The priest does not burn the oil as an offering but **burns it for the sake of wood,** i.e., not as a sacrificial rite, **in accordance with** the opinion of **Rabbi Eliezer. As it is taught** in a *baraita* that **Rabbi Eliezer says:** The verse states: “No meal offering that you shall bring unto the Lord shall be made with leaven; for you shall make no leaven, nor any honey, smoke from it as an offering made by fire unto the Lord. As an offering of first fruits you may bring them unto the Lord; but they shall not come up for a pleasing aroma on the altar” (Leviticus 2:11–12). This verse indicates that **you may not offer up** leaven and honey **as a pleasing aroma,** i.e., as an offering. **But you may offer up** leaven and honey and other substances **for the sake of wood,** not as an offering.

The resolution is offered by *Rav Yehuda brei d'Rav Shimon ben Pazi*: *d'maseik lehu l'shum etzim* — you place the second *kematz* on the altar and treat it not as a ritual burning of an offering but simply as fuel, as firewood. The stipulation is: if a *nedavah* exists here, this *kematz* is its proper *kometz* and is being offered as such; but if the entire sixty was the *chovah* and there is no *nedavah*, then this flour — which should rightfully be eaten by the *kohanim* — is being placed on the altar fire as mere kindling, not as a sacrificial act. This is permitted, and does not violate the prohibition of *lo taktiru*, since it is not being offered *l'reach nichoach*, as an act of sacrifice. This solution invokes the principle of *Rabbi Eliezer*, as cited in a *baraita*: *D'tanya Rabbi Eliezer omer l'reach nichoach ee atah ma'aleh aval atah ma'aleh l'shum etzim* — *Rabbi Eliezer* says one may not offer it up as an act of *reach nichoach*, but one may place it on the fire as fuel. The verse *al hamizbeach lo ya'alu l'reach nichoach* prohibits only the former, not the latter. That is how the *Chachamim*'s position, permitting the mixture, is made technically workable. *Rebbi*, by contrast, simply forbids the mixture outright — though we have not yet fully explained the reason.

## Rav Acha's View



### Alternate Framework

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ רַב אַחָא בְּרֵיהּ דְּרָבָא לְרַב אָשֵׁי: וְדִלְמָא דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וְהָכָא בִּדְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר קָא מִיפַּלְגִי – דְּרַבָּנַן אִית לְהוּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר, וְרַבִּי לֵית לֵיהּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר?
> **Translation:** **Rav Aḥa, son of Rava, said to Rav Ashi: But perhaps everyone agrees** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering that fulfills **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and here** Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi and the Rabbis **disagree with regard to** the statement **of Rabbi Eliezer** that it is permitted to burn on the altar as fuel even items that are prohibited from being burned as offerings. **As the Rabbis accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer,** and therefore they allow one to bring one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah, since the handful removed from it may be burned, **and Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **does not accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer.**

*Rav Acha brei d'Rava* now proposes an alternative way of understanding the entire debate to *Rav Ashi*: *v'dilma d'kulei alma mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — perhaps everyone actually agrees that mixing *chovah* and *nedavah* is in principle permitted. *V'hacha b'd'Rabbi Eliezer kamiflagi* — the real disagreement is whether to accept *Rabbi Eliezer*'s fuel doctrine. *Derabbanan it lehu d'Rabbi Eliezer* — the *Chachamim* accept *Rabbi Eliezer*'s position, and therefore, even if the second *kematz* turns out to be *shirayim*, it can be placed on the altar as fuel. *V'Rebbi leit lei d'Rabbi Eliezer* — *Rebbi* rejects *Rabbi Eliezer*'s ruling, and therefore has no way of properly disposing of the second *kematz* if the entire sixty was the *chovah*, making the whole arrangement unworkable.

### Objection to Acha

The *Gemara* objects to this proposal: *ee salka dach'takh l'Rebbi mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — if *Rebbi* in principle permitted mixing *chovah* with *nedavah* and his only problem were the technical difficulty of the second *kematz*, then a technical solution could be found, and the objection based on *Rabbi Eliezer* alone would not explain why *Rebbi* requires sixty separate vessels.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אֲמַר לֵיהּ: אִי סָלְקָא דַעְתָּךְ לְרַבִּי מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וּדְרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר לֵית לֵיהּ, אֶפְשָׁר דְּמַיְיתֵי שִׁיתִּין בְּחַד מָנָא, וְחַד בְּחַד מָנָא, וּמַגַּע לְהוּ, וְקָמֵיץ.
> **Translation:** Rav Ashi **said to him:** This cannot be the dispute, as **if it enters your mind** that **according to Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **it is permitted to mix** an offering brought as **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and he does not accept** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer,** Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi would not require sixty separate vessels. Instead, **one could bring sixty-**tenths of an ephah **in one vessel, and one** additional tenth **in one** other **vessel,** from which the handful of the gift offering would be removed. **And** he could **touch them,** i.e., place them so they touch each other, so that they are considered as one, **and** the priest could then **remove a handful** from that tenth for the gift, and remove another handful from the vessel with sixty-tenths to fulfill the obligation.

### Touching Vessels


The technical solution is as follows: *efshar d'mayti shitin b'chad mana* — take sixty *esronim* in one vessel, and also take one additional *esaron* in a separate vessel, *v'chad b'chad mana u'maga lehu* — and bring the openings of the two vessels together so they are touching, with a continuous surface of flour between them. *U'maga lehu v'kometz* — and then take the *kematz*.

### Nedava Guarantee

Here is how this solution works. The first *kematz* is taken from the large vessel of sixty and designated as the *chovah*. Then the stipulation is made: if the vow was for sixty, the sixty constitutes the *chovah*, and the separate *esaron* in the second vessel is the *nedavah* — and you take the second *kematz* from that separate vessel. If the vow was for less than sixty, then everything beyond the vow in the large vessel, plus the entire second vessel, constitutes the *nedavah*, and you still take the second *kematz* from the second vessel. The problem that motivated *Rabbi Eliezer*'s ruling was that if the person vowed sixty, there would be zero *nedavah* remaining and nothing valid to take a *kometz* from. By ensuring a separate *esaron* is always present, there is always a guaranteed *nedavah* portion from which the *kometz* may be taken. This is an elegant solution, which means *Rebbi*'s objection cannot be merely technical — it must reflect a principled opposition to mixing *chovah* with *nedavah* in any form.

## Oil Proportion



### Rava's Answer

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רָבָא אָמַר: דְּכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא מוּתָּר לְעָרֵב חוֹבָה בִּנְדָבָה, וּדְכוּלֵּי עָלְמָא אִית לְהוּ דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר, וְהָכָא בִּפְלוּגְתָּא דְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב וְרַבָּנַן קָא מִיפַּלְגִי.
> **Translation:** **Rava said** that there is an alternative explanation of the dispute between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: **Everyone agrees** that **it is permitted to mix** an offering brought as **an obligation** together **with a gift** offering, **and everyone accepts** the opinion **of Rabbi Eliezer. And here they disagree with regard to** the issue that is the subject of **the dispute between Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov and the Rabbis.**

*Rava* now offers yet another explanation: *d'kulei alma mutar l'arev chovah b'nedava* — everyone agrees that mixing *chovah* and *nedavah* is permitted, *v'kulei alma it lehu d'Rabbi Eliezer* — and *Rabbi Eliezer*'s fuel doctrine is not a point of contention either. *Vehacha bepelugta deRabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov veRabbanan kamipalgi* — the actual dispute at the heart of the *machloket* is the well-known debate between *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* and the *Chachamim* regarding the amount of oil required for a *minchah*.

### Oil Mishnah

The *Gemara* cites the relevant *mishna*: *afilu minchah shel shishim isaron noten la shishim log* — even a *minchah* of sixty *esronim* receives sixty *log* of oil, according to the *Chachamim*, who hold that oil is always proportional, one *log* per *esaron*. *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov omer afilu minchah shel shishim isaron ein la ela loga* — *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* says that regardless of how large the *minchah* is, it receives only one *log* of oil, based on the verse *shenemar laminchah velog shemen* — "for the *minchah* and one *log* of oil" — which implies a fixed quantity of one *log* for any *minchah*.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** דִּתְנַן: אֲפִילּוּ מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עִשָּׂרוֹן נוֹתֵן לָהּ שִׁשִּׁים לוֹג. רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב אוֹמֵר: אֲפִילּוּ מִנְחָה שֶׁל שִׁשִּׁים עִשָּׂרוֹן אֵין לָהּ אֶלָּא לוּגָּהּ, שֶׁנֶּאֱמַר: ״לְמִנְחָה וְלֹג שָׁמֶן״.
> **Translation:** **As we learned** in a mishna (88a): Each tenth of an ephah of flour requires one *log* of oil. Accordingly, **even** if one brings **a meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah of flour, **one adds to it sixty *log*** of oil. **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov says:** Each meal offering, irrespective of its volume, **even a meal offering of sixty-tenths** of an ephah of flour, requires **only its** single ***log*** of oil, **as it is stated** with regard to the offering brought by a poor leper on the day of his purification: “And a tenth of an ephah of fine flour mixed with oil **for a meal offering, and a *log* of oil”** (Leviticus 14:21). The juxtaposition of “a meal offering” with “a *log* of oil” teaches a principle that pertains to all meal offerings, that each offering requires only one *log* of oil.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי [כְּרַבָּנַן], דְּאָמְרִי: שִׁשִּׁים לוֹג, וְכֹל חַד וְחַד עִשָּׂרוֹן – לוּגָּה קָא שָׁקֵיל.
> **Translation:** Rava explains: **The Rabbis** in the mishna **hold in accordance with** the opinion of **the Rabbis,** who disagree with Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov and **who said:** A meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah requires **sixty *log*** of oil. Therefore, in a case of uncertainty, it is possible to bring sixty-tenths of an ephah, **and each and every** measurement of **one-tenth takes** one ***log*.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְרַבִּי סָבַר כְּרַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב, דְּאָמַר אֵין לָהּ אֶלָּא לוּגָּהּ, וְלָא יָדְעִינַן אִי חֲדָא מִנְחָה הִיא – דְּסַגִּי לַהּ בְּחַד לוֹג, אִי שְׁתֵּי מְנָחוֹת נִינְהוּ – דְּבָעֵינַן שְׁנֵי לוּגִּין.
> **Translation:** **And Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds in accordance with** the opinion of **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov, who said** that a meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah **has only its** one ***log*** of oil. **And** therefore, when one is uncertain about his vow and brings sixty-tenths, he cannot bring them with one *log* of oil, as **we do not know whether** the entire sixty-tenths **is one meal offering,** so **that one *log* suffices for it,** or **whether they are two meal offerings,** one that is obligatory and one that is a gift, **which require two *log*** of oil. For this reason, there is no way of resolving the uncertainty except by bringing sixty separate meal offerings, with one *log* of oil for each.

### One Log Problem

The practical implication is now clear. If you do not know the amount of your *neder*, and you place everything in one vessel, you face a fundamental uncertainty about the oil. *Velo yadinan ei chada mincha hi desagi la bechad log* — is this entire vessel one *minchah* requiring only one *log* of oil? *Ei shtei menachot ninhu deva'ayan shtei lugin* — or is it in fact two *menachot*, a *neder* and a *nedavah*, each requiring its own *log*? There is no way to know, and no way to fulfill the oil requirement correctly.

### Chachamim vs. Eliezer


*Rabbanan savri keRabbanan de'amri shishim log* — the *Chachamim* hold like the majority view that oil is proportional to the number of *esronim*. Therefore, whether the vessel contains one *minchah* or two, *vechol chad vechad isaron log ka shakil* — each *esaron* takes its proportional *log* of oil, and there is no difficulty in having a *neder* and a *nedavah* in the same vessel. *Rebbi savar keRabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov de'amar ein la ela log* — *Rebbi* holds like *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* that only one *log* is required per *minchah* regardless of size. Since it is unknown whether this is one *minchah* or two, the oil requirement cannot be determined, and it is therefore impossible to fulfill the *korban* in a single vessel. This, according to *Rava*, is the true reason *Rebbi* requires every amount to be brought separately.

## Exact vs. Minimum



### Ashi's Framework

*Rav Ashi* proposes yet another framework for the debate: *bekatan vehevi gadol kamipalgi* — perhaps the dispute turns on a different question altogether: whether a vow for a specific amount means that amount exactly, or whether it merely establishes a minimum. That is, if a person said "I obligate myself to bring a *minchah* of X *esronim*," does bringing more than X satisfy the vow, or does it not?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** רַב אָשֵׁי אָמַר: בְּקָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל קָמִיפַּלְגִי. רַבָּנַן סָבְרִי: קָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל – יָצָא, וְרַבִּי סָבַר: לֹא יָצָא.
> **Translation:** **Rav Ashi said** that there is alternative explanation of the disagreement between the Rabbis and Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi: **They disagree with regard to** the *halakha* of one who is obligated to bring **a small** offering **and** instead **brings a large** offering. **The Rabbis hold** that if one is obligated to bring **a small** offering **and brings a large** one instead **he has fulfilled** his obligation, **and Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **holds** that in such a case **he has not fulfilled** his obligation. Therefore, in the case of the uncertainty in the mishna, Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi holds that by bringing one meal offering of sixty-tenths of an ephah one does not fulfill his obligation if he vowed to bring a smaller offering.

### Minimum View


*Rabbanan savri katan vehevi gadol yatza* — the *Chachamim* hold that if you vowed a small amount and brought a larger one, you have fulfilled your obligation. Under this view, you need not specify the minimum amount separately at all: you bring sixty *esronim*, and since sixty certainly exceeds whatever you vowed, your *neder* is fulfilled, and nothing is left over as a distinct *nedavah*.

### Exact View

*VeRebbi savar lo yatza* — *Rebbi* holds that a vow means the exact amount specified, and bringing more does not satisfy it. Therefore, the excess beyond the vowed amount is not part of the *neder* and creates a situation of mixing obligatory and free-will offerings, with all the attendant problems already discussed.

### Verified Debate

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא אִיפְּלִגוּ בַּהּ חֲדָא זִמְנָא, דִּתְנַן: קָטָן וְהֵבִיא גָּדוֹל – יָצָא, רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: לֹא יָצָא.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But didn’t they** already **disagree about this** topic **once? As we learned** in a mishna (107b) that if one said: It is incumbent upon me to bring **a small** bull, **and he brought a large** bull instead, **he has fulfilled** his obligation, as the value of a small bull is included in the value of a large bull. **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says: He has not fulfilled** his obligation, as the offering that he brought did not correspond to his vow.

The *Gemara* notes that this exact debate is already documented elsewhere: *detnan katan vehevi gadol yatza Rebbi omer lo yatza* — there is a later *mishna* that records this same dispute regarding an animal sacrifice: if someone vowed to bring a small animal and brought a large one, the *Chachamim* say he has fulfilled his obligation, while *Rebbi* says he has not. *Umi paligu beha zimna* — is it not already known that they disagree on this? *Tzricha* — nevertheless, both instances are necessary.

### Why Mincha Differs

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** צְרִיכָא, דְּאִי אִיתְּמַר בְּהָא – בְּהָא קָא אָמְרִי רַבָּנַן, מִשּׁוּם דְּאִידֵּי וְאִידֵּי קוֹמֶץ הוּא; אֲבָל הָתָם, דְּקָא נְפִישִׁי אֵימוּרִין – אֵימָא מוֹדוּ לֵיהּ לְרַבִּי.
> **Translation:** The Gemara answers: It is **necessary** for the dispute to be mentioned with regard to both cases, **as had** their dispute **been stated** only **with regard to this** case of one who vows to bring a small meal offering and brings a large one instead, there would be room to reason that it is only **in this** case that **the Rabbis say** that he has fulfilled his obligation, **because** both **this** small meal offering **and that** large meal offering are identical with regard to the portion of the offering that is sacrificed on the altar; in both cases **it is a handful. But there,** in the case of one who vows to bring a small bull but brings a large one, **since the sacrificial portions are greater,** i.e., larger, there is room to **say** that the Rabbis **concede to Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi that he has not fulfilled his obligation.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְאִי אִיתְּמַר בְּהָהִיא – בְּהַהִיא קָאָמַר רַבִּי, אֲבָל בְּהָא אֵימָא מוֹדֵי לְהוּ לְרַבָּנַן; צְרִיכָא.
> **Translation:** **And had** their dispute **been stated** only **in that** case of one who vows to bring a small bull and brings a big one, there would be room to reason that only **in that** case **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says** that the person has not fulfilled his obligation, because the sacrificial portions are larger. **But in this** case of bringing a large meal offering instead of a small one, there is room to **say** that Rabbi Yehuda HaNasi **concedes to** the opinion of **the Rabbis.** Therefore, it is **necessary** for the mishna to teach both cases.

*De'i itmar beha beha qa'amri Rabbanan mishum de'eidi ve'eidi qomzo* — if only the *minchah* case were stated, one might say the *Chachamim* permit bringing more because regardless of the size of the *minchah*, at the end of the day the same act occurs: one *kometz* is taken and offered on the altar. Whether the *minchah* has three *esronim* or thirty, it is still one *kometz* being burned. The quantity offered on the altar is identical in form, so one might say that a larger amount simply and naturally satisfies the vow. *Eima modi leh leRabbi* — one might then think that even *Rebbi* would agree in this case. The *tzricha* tells us that *Rebbi* does not.

Conversely, *ve'i itmar beha ika le-meimar le-Rabbi* — if only the animal case were stated, *Rebbi*'s position there makes intuitive sense, since a larger animal has more *imurin* — internal organs — placed on the altar, which is a fundamentally different act in quantitative terms, not merely the same act performed on a larger substrate. *Aval be-ha* — but in the case of a *minchah* where regardless of size it is always one *kometz* being offered, *eima modu le-Rabbanan* — one might think *Rebbi* would concede to the *Chachamim*. The *tzricha* confirms that he does not, and the debate applies equally in both contexts.

### Bottom Line

To summarize: according to *Rav Ashi*, *Rebbi*'s requirement to bring sixty separate vessels rests on his position that a vow for X means exactly X — not a minimum of X. Bringing more does not satisfy the vow but rather creates a mixture of a *neder* and a *nedavah*, and that mixture, combined with all the difficulties of oil proportionality or the prohibition on mixing obligatory and free-will offerings, is why *Rebbi* insists on strict separation.

## Wood & Metals



### Wood Rules

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** סִימָן: עֵצִים, זָהָב, יַיִן, עוֹלָה, תּוֹדָה, שׁוֹר.
> **Translation:** § The Gemara gives **a mnemonic** for the *halakhot* discussed in the upcoming *mishnayot*: **Wood, gold, wine, burnt offering, thanks offering, bull.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מַתְנִי׳ הֲרֵי עָלַי עֵצִים – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִשְּׁנֵי גְזִירִין, לְבוֹנָה – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִקּוֹמֶץ.
> **Translation:** MISHNA: One who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to donate pieces of **wood** as fuel for the altar, **must** donate **no fewer than two logs.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to bring **frankincense, must** bring **no less than a handful.**

The *Gemara* moves to the next *mishna*. *Harei alai etzim* — if someone obligates himself to bring wood to the altar. Wood, as will be seen, may be vowed as a voluntary offering to be placed on the altar. *Lo yifchot mi-shnei gzirin* — the minimum is two logs of wood. *Tosafot* adds: *ve-davka ki amar etzim aval ki amar etz maytei gzir echad* — this specifically applies when you say "wood" in the plural; if you say "wood" in the singular, one log suffices.

### Frankincense

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** חֲמִשָּׁה קְמָצִים הֵם: הָאוֹמֵר ״עָלַי לְבוֹנָה״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִקּוֹמֶץ, הַמְנַדֵּב מִנְחָה – יָבִיא עִמָּהּ קוֹמֶץ לְבוֹנָה, הַמַּעֲלֶה אֶת הַקּוֹמֶץ בַּחוּץ – חַיָּיב, שְׁנֵי בָּזִיכִין טְעוּנִין שְׁנֵי קְמָצִים.
> **Translation:** The mishna states tangentially: **There are five** *halakhot* pertaining to **handfuls. One who says:** It is incumbent **upon me** to bring an offering of **frankincense, may not** bring **less than a handful. One who pledges** to bring **a meal offering must bring with it a handful of frankincense. One who** intentionally **offers up a handful** of a meal offering **outside** the Temple courtyard is **liable** to receive excision from the World-to-Come [*karet*]. The **two bowls** of frankincense that accompany the shewbread are **required** to have **two handfuls** of frankincense.

*Harei alai levonah* — if someone obligates himself to bring frankincense. *Lo yifchot mi-kometz* — the minimum is one *kometz* of frankincense. There are five standard *kematzim* of frankincense mentioned in context: *ha-omer harei alai levonah lo yifchot mi-kometz* — one who vows to bring frankincense must bring no less than a *kometz*; *ha-menadev mincha yavi ima kometz levonah* — one who brings a voluntary *minchah* brings with it a *kometz* of frankincense; *ha-ma'aleh et ha-kometz ba-chutz chayav* — one who burns a *kometz* of frankincense outside the Temple is liable; and *shnei bzichin te'unim shnei kmatzim* — the two dishes accompanying the showbread each require a *kometz* of frankincense. *Tosafot* notes that one of the novel points about the case of *ma'aleh ba-chutz* is that one is liable even for burning only one of the two dishes, though it represents only half the total amount.

### Precious Metals

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״הֲרֵי עָלַי זָהָב״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינַר זָהָב, ״כֶּסֶף״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינַר כֶּסֶף, ״נְחֹשֶׁת״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִמָּעָה כֶּסֶף. ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יְהֵא מֵבִיא עַד שֶׁיֹּאמַר ״לֹא לְכָךְ נִתְכַּוַּונְתִּי״.
> **Translation:** One who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to donate **gold** to the Temple treasury, **must** give **no less than a gold dinar.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **silver** to the Temple treasury, **must** give **no less than** the value of **a silver dinar.** One who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **copper** to the Temple maintenance, **must** give **no less than** the value of **a silver *ma’a*.** One who says: **I specified** the amount of gold, silver, or copper, **but I do not know what I specified, must bring** the maximum amount of gold, silver, or copper, **until** it reaches an amount **where he says:** I am certain that **I did not intend to** donate **that** much.

*Harei alai zahav lo yifchot mi-dinar zahav* — if one obligates himself to bring gold, the minimum is one gold *dinar*. *Kesef lo yifchot mi-dinar kesef* — silver, not less than one silver *dinar*. *Nechoshet lo yifchot mi-ma'ah kesef* — copper, not less than the value of one silver *ma'ah*, which is one-sixth of a silver *dinar*. To put this in perspective: a gold *dinar* is worth twenty-five silver *dinarin*, and each silver *dinar* contains six *ma'ah*. Thus, when one says gold, the minimum is a gold *dinar* worth twenty-five times a silver *dinar*; when one says silver, the minimum is a silver *dinar*; and when one says copper, the minimum is copper valued at one-sixth of a silver *dinar*. Finally: *perashti ve-eini yode'a mah perashti* — if one specified a type of donation but forgot how much, *mevi ad she-yomar lo le-kach nitkavanti* — one brings increasingly larger amounts until one can say, "I certainly did not intend that much."

## Frankincense Default



### Default is Frankincense

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** גְּמָ׳ תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״קׇרְבָּן״ – מְלַמֵּד שֶׁמִּתְנַדְּבִין עֵצִים, וְכַמָּה? שְׁנֵי גְּזִירִין. וְכֵן הוּא אוֹמֵר: ״וְהַגּוֹרָלוֹת הִפַּלְנוּ עַל קֻרְבַּן הָעֵצִים״. רַבִּי אוֹמֵר: עֵצִים קׇרְבָּן הֵם, טְעוּנִין מֶלַח וּטְעוּנִין הַגָּשָׁה.
> **Translation:** GEMARA: With regard to one who pledges to donate wood, **the Sages taught** in a *baraita*: The verse states: “And when one brings a meal offering [*korban minḥa*]**”** (Leviticus 2:1). The superfluous word “***korban***” **teaches that one can voluntarily give wood** as an offering for the altar. **And how much** wood must one bring if he does not specify an amount? **Two logs. And** the support for the fact that wood can be brought as a voluntary offering is **as** the verse **states: “And we cast lots for the wood offering”** (Nehemiah 10:35). **Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi **says:** This voluntary donation of **wood is an offering** like a meal offering, **and** therefore it **requires salt and requires bringing** to the corner of the altar, like a meal offering.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אָמַר רָבָא: וּלְדִבְרֵי רַבִּי, עֵצִים טְעוּנִין קְמִיצָה. אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא: לְדִבְרֵי רַבִּי, עֵצִים צְרִיכִין עֵצִים.
> **Translation:** **Rava says: According to the statement of Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi, **wood** donated in this manner **requires** the **removal of a handful;** just as in the case of a meal offering, a portion of the wood must be removed and sacrificed separately. **And Rav Pappa says** that **according to the statement of Rabbi** Yehuda HaNasi, since it is an offering for the altar, the **wood** that is brought as an offering **needs** to be placed on other **wood** to burn, like any other offering that is burned on wood on the altar.

The *Gemara* now presents a *baraita*: *Tanu Rabanan* — if someone says *harei alai la-mizbeach* — "I obligate myself to bring something to the altar" — without specifying what, *yavi levonah* — he must bring frankincense. The reason given is: *she-ein lecha davar she-karav legabei mizbeach ella levonah* — there is nothing that is offered directly on the altar except frankincense. This is a startling statement that immediately demands explanation — what about all other *korbanot*? The *Gemara* will address this shortly.

The *baraita* also addresses a further case: *perashti ve-eini yode'a mah perashti* — if one said explicitly that he is obligating himself to bring something to the altar and cannot remember what, *yavi mi-kol davar she-karav legabei mizbeach* — one must bring from every category of offering that is placed on the altar: an *olah*, a *shelamim*, a *minchah*, and so forth. Every possible fulfillment must be covered. Before turning to the frankincense minimum, it is worth noting that the *baraita* has just finished treating wood as a legitimate category of vow — and with it, a series of escalating implications. Since the verse *ve-nefesh ki takriv korban mincha* uses the word *korban* before *mincha*, the *Gemara* derives that one may vow to donate wood, *melamed she-mitnadvim etzim*, and this is further confirmed by the verse in Nechemiah referring to *korban ha-etzim*, a wood offering actually brought on the *mizbeach*. Rabbi then draws the consequence that since wood is called a *korban*, *te'unim melach* — it requires salt like any *korban* — and *te'unim hagasha* — it must be brought to the altar in the manner of a *minchah*, since wood, coming from the plant world rather than an animal, is most naturally assimilated to that category. Rava pushes further: *le-divrei Rabbi etzim te'unim kmitza* — according to Rabbi, wood would also require a *kmitza*, a taking of a handful, since if we are reading it into the *parsha* of *mincha* in full, that obligation should follow as well, and he means this seriously rather than as a mere theoretical extension. Rav Pappa then takes it to its logical, almost absurdist conclusion: *le-divrei Rabbi etzim tzrichim etzim* — according to Rabbi, wood requires wood, meaning it would have to be burned on top of other wood on the *ma'aracha*, the fire-pile. The point is that treating the wood vow as a full-fledged *korban* on the model of *mincha* generates increasingly strained requirements, which casts some doubt on whether this is precisely what the *Mishna* intended — though that is where the analysis leads.

### Frankincense Kometz

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** לְבוֹנָה – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִן הַקּוֹמֶץ. מְנָלַן? דִּכְתִיב: ״וְהֵרִים מִמֶּנּוּ בְּקֻמְצוֹ מִסֹּלֶת הַמִּנְחָה וּמִשַּׁמְנָהּ וְאֵת כׇּל הַלְּבֹנָה״. מַקִּישׁ לְבוֹנָה לַהֲרָמָה דְּמִנְחָה – מָה הֲרָמָה דְּמִנְחָה קוֹמֶץ, אַף לְבוֹנָה נָמֵי קוֹמֶץ.
> **Translation:** The mishna teaches: One who says: It is incumbent upon me to bring **frankincense, may not** bring **less than a handful.** The Gemara asks: **From where do we** derive this *halakha*? The Gemara answers: It is derived from a verse, **as it is written: “And he shall take up from it his handful, of the fine flour of the meal offering, and of its oil, and all the frankincense** which is upon the meal offering, and shall make the memorial part of it smoke upon the altar for an aroma pleasing to the Lord” (Leviticus 6:8). The Torah **juxtaposes** the **frankincense with the taking up** of a handful **of the meal offering.** This indicates that **just as** the **taking up from a meal offering** is referring to **a handful, so too,** the minimum size of **a frankincense** offering is **also a handful.**

The minimum quantity for a vow of frankincense is one *kometz*, derived from the following verse: *veherim mimenu vekomzo misolet hamincha umishamnah ve-et kol halevona* — "and he shall raise from it his handful of the fine flour of the *minchah* and its oil, and all the frankincense." *Maquish levona le-harama de-mincha* — the frankincense is hereby equated to the taking of the *kometz* of the *minchah* itself. *Mah harama de-mincha kometz af levona nami kometz* — just as the standard unit of the *minchah* offering is a *kometz*, so too the standard unit of frankincense is a *kometz*. The *Gemara* here is explaining not how we know one can vow frankincense in the first place, but how we know that the unit of frankincense — once vowed — is measured in *kematzim*.

## Wood, Metals & Incense



### Uncertainty Rule

If a person declared *harei alai la-mizbeach* but forgot what he had in mind, *yavi mi-kol davar she-karav legabei mizbeach* — he must bring from every category of permitted altar offerings. Since he cannot determine which type of offering he intended, he covers every possibility.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** תָּנוּ רַבָּנַן: ״הֲרֵי עָלַי לַמִּזְבֵּחַ״ – יָבִיא לְבוֹנָה, שֶׁאֵין לְךָ דָּבָר שֶׁקָּרֵב לְגַבֵּי מִזְבֵּחַ אֶלָּא לְבוֹנָה. ״פֵּירַשְׁתִּי וְאֵינִי יוֹדֵעַ מָה פֵּירַשְׁתִּי״ – יָבִיא מִכׇּל דָּבָר שֶׁקָּרֵב לַמִּזְבֵּחַ.
> **Translation:** § **The Sages taught** in a *baraita* which discusses vows to bring frankincense that one who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to bring **to the altar,** and does not specify what he will bring, **must bring frankincense, as you have nothing that is** entirely **sacrificed on the altar other than frankincense.** One who says: **I specified** what I intended to bring to the altar, **but I do not know what I specified, must bring** one **of everything that is sacrificed on the altar.**

## Frankincense Default (II)



### Why Frankincense


> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְתוּ לֵיכָּא? וְהָא אִיכָּא עוֹלָה! אִיכָּא עוֹרָהּ לְכֹהֲנִים.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **And is there nothing else** that is entirely sacrificed on the altar other than frankincense? **But isn’t there a burnt offering,** which is entirely burned on the altar? The Gemara answers that **there is its hide,** which is given **to the priests** and not burned on the altar.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָא אִיכָּא עוֹלַת הָעוֹף, אִיכָּא
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But isn’t there a bird burnt offering,** which is entirely burned on the altar, including its skin? The Gemara answers: **There are**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** מוּרְאָה וְנוֹצָה.
> **Translation:** **the crop and feathers,** which are thrown on the ground next to the altar and are not burned.

Returning to the claim that frankincense is the only offering placed on the altar: *v'tu lo* — is that really so? *Ve-ika olah* — what about a burnt offering, which is entirely consumed? The *Gemara* responds: *or le-kohanim* — the hide of the animal goes to the *kohanim*, so not everything goes to the altar. *Ve-ika olat ha-of* — what about a bird burnt offering, where everything is thrown on the altar? The *Gemara* responds: *ika mur'ah ve-notzah* — the crop and feathers are removed and do not go on the altar.

### Exceptions Discussed

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָאִיכָּא נְסָכִים, לַשִּׁיתִין אָזְלִי.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But aren’t there** wine **libations,** which are poured entirely onto the altar? The Gemara answers that the wine is not actually poured onto the altar; it is poured into ducts on the side of the altar and **goes** down **to the drainpipes.**

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** וְהָאִיכָּא מִנְחַת נְסָכִים? כֵּיוָן דְּאִיכָּא מִנְחָה דְּאָכְלִי כֹּהֲנִים מִינַּהּ, לָא פְּסִיקָא לֵיהּ.
> **Translation:** The Gemara asks: **But isn’t there a meal offering** brought with **libations,** which is entirely burned on the altar? The Gemara answers that when one says: It is incumbent upon me to bring to the altar, his intent is clearly to bring a type of offering that is only sacrificed on the altar. A meal offering brought with libations is a type of meal offering. Therefore, **since there are** other types of **a meal offering from which the priests eat,** after a handful has been removed from it and burned, a meal offering brought with libations **is not a clear** example of an offering that is sacrificed on the altar in its entirety, and certainly was not **his** intent. Therefore, his intent must have been to bring frankincense.

Continuing into 107a: *ve-ika nesachim* — what about wine libations, which go entirely on the altar? *Le-shitin azli* — the wine pours into the underground channels, *shitin*, beneath the altar; it is assumed that when someone says *la-mizbeach* he means to be burned on the altar fire, not poured into channels. *Ve-ika minchat nesachim* — what about the *minchat nesachim*, the meal offering accompanying libations, which is entirely burned? The *Gemara* responds: *keivan de-ika mincha de-achli kohanim minah, lo paskinah beh* — since *menachot* in general are eaten by *kohanim*, when a person declares he is bringing something fully to the altar, the category of *minchah* does not come to mind as a wholly burned offering. Even though a specific type of *minchah* would indeed satisfy that description, the mental default image of a *minchah* is one that the *kohanim* eat.

### Mental Default

The upshot is this: *Tosafot* further asks, why not include oil — at least according to the view in an earlier chapter that oil may be vowed on its own and fully burned? The answer is the same: *keivan de-ika shemen de-achli Kohanim lo paski leh* — since oil is also eaten by the *kohanim* when mixed with a *minchah*, it does not register as a wholly altar-consumed offering. *Tosafot* also asks about wood, noting that even according to *Rebbi* who requires a *kemitzah* from it, all of it goes on the altar — yet it is not listed. The answer is that *levonah* is the paradigmatic mental image of something wholly consumed by the altar fire. When a person says *harei alai la-mizbeach* without specification, frankincense — the one substance that exists solely to be burned on the outer altar — is what he must have meant.

## Metal Precision



### Gold Coin

The *Gemara* now examines the metal vow rulings in greater detail. Regarding the gold minimum of one gold *dinar*: *ve-dilma naska* — perhaps when one said gold, he meant a gold ingot or bar rather than a coin? *Amar Rabbi Elazar de-amar matbe'a* — *Rabbi Elazar* explains that we interpret the vow as referring to a gold coin, not an ingot. *Tosafot* discusses the further question of whether an ingot would be considered more or less than the minimum, though that is not resolved here.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״הֲרֵי עָלַי זָהָב״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינַר זָהָב. וְדִלְמָא נְסָכָא? אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר: דְּאָמַר ״מַטְבֵּעַ״. וְדִלְמָא פְּרִיטֵי? אָמַר רַב פָּפָּא: פְּרִיטֵי דְּדַהֲבָא לָא עָבְדִי אִינָשֵׁי.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches that one who says: **It** is incumbent **upon me** to donate **gold** to the Temple treasury, **must** donate **no less than a gold dinar.** The Gemara challenges: **But perhaps** his intention in using the word gold is not to a coin at all, but to a small **piece [*naskha*]** of gold. **Rabbi Elazar said:** The case of the *baraita* is **where he said** the word **coin.** The Gemara challenges further: **But perhaps** his intention is not to a dinar, but to smaller coins, such as ***perutot*. Rav Pappa said: People do not make *perutot* of gold.** Therefore, it is unlikely that this was his intent.

### Silver Coin


*Ve-dilma priti* — perhaps the person meant small change, like a small gold piece? *Amar Abaye priti de-dahava lo avdi inshi* — *Abaye* responds: people do not make gold small change. Gold simply does not come in the denomination of small coins; the standard unit of gold currency is the *dinar*, and that is what one intends.

*Kesef lo yifchos mi-dinar* — for silver, the minimum is likewise a silver *dinar*. *Ve-dilma naska* — perhaps a silver ingot was intended? *Amar Rabbi Elazar de-amar matbe'a* — again, *Rabbi Elazar* rules that a coin is intended. *Ve-dilma priti* — perhaps silver small change?

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״כֶּסֶף״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִדִּינָר. וְדִלְמָא נְסָכָא? אָמַר רַבִּי אֶלְעָזָר: דְּאָמַר ״מַטְבֵּעַ״, וְדִלְמָא פְּרִיטֵי? אָמַר רַב שֵׁשֶׁת: בְּאַתְרָא דְּלָא סָגוּ פְּרִיטֵי דְכַסְפָּא.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches that one who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **silver** to the Temple maintenance, **must** donate **no less than** the value of a silver **dinar.** The Gemara challenges: **But perhaps** his intention in using the word silver is not to a coin at all, but to a small **piece** of silver. **Rabbi Elazar said:** The case of the *baraita* is **where he said** the word **coin.** The Gemara challenges further: **But perhaps** his intention is not to a dinar, but to smaller coins, such as ***perutot*. Rav Pappa said:** The *halakha* of the *baraita* is stated **with regard to a place where silver *perutot* do not circulate.**

### Regional Context


*Amar Rav Sheshes be-asra lo sagi priti de-kaspa* — *Rav Sheshes* qualifies: this ruling applies in a place where silver small change is not commonly used for transactions, where people use copper coins for that purpose. In such a locale, when one says silver, one certainly means a silver *dinar*, not small change. The implication is that in a place where silver small change is prevalent, the ruling might differ.

### Copper Fork

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** ״נְחוֹשֶׁת״ – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִמָּעָה כֶּסֶף. תַּנְיָא: רַבִּי אֱלִיעֶזֶר בֶּן יַעֲקֹב אוֹמֵר – לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִצִּינּוֹרָא קְטַנָּה שֶׁל נְחֹשֶׁת. לְמַאי חַזְיָא? אָמַר אַבָּיֵי: שֶׁמְּחַטְּטִין בָּהּ פְּתִילוֹת, וּמְקַנְּחִין בָּהּ נֵרוֹת.
> **Translation:** § The mishna teaches that one who says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **copper** to the Temple maintenance, **must** donate **no less than** the value of **a silver *ma’a*. It is taught** in a *baraita* that **Rabbi Eliezer ben Ya’akov says: He must** donate **no less than** the amount needed to forge **a small copper hook.** The Gemara asks: **For what** use **is** that **suitable** in the Temple? **Abaye said: They scrape** the **wicks** from the Candelabrum **with it and clean** the **lamps** of the Candelabrum **with it.**

*Nechoshes lo yifchos mi-ma'ah kesef* — copper must be worth at least a silver *ma'ah*. A *baraita* records: *Tanya Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov omer lo yifchos mi-tzinnora ketana shel nechoshes* — *Rabbi Eliezer ben Yaakov* says the minimum is not less than a small copper fork or tine. *Le-mai chazei* — what is this used for? *Amar Abaye she-mechattin bo pesilos u-mekanchin bo neros* — *Abaye* explains that it is used to clean out lamp wicks and maintain lamps. *Tosafot* specifies that this is not referring to the Temple *menorah* specifically, but to ordinary lamps. Thus, for copper, rather than specifying a monetary value equivalent, the *baraita* describes a concrete minimum object made of copper that people actually produce and use.

### Iron Spike

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** בַּרְזֶל, תַּנְיָא, אֲחֵרִים אוֹמְרִים: לֹא יִפְחוֹת מִכָּלְיָה עוֹרֵב, וְכַמָּה? אָמַר רַב יוֹסֵף: אַמָּה עַל אַמָּה.
> **Translation:** The mishna discusses pledges of gold, silver, and copper. What is the *halakha* if one says: It is incumbent upon me to donate **iron? It is taught** in a *baraita* that **others say:** He **must** donate **no less than** the amount that can be made into a base and spike designed to **eliminate the ravens [*mikkalya orev*].** The Gemara asks: **And how much** is that? **Rav Yosef said:** One **cubit** in width **by** one **cubit** in length.

> **Hebrew/Aramaic:** אִיכָּא דְאָמְרִי: לֹא יִפְחוֹת מֵאַמָּה עַל אַמָּה. לְמַאי חַזְיָא? אָמַר רַב יוֹסֵף: לְכָלְיָה עוֹרֵב.
> **Translation:** **There are** those **who say** another version of this *baraita* and the subsequent explanation: One who pledges to donate iron **must** donate **no less than** one **cubit** in width **by** one **cubit** in length. The Gemara asks: **For what is** this amount of iron **suitable? Rav Yosef said:** It is suitable **for** a base and spike designed to **eliminate the ravens.**

Finally, the *mishna*'s category of iron: *Tanya hachi nami lo yifchos mi-kila orev* — a *baraita* likewise teaches that the minimum for an iron vow is no less than the amount of iron needed to make a spike for warding off ravens. *Ve-kamma* — how large is this? *Amar Rav Yosef amma al amma* — an *amma* by an *amma* of iron. Some versions of the text read: *ika de-amri lo yifchos me-amma al amma* — others record this as "not less than an *amma* by an *amma*." *Le-mai chazei* — what is such an iron piece used for? *Amar Rav Yosef le-kila orev* — for the spike used to drive away ravens. The Temple rooftop would attract ravens drawn by the smell of the sacrificial meat, and iron spikes were installed to prevent them from perching. This serves as the standard unit of iron, much as the copper fork serves as the standard unit for copper.
