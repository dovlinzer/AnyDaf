Word Gloss Pilot

Every word of 12 amudim, glossed by two models, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, and linked to an entry in Jastrow's dictionary. Click a gloss or an entry to mark it wrong; click again to unmark. Yellow rows are where the two models chose different Jastrow entries.

Where each part comes from
  1. The English gloss is written by the model. For each line it reads the Aramaic, the William Davidson English translation and Steinsaltz's Hebrew explanation, and writes a short gloss of what each word means in that line ("all of it", "but let him teach"). Nothing is looked up for this part.
  2. The dictionary form (the small Hebrew before the gloss) is the model's own guess at the form Jastrow lists the word under. It is only a spelling: Jastrow often has several entries with that spelling.
  3. The Jastrow entry is chosen in a second step. We fetch from Sefaria every Jastrow entry the word or its dictionary form could point to (headword plus the opening of the definition), and the model picks the one that fits the gloss. Shown here: the chosen headword and the opening of its definition.

So the gloss and the entry can disagree. In the first pilot כולה got the right gloss, "all of it", but its spelling also matches Jastrow's כּוּלָה, "a measure", and the old matching picked that one. Step 3 is meant to catch exactly this. Note also that an entry's first words can mislead: Jastrow's אָמַר I opens "to join, knot" but is the right entry for Hebrew "to say", which he derives from joining words.
