# Sheidim (Demons) in the AnyDaf Corpus — 51 Dapim

A survey of every reference to sheidim, witchcraft, necromancy, demonic spirits, and related supernatural content across the 2,350 processed dapim of Gemara, drawn from the segmentation keyword files.

---

## I. Ashmadai (Ashmodai) — King of Demons

**Gittin 68a** — The central sheidim daf in the corpus. An extended aggadic narrative:
- Solomon needs the Shamir worm to cut the Temple stones without iron. He learns from male and female demons that Ashmadai (king of the sheidim) knows where it is.
- Benayahu captures Ashmadai using wine, wool, and a chain.
- There are 300 types of demons at a place called Shichin.
- Ashmadai is described as righteous and merciful in his own way — he makes moral observations while captive.
- Solomon releases Ashmadai to test his power. Ashmadai swallows Solomon and casts him 400 parasangs away.
- Ashmadai impersonates Solomon; the Sanhedrin detects the deception. Solomon is restored; Ashmadai escapes.
- Solomon ever after posts 60 warriors around his bed as guards against Ashmadai.
- The "Kordyakos" mental illness is linked to a demon; knowing the demon's name matters for writing protective amulets.

**Pesachim 110a** — Rav Yosef and "Yosef the Shed" (a shed named Yosef): the demon Ashmadai controls supernatural forces behind the danger of drinking in pairs. Includes a named female demon (Meimar/Rishtina) who heads women witches, and anti-witchcraft incantations.

## II. Lilith and the Lilith-like Fetus

**Niddah 24b** — A fetus with a winged, human-like form (Lilith-like appearance) is discussed. The case of "Simuni" — confirmed by the Sages as a kosher fetus despite its Lilith-like appearance. Defines the Lilith as a winged human being.

**Bava Batra 73a** — Hormin bar Lilit: Rabbah sees the demon Hormin bar Lilit running on the wall of Machuza. He performs an acrobatic feat (jumping between mules while holding wine cups). Hormin is identified as a half-demon — offspring of a human and a demoness (Lilit). He eventually dies. A student interprets him allegorically as the yetzer hara leading to self-indulgent barrenness.

## III. Agrath Bath Mahlath and Nocturnal Demons

**Pesachim 113** — A sustained passage on night demons:
- Agrath Bath Mahlath and her retinue of 180,000 angels of destruction go out at night.
- Rabbi Chanina ben Dosa encounters Agrath and she decrees she will not harm inhabited places.
- Abaye later encounters her and further restricts her.
- Demons still pass through narrow alleyways; someone on a horse can escape.
- The daf warns: don't go out alone at night.

## IV. Ketev Meriri and Ketev Yashud Tzahorayim (Noon and Afternoon Demons)

**Pesachim 111b** — Two named demonic pestilences:
- Ketev Meriri: a noon demon, appears when sunlight hits shadow going westward.
- Ketev Yashud Tzahorayim: an afternoon demon.
- A story: Abaye encounters Ketev Meriri while walking with Rav Papa and Rav Huna.
- Also: the demon of a sorb tree near a city (sixty demons); amulets written against multiple named demons; demonic spirits associated with palm tree stumps; a special tree (Karumashe) whose demonic parents are forbidden to inhabit it.

## V. Sheidim in Everyday Life (Protective Practices)

**Avodah Zarah 12a-b** — Drinking water at night:
- Prohibition on drinking water at night due to "Shed" dangers.
- A specific demon: Shavrizi (blindness demon) lurks in nighttime water.
- Protective measures: bang a jug, wake someone else, recite an incantation that "eradicates letters."

**Bava Kamma 21b** — Mar bar Rav Ashi observes: desolate/ruined houses are where sheidim congregate — cited as evidence that desolation is dangerous.

**Berakhot 5** — Rabbi Yitzchak: reciting Kriat Shema at bedtime drives away mazikin (the demon-plural). Reish Lakish: Torah study drives away yissurin (sufferings).

**Bava Metzia 102a** — Why a departing tenant must leave the mezuzah behind: Tosafot's reason is demon protection; Ritva's reason is Divine Presence.

**Bava Metzia 107b** — Thirteen wonders of eating Pat Shacharit (morning bread), including: protection from the sun, cold, wind, and demons.

**Sanhedrin 93b** — On sudden fear: the Gemara discusses mazal (fate), demons, and protective remedies.

**Sanhedrin 44a** — Joshua encounters a figure at night; the halacha is that you cannot greet someone at night because it might be a demon. Demons will not say God's name, which is how the angel identifies himself as not a demon.

**Yevamot 122a-b** — Demons and co-wives are listed together as inherently untrustworthy sources for testimony (e.g., about a husband's death).

**Sanhedrin 95a** — David pursues enemies into Philistine territory and encounters a demonic deer that leads him astray.

## VI. Spirits Under Drain Pipes and Household Witchcraft

**Hullin 105** — A rich passage on mystical explanations for practical rules:
- Mayim acharonim (washing after meals): the real reason is ruach ra'a (evil spirits) in the water.
- Foam from a cup: Rabba says it causes vulnerability to witchcraft (kshafim).
- Eating a vegetable straight from a bundle: exposes you to witchcraft.
- Sitting under a drain pipe: mazikin (demons) inhabit drain pipes.
- A story about schleppers who encounter the spirit under a drain pipe — involves witchcraft, cherem, and a negotiation over payment.

## VII. Healing Incantations and Named Demonic Spirits

**Shabbat 67a-b** — Abaye's healing incantations, learned from his nurse-maid:
- One-day fever: salt remedy and river incantation.
- Boils and skin conditions: doubled-word incantations.
- Bathroom demon and other shades: exorcism incantations against toilet demons.
- Discussion of the rational vs. mystical basis of incantations (Rambam's scientific skepticism vs. the Gra's defense).
- Darchei Emori list from Tosefta: forbidden incantations involving luck, marital names, wine.

**Gittin 69a-b** — Medical remedies involving ritual incantations:
- Night blindness (shavrire de-leilya): complex incantation remedy.
- Day blindness (shavrire de-yami): using spleens and incantations.
- Nosebleed treatment: writing and incantations; role of a Kohen named Levi.
- Spleen ailment: using hand of person who died on Shabbat with a drying incantation.

**Sanhedrin 101b** — Reciting verses over wounds: incantations and divine names. Permitted incantations against snakes and scorpions on Shabbat. Restrictions on inquiring of demons and the danger involved. A story about Rabbi Yitzchak bar Shmuel and dangerous oil with harmful incantations.

**Yoma 84a-b** — Rabid dog: two competing explanations — witchcraft vs. evil spirit. The practical difference between the two theories determines the remedy. Abaye's remedy includes a hyena leather incantation and burial process.

## VIII. Ba'al Ov (Necromancy)

**Keritot 4** — What counts as a maaseh (legal act) for ba'al ov:
- Flapping the arms as a halakhic action.
- Ulla: Ba'al Ov involves burning incense to a demon/spirit, not idol worship.
- Abaye: invoking spirits (chover chaver) is a separate prohibition from avodah zarah.

**Sanhedrin 65a-b** — Ba'al Ov and Yidoni (death penalty offenses):
- Definitions: Ba'al Ov speaks from the mouth/between joints/armpits.
- Ulla: burning incense to a demon.
- Rava: Mechattar lechever = necromantic witchcraft (different from avodah zarah).
- Rabbi Yochanan: why Ba'al Ov is listed first.

**Sanhedrin 66b** — Forms of Ba'al Ov in detail:
- Raising the dead through spirit possession.
- Two forms: through the male organ vs. through a skull.
- Ba'al Ov (necromancer) vs. Doreish El HaMetim (simpler prohibition).
- Rambam vs. Ramban: Rambam says all witchcraft is illusion and punished only as fraud; Ramban says witchcraft is real and prohibited precisely because it is real.

**Shabbat 152** — The heretic challenges Rabbi Abahu: how could the Witch of Ein Dor bring up Samuel's soul if souls are in heaven? (Halachic/theological debate about what actually happened at Endor.)

## IX. Witchcraft (Kishuf/Keshafim) — Legal Discussions

**Sanhedrin 67a-b** — Core daf on witchcraft theology:
- Mechashef (sorcerer): actual magic vs. ha'ochaz et ha'einayim (illusionist).
- Tannaitic debate: Rabbi Yossi HaGlili says sword; Rabbi Akiva says stoning.
- Rabbi Yochanan: Mechashefim "weaken heavenly powers"; Rabbi Chanina's personal immunity to witchcraft through trust in God.
- Egyptian sorcerers: their "baltihem" = demons; "belahateihem" = direct magic.
- Abaye's distinction: when a particular vessel is used → indicates shedim are involved; no vessel needed → direct keshafim.
- Yanai at the inn: detects witchcraft by watching lip movement; pours water test; retaliatory enchantment.

**Sanhedrin 68a** — Rabbi Eliezer plants and uproots gourds through witchcraft on the way to Rabbi Akiva; this raises a question: how could a rabbi do this? Answer: learning witchcraft to understand and teach it is permitted; doing it is not.

**Sanhedrin 17b** — Sanhedrin composition requirements: judges must have knowledge of witchcraft (to be able to judge witchcraft cases).

**Sanhedrin 53a-b** — Witchcraft is one of the sekilah (stoning) offenses in the Mishnah's list.

**Sanhedrin 56a** — The Seven Noahide Laws: kishuf (witchcraft) is mentioned in additional interpretations.

**Sanhedrin 91a** — Gifts to sons of the concubines: the Gemara links these names to witchcraft and heretical beliefs.

**Sanhedrin 108a** — The story of Yehoshua ben Perachiah and Yeishu HaNotzri: kishuf (magic) as a framework for understanding.

**Shabbat 81b–82a** — Bathroom use on Shabbat: witchcraft concerns when using pottery; three practices that make one vulnerable to witchcraft; flammable objects and witchcraft hazards.

**Shabbat 33** — Three causes of intestinal dropsy (hidrokan): sin, famine, or witchcraft.

**Shabbat 104b** — Ben Stada smuggled witchcraft from Egypt by scratching formulas into his flesh.

**Hullin 16a-b** — Hand-wiping with reeds: the concern is witchcraft (not just splinters).

**Bekhorot 45b** — Demonic spirits as physical defects disqualifying a kohen: Ben Nephalim (possibly an evil spirit or epilepsy); Ruach Shtus (spirit of folly) and demonic possession per Rashi.

**Berakhot 53** — Walking outside a city: witchcraft concerns when saying a blessing on spices. Rabbi Yossi: even if most people are Jewish, witchcraft is practiced, so a blessing cannot be made.

**Berakhot 9a** — Elijah at Carmel: his double prayer "Aneini Hashem Aneini" was to prevent the crowd from thinking the fire was caused by witchcraft.

**Eruvin 62a** — A non-Jew refuses to participate in an eruv rental: the reason given is a witchcraft concern.

**Gittin 45b** — Rav Nachman's daughters: debate over whether they were righteous women or witches. Rashi's witchcraft theory; a bird-listener's death is attributed to witchcraft.

**Sotah 22a-b** — "Destroyers of the world" includes: Yochni bat Retivi, identified by Rashi as a witch-midwife. Also: a "magus" is compared to someone reciting incantations without understanding them.

**Yoma 11a** — The Artavin story: a fine of 1000 zuz is connected to a perception of witchcraft.

**Yoma 75b** — The manna: one opinion says it changed colors "like a demon" or like breast milk.

**Yevamot 4b** — Smuchim (adjacent verses in Torah): ochshin (fruit stalks) and witchcraft are cited as a paradigm case where proximity of verses matters.

**Shevuot 15b** — Using verses as healing: debate over whether this is protection or forbidden magical incantation.

**Pesachim 111b** — Witchcraft:
- Four dangerous acts that expose a person to mortal danger from demons and witchcraft.
- Two women facing each other at a crossroads = evidence of witchcraft.
- Remedies: take alternate route, find a companion, or recite demonic names and curses.
- Bumping into a woman leaving the mikveh: can lead to a demonic spirit of licentiousness.
- Tosafot's concern: using a Torah verse as incantation vs. preventative prayer.

**Pesachim 112** — Household demon dangers:
- Demons go out on Friday nights and Wednesday nights.
- Rambam rejects demonic concerns entirely ("nature has changed").
- Food left under the bed: ruach ra'ah (evil spirit) rests on it.
- Protective incantations and rituals for drinking water at night.

## X. Demonic Possession and Exorcism

**Meilah 17a** — Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai's mission to Rome:
- A demon named Ben Tamlayon appears and offers to help.
- Rabbi Shimon complains: why do I get a demon, not an angel?
- Ben Tamlayon possesses Caesar's daughter.
- Rabbi Shimon is summoned and exorcises Ben Tamlayon by calling his name — the possession ends, Rome's evil decrees are reversed.

**Kiddushin 29a** — A demon (referred to as "mazik" = destructive spirit) inhabits the yeshiva of Abaye:
- Rav Acha bar Yaakov comes to town and sleeps in the study hall.
- The demon appears in the form of a seven-headed serpent.
- Rav Acha bows each time; each bow knocks off a head; demon is defeated.

## XI. Transformation Chain

**Bava Kamma 16** — A mystical transformation baraita:
- A male hyena after 7 years becomes a bat → after 7 more years becomes an arpad (vampire bat?) → then thorn bushes → then a demon → then a snake.
