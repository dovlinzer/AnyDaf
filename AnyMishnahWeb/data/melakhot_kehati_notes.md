Kehati's frame (7:2, PDF p. 65): the 39 are the "avot"; related acts are "toldot" and carry
the same status. Doing two acts that are both toldot of ONE av is one sin-offering; two acts
from different avot are two. The sages derived the list from the work of the Mishkan
(building; making the curtains from goat and ram skins; dyeing).

## Group 1: Making Bread (1-11)
1. Sowing (zore'a): putting seed in the ground; Kehati also counts planting a tree, grafting,
   and watering seeds so they grow.
2. Plowing (choresh): working the soil to open it up; also digging a pit in the field or
   levelling uneven ground to improve the land.
3. Reaping (koitzer): cutting the standing crop; Kehati extends it to picking grapes, olives,
   dates, and the like.
4. Binding sheaves (me'amer): gathering the cut stalks into a bundle; also gathering fruit
   into a pile.
5. Threshing (dash): beating the cut stalks to knock the kernels out of their husks.
6. Winnowing (zoreh): tossing the threshed grain into the wind so the light chaff blows away.
7. Selecting (borer): picking the waste out of the food by hand, leaving the good.
8. Grinding (tochen): crushing grain into flour.
9. Sifting (merakked): shaking the flour through a sieve so the coarse bran stays behind.
10. Kneading (lash): mixing flour and water into dough.
11. Baking (ofeh): cooking the dough; Kehati ties this to the Mishkan's dye-cooking.

## Group 2: Making a Garment (12-24)
12. Shearing (gozez tzemer): cutting the wool off the sheep.
13. Bleaching (melabno): washing the wool clean and white.
14. Hackling (menapetz): combing out the tangled wool.
15. Dyeing (tzove'a): coloring the wool.
16. Spinning (toveh): twisting the wool into thread.
17. Setting the warp (mesech): stretching the long threads on the loom.
18. Two heddle loops (oseh shnei batei nirin): fitting two threads into two loops of the loom.
19. Weaving two threads (oreg shnei chutin): crossing two threads through the warp.
20. Dividing two threads (potze'a shnei chutin): separating two threads of the weave.
21. Tying (koshair): making a knot.
22. Untying (matir): undoing a knot.
23. Sewing two stitches (tofer shtei tefirot): two stitches with the needle.
24. Tearing to sew two stitches (koreah al menat litfor shtei tefirot): ripping so as to sew.

## Group 3: Making a Scroll (25-33)
25. Trapping a deer (tzad tzvi) - and, says Kehati, any animal that needs trapping.
26. Slaughtering (shochet).
27. Flaying (mafshito).
28. Salting (molcho) the hide.
29. Curing (me'abdo) the hide.
30. Scraping (mamacheko) the hide.
31. Cutting (mechatecho) it to size.
32. Writing two letters (kotev shtei otiyot).
33. Erasing to write two letters (mocheik al menat litkov shtei otiyot).

## Group 4: Building (34-38)
34. Building (boneh).
35. Tearing down (sother).
36. Extinguishing (mechabeh).
37. Kindling (mav'ir).
38. Striking with the hammer (makeh be-patish): the last blow that finishes the object.

## Group 5: Carrying (39)
39. Carrying out from one domain to another (motzi me-reshut le-reshut).

## ESSENCE of each melakha drawn so far (what must be visibly different before and after)
1. Sowing: seed goes from the hand into the ground. 2. Plowing: hard unbroken ground becomes broken, turned soil.
3. Reaping: standing crop becomes cut stalks. 4. Binding: loose cut stalks become one bundle.
5. Threshing: kernels are knocked out of the stalks. 6. Winnowing: kernels and chaff are separated by throwing into the wind (heavy falls, light blows away).
7. Selecting: waste is removed from the good food (a mixture becomes clean food plus a heap of waste).
8. Grinding: whole kernels become coarse meal (flour mixed with bran). 9. Sifting: the mixed meal becomes pure white flour; the bran is left behind.
10. Kneading: flour and water become one dough. 11. Baking: raw dough becomes bread in the heat of the oven.

## ESSENCE of the Garment set (12-24), from Kehati's reading (PDF pp. 67-68)
12. Shearing: wool is cut off the animal. 13. Bleaching: dirty wool is washed white. 14. Hackling: tangled wool becomes loose, fluffy, straight fibre.
15. Dyeing: white wool becomes coloured. 16. Spinning: loose wool becomes one twisted thread. 17. Setting the warp: threads are stretched parallel and tight on the loom.
18. Two heddle loops: threads are set into loops so they can be lifted. 19. Weaving: cross-threads pass through the tight threads and cloth begins.
20. Dividing two threads: two threads are taken out of the weave, leaving a gap (Kehati also gives a second reading, joining broken ends; the first is drawn).
21. Tying: two ends become one firm knot. 22. Untying: a knot becomes loose ends.
23. Sewing two stitches: two pieces of cloth are joined by stitches. 24. Tearing to sew: cloth is torn so that it can be sewn together.

## Close read of the Garment set (PDF pp. 67-68), 2026-10-14: what Kehati says, and where my pictures go beyond him
Read closely: 12-24 and the mishnah order (the 13 garment melakhot, all from the Mishkan's curtain work). Not yet closely read: 25-33, 34-39 (baking is done; no further reading needed).
- Tools in my pictures that are NOT from Kehati (general knowledge, needed to draw anything): hand shears, basin, dye vat, distaff and spindle, stool. From Kehati: a comb (hackling), the loom with an upper and a lower beam (setting the warp), loops/rings (heddles), needle and thread.
- 20 Dividing two threads: Kehati gives three readings: (a) removing the cross-threads from the warp (or the warp from the cross-threads) to repair the weave [DRAWN]; (b) when a thread breaks mid-cloth, splitting the two ends and spinning them together with the fingertips; (c) after the weaving is finished, cutting off the leftover warp threads.
- 21 Tying: a lasting knot (kesher shel kayama). 22 Untying: undoing such a knot.
- 23 Sewing two stitches: Kehati says a stitch alone is not lasting, so the sewer also ties a knot (two melakhot: sewing and tying). The picture shows only the two stitches.
- 24 Tearing: tearing for no purpose is mere destruction and is exempt; tearing in order to sew (repair) counts. The picture shows a torn cloth and a threaded needle ready.
- 18 Heddles: two rings or loops, a warp thread put through each (a second reading: threading the warp through the loops and making two sets).

## CORRECTION (author, 2026-10-14): 18. Oseh shnei batei nirin = setting up TWO HEDDLES
I took Kehati's first reading (two rings/loops, a warp thread in each) too literally and drew two single loops. The author pointed to the heddles: the loom has two heddle frames (nirin), each full of small loops (batei nirin); alternate warp threads are threaded through the loops of each frame so the warp divides into two sets that are lifted alternately to open the weft's path. This is Kehati's SECOND reading (the warp threads put through the nirin, making two sets/'houses'). DRAWN NOW: two heddle frames, alternate threads through each, the weaver threading one more thread.
