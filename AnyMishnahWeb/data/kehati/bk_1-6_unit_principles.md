# Bava Kamma 1-6: the unit of property damage. Foundation pass on Kehati (2026-10-12)

## What the unit is
Ch.1-6 = damage done by a person's property or act: the four avot (1), how the "foot" and "tooth" and animals' habits create liability (2), objects and acts in the public domain (3), the goring ox (4), the ox and pit in another's property (5), animals that escape, and fire (6). Kehati says at the start of ch.7 that from there to the end of the tractate the subject is theft, robbery and personal injury.

## Governing principles (each tagged with where it appears)
1. **The categories.** Torah's damage categories: keren (horn), shen (tooth), regel (foot), bor (pit), esh (fire), adam (a person). The "four avot" of 1:1 are, on Shmuel's reading (which Kehati follows, with Rambam and Bartenura): regel, shen, bor, esh; Rav counts the whole system; the Yerushalmi reads "shor" as keren. (1:1, pp.1-3)
2. **Why the Torah wrote several.** Each pair differs (living vs not; always damaging vs not), so no one teaches the rest. What they share: damage is their way (derekh lehazik), guarding them is the owner's job, and if one damages the owner pays from the best of his land (b'meitav ha'aretz). (1:1 end, p.4)
3. **Duty to guard = liability (hichsharti).** What I am bound to guard and fail to guard, I am treated as having prepared its damage. Partial preparation counts as full (a pit I deepen from 9 to 10 handbreadths). (1:2, p.4-5; 5:5)
4. **Whose property, whose money.** Liability for damage to property that has a real owner (not hefker), belongs to Israelites (bnei brit; a gentile's ox that damages an Israelite's pays in full, tam or mu'ad, 4:3), and is not hekdesh (no me'ilah). (1:2; 4:3; 4:7)
5. **Where it happened (reshut).** The damager's own domain: exempt. A domain shared by damager and damaged: the "other field" rule does not apply to tooth and foot, but keren is liable (1:2). Public domain: tooth and foot exempt (people may walk there). The damaged person's domain: tooth and foot are liable in full; keren by tam is half (Sages) or full (R. Tarfon). (1:2, 1:4, 2:2, 2:5, 5:2, 5:3)
6. **Tam and mu'ad.** Tam (harmless, not yet established as a danger): half damages, paid only from the body of the ox. Mu'ad (established): full damages from the best property (aliyah). Five tamim (not mu'ad to gore, push, bite, lie on, kick) and five mu'adim (tooth, foot, an ox that became mu'ad, an ox that damaged in the damaged person's domain, a person; also wild beasts, with R. Eliezer limiting this to tamed ones; the snake always). (1:4, 2:1-2:6)
7. **How an ox becomes mu'ad, and stops.** Three incidents with witnesses; the halakha is like R. Yehuda for becoming mu'ad (three days) and like R. Meir for returning to tam (when children play with it and it does not gore). Mu'ad is specific: to a kind of victim (a person, a minor), to a day (Shabbat only). (2:4, 4:2)
8. **Normal vs unusual damage.** Damage in the animal's normal way is full (foot in its walk, tooth eating what it likes). Damage that comes out of the ordinary (kicking, pebbles flung, a dog that carries a coal) is treated as keren: half. First vessel full, vessel broken by the flung one half. (2:1, 2:3)
9. **A person is always mu'ad,** awake or asleep, accident or intent, for damage; the other four payments (pain, healing, loss of work, shame) come in ch.8. (2:6)
10. **"Dayo lavo min hadin lihyot kenidon"** (a conclusion from reasoning cannot be stricter than the law it is drawn from) keeps keren in the damaged person's domain at half, against R. Tarfon. (2:5)
11. **Public domain is shared.** Each person has the right to walk; neither pays when two lawful users collide (beam and jar side by side, 3:5). Responsibility moves with the facts: who was ahead, who stopped suddenly, who warned. A hazard left or created in the public domain makes its maker liable (jug, water, thorns, glass, fence, straw, rubbish); whoever takes such rubbish first acquires it (3:1-3:3, 3:7).
12. **Negligence vs force majeure.** Liability turns on whether the person could have prevented it. The fallen pot-seller (3:4) pays only if he could have got up before the other fell on him (Rambam, Rif follow R. Yochanan; Rav Nachman would add being able to warn). A guarded animal that escapes through no fault (door blown down by ordinary wind or broken by thieves) is exempt; a poorly locked one is not. (3:4, 6:1) VERIFY details of each.
13. **Mutual damage: "bemuter."** When each owner's ox damaged the other's, they settle the difference: tam pays half the difference, mu'ad the full difference; a person is always mu'ad (R. Akiva: even a tam that hurt a person pays full). (3:8)
14. **Burden of proof.** The one who wants the money must bring the proof ("hamotzi mechavero alav hara'ayah"): the halakha. In a real doubt, Sumchos would split the money; the Sages do not. Mishnah 5:1 (cow and calf: half, then a quarter) follows Sumchos; the halakha is like the Sages (the owner of the cow collects nothing for the calf if he cannot prove). (3:11; 5:1)
15. **Two kinds of liability do not combine.** One who owes his life (Shabbat fire) is exempt from payment; an ox's owner pays for what the ox did but not for what the owner himself would be exempt. (3:10) VERIFY the exact list.
16. **Killing needs intent.** An ox that meant to kill an animal and killed a person, or meant one person and killed another type, is exempt from death and ransom. Mu'ad ox: kofer (ransom) to the heirs; tam: none; both stoned. (4:5-4:6)
17. **Four guards.** An owner who entrusts his ox to a guard (unpaid, borrower, paid guard, renter) shifts the duty; the guard pays if the ox damages. (4:9)
18. **The pit.** A pit pays for an animal killed (10 handbreadths = enough to kill) or injured (less); not for people (no ransom for a person killed, but a person injured is covered: Kehati on 3:1), not for objects. The rule names ox and donkey, but all animals are covered ("dibra Torah kahoveh"). Whoever digs, deepens or fails to cover is liable; a pit's covering must be proper. (5:5-5:7)
19. **Fire.** The fire-setter pays; sending it by a deaf-mute, imbecile or minor is exempt in human courts but liable by heaven; if an adult is the agent, the adult pays (no agent for a wrong act). Between several, the one who made the final act pays (wood vs light; the one who fanned it). A wind that carried it frees all. Spread beyond a high fence or a road or river is not the person's fault; R. Eliezer ben Azariah 16 amot... distances are disputed; the halakha is like R. Shimon (all by the size of the fire). Burnt goods: the Sages pay only the stack, R. Yehuda the contents. (6:4-6:6) VERIFY.
20. **Practical cases of shared guarding.** A damaged piece goes with the guard. The gored owner may take an ox as payment. Tam payment: R. Akiva says the victim becomes a partner in the ox; R. Ishmael, a debt paid only from the ox; for several victims the later is paid first from the body. (4:1) VERIFY.
