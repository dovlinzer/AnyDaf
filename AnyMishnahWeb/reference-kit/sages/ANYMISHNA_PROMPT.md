## What it is
Live in iTalmud (www.italmud.com: open a daf's Sages window, press "Relationships" on a card, or
"All sages"; and www.italmud.com/sages).
- **Relationship box** (`AnySages.open(slug)`): the sage in the centre, teachers above with
  arrows down to him, students below with arrows from him, colleagues beside him on dotted lines
  (no arrows), family under him with labels. Click any sage to make them the centre (the others
  glide to new places); a trail goes back. Info box: generation, region, approximate dates,
  family, bio, how often he is named and where (each place a link), Sefaria and Wikipedia.
- **Shas-wide chart** (`AnySages.chart(el)` on a page, `AnySages.openChart()` over a page):
  generations across (Zugot, tanna'im 1–6 and the transition, amora'im 1–8, generation unknown),
  Eretz Yisrael above and Bavel below (tanna'im columns are one tall cell). Each cell shows its
  most quoted sages with "+N more"; search reaches anyone and marks them in their cell.
  Filter: All / Tanna'im / Amora'im. A name opens the relationship box.

## What to do in Illustrated Mishna
1. Wire the build as above.
2. Initialise on every page, for the Mishnah:
   ```js
   AnySages.init({base: 'sages/', corpus: 'mishnah', lang: <'en' or 'he' for the page>,
                  tractateName: tr => <the site's name for it>,
                  link: (corpus, tractate, place) => <url>});   // place is '3:4' (chapter:mishnah)
   ```
   With `corpus: 'mishnah'` the box uses the Mishnah counts ("Named in N mishnayot") and the
   Mishnah tiers (`tier_mishnah`: the 25 most named tanna'im are major).
   `link`: the chapter page and mishnah when the site has it (e.g. `bava-kamma-3.html#m4`; check
   the real file names and the `section id="m{n}"` anchors in `build_site.py`), otherwise the
   mishnah on Sefaria (`https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Bava_Kamma.3.4`). Tractate names in the
   data are Sefaria's without "Mishnah " (`Bava Kamma`, `Avot` for Pirkei Avot).
3. Where to open it: the site's Sages box is not built yet (see `AnyMishnahWeb/CLAUDE.md`, the
   deferred glossary / key-terms / sages plan). Until it is, two entry points:
   - a sage's name in the outline or the Mishnah text opens `AnySages.open(slug)`. Match names
     with the slugs in `sages.json` (`en`, `he`, `alt_en`, `alt_he`); only link a name that fits
     one sage, and leave ambiguous ones (Rabbi Elazar, Rabban Gamliel) unlinked or ask the author.
   - an "All sages" link to a page that calls `AnySages.chart(...)` (as iTalmud's `sages.html`
     does; see `sages_page()` in `AnyDafWeb/prototype/build_prototype.py`), and later the chart
     inside the planned Reference Tools button.
   Ask the author which of these to do first.
4. The box reads the host's CSS variables `--card --fg --blue`; set them from the site's theme if
   the names differ, as iTalmud does (`.sgx{--card:var(--panel);--fg:var(--ink);...}`). Check light
   and dark, Hebrew (right to left), and phone width (no sideways scroll on the page).
5. Rebuild and deploy Illustrated Mishna; tell the author what to try.

## Rules to keep
- The audience is casual learners and high-school students: colour and clarity before citations.
- Facts only from Sefaria and Hebrew Wikipedia (Wikipedia's wording is CC BY-SA and is never
  copied); bios are Sefaria's. Dates are approximate, from the generation, unless Wikipedia gives
  a year.
- Sefaria and Wikipedia have errors (Sefaria lists the amora R. Chanina b. Chama as R. Akiva's
  student). Don't fix data in the site: report it, and the fix goes into `kit_overrides.json`.
