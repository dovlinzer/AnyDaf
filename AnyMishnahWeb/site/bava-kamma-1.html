Mishnah 1: The four primary causes of injury

The Torah names four primary causes of injury: the ox, the pit, the crop-destroying beast and fire. Each has a feature the others lack, and what they share is that you are responsible to guard them.

The four

  • The four primary causes of injury (avot nezikin)avot nezikin (primary causes of injury) are the ox, the pit, the crop-destroying beast and fire.
  • Each comes from the Torah: the ox (Exodus 21:35-36), the pit (21:33-34), the crop-destroying beast (22:4) and fire (22:5).

Why four?

  • The ox is not like the crop-destroying beast; neither is like fire, which is not alive; none of these, whose way is to go out and damage, is like the pit, whose way is not.
  • The Rabbis held that no law in the Torah is superfluous, so each case must teach something the others could not.

What they share

  • It is their way to do injury, and you are responsible to guard them.
  • When one of them does injury, whoever is responsible pays from the best of his land.

Kehati's notes

  • The Gemara disputes what the four are. Rav says the mishnah names the main kinds of damage in the Torah (the ox covers horn, tooth and foot, the "crop-destroying beast" is a person who damages). Shmuel says it names only four kinds of property damage that are attested dangers from the start: the foot ("ox"), the tooth ("crop-destroyer", from a word meaning to bulge out), the pit and fire. Kehati explains the mishnah as Shmuel does, with Rambam and Bartenura.
ארבעה אבות נזיקים. השור. והבור. והמבעה. וההבער. לא הרי השור. כהרי המבעה. ולא הרי המבעה כהרי השור. ולא זה וזה שיש בהן רוח חיים. כהרי האש שאין בו רוח חיים. ולא זה וזה שדרכן לילך ולהזיק. כהרי הבור שאין דרכו לילך ולהזיק. הצד השוה שבהן שדרכן להזיק. ושמירתן עליך. וכשהזיק חב המזיק לשלם תשלומי נזק במיטב הארץ:
There are four primary causes of injury: the ox and the pit and the crop-destroying beast and fire. [The distinctive feature of] the ox is not like [that of] the crop-destroying beast, nor is [the distinctive feature of] either of these, which are alive, like [that of] fire, which is not alive; nor is [the distinctive feature of] any of these, whose way it is to go forth and do injury, like [that of] the pit, whose way it is not to go forth and do injury. What they have in common is that it is their way to do injury and that you are responsible for caring over them; and if one of them did injury whoever [is responsible] for the injury must make restitution [to the damaged party] with the best of his land.

Mishnah 2: What I must guard, and for what damage I pay

A person is liable for what he is bound to guard, even if he only partly caused the damage. Payment is only for property that is not sacred, belongs to Jews, is owned, and damaged outside the damager's own domain and the domain shared by both.

The principle

  • Anything I am responsible to guard, I have rendered it possible to do injury (hichsharti).
  • If I partially rendered it possible, I pay as if I totally did.
  • Example: a pit that was already four feet deep, and a six foot pit would kill an animal; if I deepen it to six, I am liable as if I dug the whole pit.

When the damager pays

  • The property damaged is not sacred (no "sacrilege", me'ilah), belongs to members of the covenant (Jews), and is owned.
  • The injury is done in any place other than the private domain of the damager and the domain shared by damager and damaged.
  • Payment is from the best of his land.

Kehati's notes

  • The partial case is clearer with Kehati's example: a pit of nine handbreadths that he deepened by one, to ten, so an animal that falls dies.
  • The conditions: sacred property is excluded because the verse says "his neighbour's ox", not the Temple's; gentiles' property is covered in 4:3; ownerless property is excluded too.
כל שחבתי בשמירתו הכשרתי את נזקו. הכשרתי במקצת נזקו חבתי בתשלומין כהכשר כל נזקו. נכסים שאין בהן מעילה. נכסים של בני ברית. נכסים המיוחדים ובכל מקום חוץ מרשות המיוחדת למזיק ורשות הניזק והמזיק. וכשהזיק חב המזיק לשלם תשלומי נזק במיטב הארץ:
Anything that I am responsible to guard, I have rendered it possible to do injury [for which I will become obligated]. If I have partially rendered it possible to do injury, I must make restitution for that injury as if I totally rendered it possible to do injury. When one damages [property that fits all of the following categories]: property that does not have “sacrilege” [i.e. sacrificial animals or property that belongs to the Temple in Jerusalem], property that belongs to other members of the covenant [Jews], property that is owned, and the injury is done in any place other than the private domain of the injurer and the common domain of the injured and injurer, in these cases the injurer must make restitution for the injury with the best of his land.

Mishnah 3: How damages are assessed

Damages are assessed in money before a court, on the evidence of free Jewish witnesses. Women are included. The injured and the injurer can share the loss.

The procedure

  • Assessment of the injury in money or things worth money is made before a court of law and by witnesses who are free and Children of the Covenant.
  • Two things are assessed: what the damaged animal was worth, and how large a piece of land the damager must give.

Women

  • Women may be parties in suits concerning injury.
  • The Torah uses "man" in many of these verses, so one might have thought only men's damages count.
  • Kehati: the Torah's law applies to women too, from the verse that follows the Sinai chapters, "these are the laws that you shall set before them" (Exodus 21:1).

Sharing the compensation

  • The injured and the injurer may share in the compensation.
  • The clearest case is half damages: the owner of an ox not known to gore pays only half, so the injured owner bears the other half.

Kehati's notes

  • The assessment is made only before a court of experts, not a lay court.
  • The Gemara disputes whether the half damages of a harmless ox is money or a fine (Rav Pappa: money; Rav Huna: a fine).

Mishnah 4: Harmless and attested dangers

Five agents are harmless (tam)tam (harmless) and five are attested dangers (mu'ad)mu'ad (attested dangers). A harmless one pays half damages from its own body; an attested danger pays full damages from the best property.

Five harmless

  • An animal is not an attested danger to butt, push, bite, lie down or kick.

Five attested dangers

  • The tooth is an attested danger to eat what is fit for it; the leg to break as it walks; an ox that has been warned; an ox that damages in the domain of the damaged party; and human beings.
  • The wolf, lion, bear, leopard, panther and snake are attested dangers. Rabbi Eliezer: when tame they are not, but the snake always is.

The difference

  • The harmless pays half damages from its own body; the attested danger pays full damages from the best property.
  • The idea: an owner whose animal is likely to do a certain damage should guard it more carefully.

Kehati's notes

  • The Torah (Exodus 21:35): "sell the live ox and divide its price": a harmless ox pays only from its own value. If it died or was lost, the owner pays nothing more.

Halakha

  • The halakha does not follow Rabbi Eliezer.

Five [agents of damage] rank as harmless and five as an attested danger. Cattle are not an attested danger to butt, push, bite, lie down, or kick. The tooth [of an animal] is an attested danger to eat that which is for it; The leg [of an animal] is an attested danger to break [things] as it walks along; So also is a warned ox [an ox that has gored before]; And an ox that damages in the domain of the damaged party, and human beings. The wolf, the lion, the bear, the leopard, the panther and the snake all rank as attested danger. Rabbi Eliezer says: When they are tame they are not attested danger, but the snake is always an attested danger. What is the difference between that which is harmless and that which is an attested danger? The harmless pays half-damages from its own body and the attested danger pays full damages from the best property (of its owner and guardian).
